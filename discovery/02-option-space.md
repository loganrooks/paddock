# Option Space

This document maps plausible directions without choosing one yet.

## Axis 1: How Location-Centric Is The Product?

### Option A: Pure Grand Prix GeoGuessr

The product stays tightly focused on guessing race locations, circuits, host cities, and nearby landmarks.

Potential strengths:
- very legible elevator pitch
- clear initial hook
- strong replay loop if content is good

Potential risks:
- novelty may decay quickly
- may not expand naturally into a broader party identity

### Option B: Location-Led F1 Party Game

The geography mode is the anchor, but the surrounding system supports adjacent party modes.

Potential strengths:
- preserves the clean hook while opening room for expansion
- easier to build a "night with friends" product identity

Potential risks:
- requires careful packaging so the core identity stays clear
- mode sprawl can dilute quality

### Option C: F1 Fan Party Platform With Geography As One Pillar

The long-term framing is broader from the start. Geography is one mode among several.

Potential strengths:
- most expansion headroom
- better aligned with the stated long-term ambition

Potential risks:
- weaker initial pitch
- harder to scope
- easier to become generic

## Axis 2: How Much Does It Lean On Real-World Fidelity?

### Low Fidelity

Uses original abstractions, lightly inspired venue identities, and generic motorsport framing.

### Medium Fidelity

Uses real cities, public geographic context, and F1-adjacent facts, while avoiding official visual assets and risky branding.

### High Fidelity

Uses recognizable circuit geometry, official naming conventions, and detailed race-specific material.

For this discovery phase, high fidelity is not automatically disqualified because the project is private-only. The real question is whether higher fidelity makes the game materially better, or only more obvious.

## Axis 3: What Kind Of Social Structure Does It Prioritize?

### Solo-first

Practice and daily challenge orientation.

### Remote room-first

Private lobbies, live scoring, spectators, and shareable rounds.

### Couch-first

One big screen, pass-and-play or phone controllers, easy onboarding for a room.

### Hybrid

A shared content substrate that supports solo, remote, and couch variants without splitting the product into separate games.

Research pattern:
- GeoGuessr-like products show the value of one clue substrate with multiple wrappers
- Jackbox-like products show the value of one theatrical shared-screen wrapper
- the strongest path may be a content model that supports both

## Axis 4: What Kind Of Knowledge Does It Reward?

### Recognition

Players identify a place, circuit, driver, or year from surface clues.

### Interpretation

Players explain what a clue means, why it matters, or what it implies strategically.

### Performance

Players bluff, argue, sequence, draft, predict, or collaborate under time pressure.

The research suggests the deepest fan delight will likely come from mixing recognition with interpretation rather than staying in pure identification mode.

## Working Assumption

The most promising zone may be:
- location-led rather than location-only
- medium-to-high fidelity where it increases play value
- hybrid social structure rather than single-mode delivery
- interpretation-heavy rather than recognition-only

This is still a hypothesis, not a decision.
