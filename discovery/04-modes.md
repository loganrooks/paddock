# Mode Families

This document is intentionally expansive.

## Anchor Mode

### Grand Prix Guesser

Players infer a grand prix location from layered clues such as:
- street view or map context
- surrounding landmarks
- weather and terrain cues
- circuit-adjacent infrastructure
- race-weekend atmosphere and venue trivia

Best current clue pattern:
- `ambient clue`
- `technical clue`
- `narrative clue`

The important distinction is that the round should not only ask "what is this place?" It should increasingly ask "why is this place recognizably this place to a real fan?"

Possible answer surfaces for this anchor family:
- full circuit
- venue complex
- exact section or corner
- approach-to-circuit context
- mixed answers such as `circuit + corner`

These are not commitments. They are examples of the shape we seem to be looking for.

## Adjacent Mode Families

### Circuit Recognition

Guess the circuit from shape fragments, sector logic, corner sequences, or partial maps.

Sub-variants:
- partial outline
- corner-order memory
- sector personality
- overtaking profile
- famous incident by layout fragment

### Race Weekend Texture

Guess the venue or event from clues like marina skylines, fan-zone details, elevation, night-race lighting, paddock style, or local visual markers.

This family rewards lived fandom and weekend-following habits more than pure database memory.

### History And Memory

Guess the year, weekend, driver, or incident from contextual clues.

Strong pattern:
- identify both `place` and `era`
- use season stakes, regulations, sponsor era, or rules context as part of the answer surface

### Strategy And Prediction

Modes built around pit windows, tyre choices, weather swings, and race control chaos.

Possible sub-modes:
- confidence betting
- undercut / overcut calls
- safety-car timing swings
- quali-vs-race setup tradeoffs
- "paper-fast vs actually viable" scenarios

### Bluffing And Social Mischief

Modes where one or more players insert fake answers, fake clues, or misleading confidence signals.

Strong references from research:
- fake expertise
- fake stewarding precedent
- fake radio or rules wording
- debate with secret true answer

### Team Modes

Partner play, relay formats, captain-and-crew formats, or teams racing toward a score target.

Most promising team structures:
- best team guess counts
- clue-giver and solver split
- one player sees a map while another sees telemetry or text
- host-led audience rounds with overflow spectators

### Regulation And Stewarding

Modes where players must judge incidents, penalties, restarts, or overtaking standards.

Why it matters:
- it rewards fans who understand the sport as a rules system
- it creates argument naturally
- it feels different from generic sports trivia

### Debate And Ranking

Modes where players seed, rank, or argue for outcomes:
- best street circuit
- most chaotic wet race
- strongest underdog podium
- whether a move was fair under current standards

### Daily Companion Modes

Compact repeatable formats inspired by GeoGrid or Worldle:
- daily circuit silhouette
- grid of driver x circuit x condition constraints
- era and venue mini-puzzles
- fast warm-up rounds before a group session

### Promising Weird Directions

- `Steward Room`: bluffing-deduction around real or fake stewarding precedent
- `Ghost Lap`: identify a lap or incident from abstract media fragments
- `Radio Cryptography`: decode engineer shorthand with team-vs-team clue economy
- `Parc Ferme Draft`: draft weekend packages before race-weekend reveals lock choices in
- `Incident Bracket Night`: argue and bet through a seeded bracket of famous moments

## Design Guardrails

The project should not assume every future mode belongs in v1.

The purpose of this doc is to widen the creative space before later deciding:
- which modes are core
- which modes are future expansion
- which ideas are better left out

The mode family that looks strongest so far is not "more map variants." It is "ways for fans to perform layered knowledge socially."
