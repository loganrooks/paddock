## Task Type

Initial architecture research/planning.

## Scope

Investigate how Prix Guesser could think about funding, access, contribution, and capacity communication without drifting into manipulative scarcity design or prematurely becoming a commercial product.

This lane is not a generic monetization study.

It is a study of:

- what models align with the project's ethos
- what obligations each model creates
- how capacity and support can be communicated honestly
- how monetary contribution might compare with non-monetary contribution such as community hosting or compute contribution

## Starting Context

Read the shared starting context from the orchestration brief first.

## Research Instructions

Explore and compare possibilities such as:

- pure donation support
- donation tiers with no access guarantees
- paid/free split with guaranteed access for paid users
- contribution models where support may be monetary or infrastructural
- invite-only/public-beta hybrids
- queueing/waitlisting/capacity-window systems

You must attend to:

- trust and fairness
- the difference between honest transparency and manipulative scarcity
- what promises each model would implicitly make to users
- how each model interacts with small-scale/private-first hosting realities
- whether certain models would distort the product toward public-service expectations too early

## Special Requirements

You must include sections titled:

- `Ethically Tolerable Scarcity`
- `What Users Might Hear Versus What We Mean`
- `Promises Each Model Forces Us To Keep`

You must explicitly distinguish:

- sustainable support models
- extractive or trust-eroding models
- models that only make sense after a different product stage has already been reached
