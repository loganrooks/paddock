## Anti-goals

Do NOT:
- turn this into a go/no-go pass
- ask whether "F1 fans" in the abstract will like it
- collapse to feasibility-first reasoning
- assume one ideal fan or one ideal play context
- optimize every idea for universal compatibility
- mistake user-origin for proof of quality
- overreward memey instant legibility while ignoring replay loops

## Lane-specific questions

You must pressure this lane with questions like:

- what makes the reveal satisfying rather than merely format-shaped?
- how much of the fun depends on authored content quality?
- what kinds of authored content create durable replay versus quick exhaustion?
- where does player interpretation, debate, or performance add enough variation to keep authored formats alive?
- what variant forks already exist here:
  for example cleaner trivia-like forms, more social reading forms, bluff/deception variants, race-weekend/event variants, async derivatives, or creator/community-authored extensions
- what makes these modes strong for local play, sync play, async play, spectator play, or calendar/event play?
- where does async preserve the fun, and where does it flatten the reveal?
- where does a community/content pipeline help, and where would it become content burden?

## Required lenses

Address each of these explicitly:

1. `What is already here`
   What is concrete, what is thin, and what has already been pressure-tested more directly by the user.

2. `Possibility expansion`
   Variant forks, tone shifts, tuning knobs, authored-pack ideas, cadence ideas, and ways the reveal structure could support more than one social shape.

3. `Context profile / tension map`
   Where the mode family is clearly strong, where it can balance multiple contexts, where variant forks should make different commitments, and where a context fit would feel forced.

4. `User situations`
   At minimum consider:
   - mixed-knowledge couch group
   - remote Discord group
   - solo fan between race weekends
   - watch-party crowd
   - recurring friend group with inside jokes/history

5. `Community / online potential`
   Friend-group history, public competition, UGC, spectator/streamer value, race-weekend cadence, and plausible async forms.

6. `Virality vs replayability`
   What gets clipped/shared versus what creates another round, another pack, another night, or another weekend.

7. `Launch-gap lens`
   Onboarding, content burden, session-length fit, mixed-skill friendliness, spectator dead time, moderation/safety where relevant, and funny-once risk.

8. `Category exceedance`
   What good play shape appears here that our current grid does not capture well.

Good output for this lane should:
- sharpen distinctions between mode versions rather than flattening them

- notice when authored content is the engine versus when social interpretation is the engine
