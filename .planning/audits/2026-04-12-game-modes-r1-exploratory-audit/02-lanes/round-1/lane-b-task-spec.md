# Lane B Task Spec

## What this lane owns

This lane handles the judgment / information part of Round 1 for the game-mode exploratory audit.

Owned scope:
- `The Stewards' Room`
- `Team Principal's Desk`
- `The Grid Walk`
- `Relive the Moment` as a content/mechanical lens where it sharpens judgment or information-play

Out of scope:
- final portfolio ranking across all modes
- final cross-lane synthesis
- architecture-first elimination of promising ideas
- treating any one expertise floor as the only correct audience target

This is a provisional Round 1 bundle. If the main thread determines that one of these entries belongs elsewhere or deserves standalone treatment, the boundary may be revised.

## Purpose

Your job is to explore the part of the portfolio where the fun may come from judgment, inference, debate, partial information, cooperation, or asymmetric perspective.

You should:
- identify which modes are genuinely game-shaped versus merely scenario-shaped
- surface how much expertise they demand and how that demand can be tuned
- distinguish between debate fun, reasoning fun, role tension, and information tension
- identify strong forks instead of collapsing everything into one “serious fan” bucket

## Anti-goals

Do NOT:
- reduce the lane to “hardcore fans only” versus “casual fans only”
- turn it into a feasibility pass about data supply or implementation burden
- ask yes/no fan-appeal questions
- assume each mode needs one true play context
- reward seriousness just because it sounds less memey
- punish memey or dramatic variants just because they are lighter

## Lane-specific questions

You must pressure this lane with questions like:

- what is the actual source of tension here:
  debate, cooperation, hidden info, bluffing, judgment under uncertainty, social reading, authored incident interpretation, or some mixture?
- how much domain knowledge is required to make the mode sing, and what tuning knobs could lower or redirect that burden?
- where does a mode want symmetric information versus asymmetric roles?
- where does cooperation make it better, and where would competition sharpen it?
- which ideas are really “interesting scenario prompts” but not yet game loops?
- what variant forks could make these modes accessible without flattening their distinctiveness?
- which of these could work remotely because discussion is the content, and which ones would die without a room’s energy?
- where could community, history, or recurrent packs deepen them without turning them into admin-heavy systems?

## Required lenses

Address each of these explicitly:

1. `What is already here`
   What is concrete, what is still mostly framing, and what has or has not been meaningfully pressure-tested with the user.

2. `Possibility expansion`
   Variants around cooperation, competition, secrecy, structured debate, timed judgment, party accessibility, and seriousness/comedy balance.

3. `Context profile / tension map`
   Where these modes work best, where multi-context support is real, where it is forced, and where different forks should make different commitments.

4. `User situations`
   At minimum consider:
   - mixed-knowledge couch group
   - 2 hardcore fans plus 2 casual partners
   - remote Discord group
   - watch-party crowd with spectators
   - recurring friend group with persistent history

5. `Community / online potential`
   Friend-group history, debate spectacle, UGC incidents/prompts, calendar/event cadence, async possibilities, and whether public competition helps or cheapens the mode.

6. `Virality vs replayability`
   What creates memorable arguments or clips versus what sustains repeat play and evolving mastery/social history.

7. `Launch-gap lens`
   Onboarding burden, rules explanation, expertise floor, spectator boredom, social dead zones, content burden, moderation/safety where relevant, and funny-once or clever-once risk.

8. `Category exceedance`
   What play shape appears here that our current category grid does not describe well.
