# Lane F2 Task Spec

## What this is

This is a corrective rerun of the driver-themed ideation lane.

It exists because the first driver-themed pass was judged unsatisfactory by the creator:

- too contaminated by Lane E abstractions
- too willing to lock into prior meh ideas
- not genuinely exploratory enough
- too quick to reuse press/PR structures that are not actually driver-led unless a specific driver strongly pushes there

This rerun should behave like a reset, not a minor revision.

## Core correction

The first pass overfit to previously named branches.

This rerun must instead:

- reopen the terrain from the seeded driver ideas outward
- privilege driver-to-mechanic discovery over inheritance from Lane E
- treat previous abstractions as suspect unless they are re-earned from the driver signal itself
- search for better realizations, not merely cleaner summaries of prior work

## Root contract

This rerun still sits under:
- `.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/01-round-1/game-modes-r1-task-spec.md`

It must also obey the methodological correction already recorded in:

- `.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/03-next-round/round-2-prompts.md`

Especially:

- treat each promising idea as design terrain
- compare multiple realizations where useful
- do not impose one shared ontology across all games
- let the ontology emerge from the game itself

## Explicit anti-contamination rule

Do not default to importing Lane E net-new families.

In particular, do not lean on:

- `The Press Room`
- `Press Conference`
- `Silly Season`
- `Rumor Mill`
- `Qualifying Window`
- any adjacent Lane E family

unless a specific driver signal genuinely and strongly pushes there.

If you reuse one of those directions, you must explicitly justify:

- why this is truly driver-led rather than Lane E contamination
- why the driver signal changes the mechanic rather than merely reskinning it

The burden of proof is high.

## Creator steering

Use these as direct constraints:

- the existing user-origin seeds are evidence about what kinds of driver ideas can work, not the spine of the search
- some of the work should look back at those seeds and ask what made them strong
- but a substantial part of the work must be genuinely open exploration not predetermined by those seeds
- explore both current/recent grid and greats where fruitful
- look for driver quirks, public myths, reputational patterns, and behavioral signatures that can become mechanics
- do not assume every promising direction is a standalone game
- do not assume every promising direction is only a cosmetic pack either
- couch-first still matters, but online, streamer, community, and persistence potential should be explored where they reveal stronger realizations
- if an idea wants unlock ladders, selectable modes, driver rosters, or themed content packs, that should emerge from the game rather than be forced on all ideas

## Anti-goals

Do NOT:

- recycle Lane E structures just because they already exist on paper
- treat “driver-themed” as mainly prompt-writing, press roleplay, or off-screen talking
- reduce driver ideation to trivia, quotes, or skinning
- overfocus on one tone only:
  not only meme, not only serious, not only public-performance
- assume the best driver directions are the most currently documented ones
- stop at “X driver would be funny in Y format” without naming the mechanic

## Required lenses

### 1. Driver signal -> mechanic

For each promising direction, identify:

- the driver signal
- the mechanic it becomes
- what changes in player behavior because of that signal
- whether the idea survives if the room only half-knows the lore

### 2. Competing realizations

For strong territories, compare multiple realizations:

- couch / host-screen
- private online sync
- streamer / spectator
- persistence / challenge ladder / private-group memory

Ask:

- which realization is funniest?
- which has the strongest loop?
- which feels most F1-native?
- which feels most likely to survive beyond first-joke novelty?

### 3. Native ontology

Do not force a standard ontology.

Instead ask:

- what are the real moving parts here?
- if this wants `modes`, what are they?
- if this wants `packs`, `profiles`, `match types`, `unlock ladders`, `roles`, or `mutators`, where do those actually belong?

### 4. Novelty beyond the seeded core

You must search for genuinely new driver-mechanic territories beyond the seeded trio.

Examples of the kind of discovery sought:

- not “another crash guy”
- but “what entirely different mechanic family opens from another driver signal?”

The seeded trio should be used partly as a diagnostic question:

- what properties made those ideas feel alive?

But the lane must also ask:

- what other driver territories become visible once we stop orbiting those seeds?

At least a meaningful portion of the shortlist should come from that second movement.

### 5. Public-surface and packaging risk

Some private-room seeds are stronger as mechanic discoveries than as public-facing names.

Do not confuse:

- private meme energy
- official surface viability

If a mechanic is strong but the naming/public packaging is fragile, say so clearly.
