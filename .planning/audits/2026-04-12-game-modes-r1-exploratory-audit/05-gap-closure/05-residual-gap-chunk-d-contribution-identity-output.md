# 05 Residual Gap Chunk D: Contribution/Discovery Identity And Endorsement Surface

## Chunk framing

- Mode: `solution evaluation`
- Classification: `initial architecture research/planning`
- Question: if Prix Guesser later accepts approved outside packs, what product identity should those packs inhabit, where should unsupported listings live, and how should endorsement/support language differ across those surfaces?
- Scope:
  - `partner brand` versus `guest editor` versus `Prix-curated copy`
  - reviewed/approved/curated semantics
  - unsupported-listing placement
  - core product discovery versus admin/self-host discovery
  - endorsement, support, and moderation implications
- Non-goals:
  - not reopening open marketplace/public publishing as an active branch
  - not designing creator economics, payout, or revenue split
  - not finalizing moderation tooling
  - not deciding exact future UI copy or exact future namespace labels
- Stop condition:
  - enough direct external comparison exists to narrow the rough identity family, isolate unsupported-listing placement, and expose which terms remain semantically dangerous before sensitivity

`[governing:cited:internal]` This chunk inherits the remediation packet's doctrine that `official curated packs` remain the baseline, `trusted-private sharing / import` is the first non-internal seam, `invited / partner authors with editorial activation` is the main later middle-space branch, and `open marketplace / public publishing` remains out.

## Gap justification

This chunk directly addresses:

- `RGR-04`
- supporting register anchors:
  - `GCO-06`
  - `GCO-08`
  - `GCO-08B`
  - `GCO-08C`

`[evidenced:cited:internal]` These belong together because the remaining problem is not `should outside contribution exist at all?` The remaining problem is:

- what later approved outside packs would be *called*
- where users would *encounter* them
- what endorsement or support users would *infer*
- which discovery surface silently changes the product identity

`[assumed:reasoned:internal]` Those are one chunk because naming, surface placement, and support implication are coupled. A `partner brand` inside the main library, a `guest editor` inside Prix's editorial shell, and an `unsupported listing` in an admin/self-host channel are not cosmetic variants of the same thing.

This chunk is not trying to close:

- open self-service publishing
- a creator marketplace roadmap
- exact moderation workflow
- broad community-platform features beyond contribution/discovery identity

## What is already settled

`[governing:cited:internal]` The following should constrain this chunk rather than be reopened:

- Prix Guesser remains private-first, browser-first, curated, and host-led in its current product center (`LONG-ARC.md`, `REQUIREMENTS.md`).
- `Open marketplace / public publishing` is substantively `rejected-for-now`, even if some earlier packet language used looser `deferred` phrasing (`05-deferral-rejection-review-output.md`).
- `Trusted-private sharing / import` remains the first non-internal seam worth protecting (`05-remediation-packet-d-contribution-discovery-output.md`).
- `Invited / partner authors with editorial activation` remains the main later middle-space branch worth preserving (`05-remediation-packet-d-contribution-discovery-output.md`, `05-remediation-converged-synthesis-output.md`).
- `Reviewed external submissions` and `directory-listed but unsupported additions` remain later and conditional, not first-value branches (`05-remediation-packet-d-contribution-discovery-output.md`, `05-deferral-rejection-review-output.md`).
- Discovery posture and contribution posture cannot be separated; searchable discovery changes endorsement and support expectations (`05-remediation-packet-d-contribution-discovery-output.md`).

`[assumed:reasoned:internal]` Practical consequence: this chunk is not choosing whether Prix becomes open or closed. It is choosing which later approved-outside-pack identity best preserves the already-settled curated product center.

## Negative-case / failure evidence note

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` The negative cases in the directly engaged sources point in the same direction:

- Kahoot says verified creators and partners still sit under the same moderation rules as other user-generated content, and flagged public items can be quarantined, made private, or removed.
- Kahoot's public Discover surface mixes team, partners, and community content in one search experience, which raises the labeling burden if trust levels differ.
- HACS says custom elements are published for others to use `at their own risk`, are not supported by the Home Assistant project, and even default-directory additions still take months to review and include.
- Obsidian keeps community plugins behind Restricted Mode, makes users opt in from Settings, performs only initial review, and says the team cannot officially endorse any specific plugin.
- WordPress has to remind plugin authors that the directory is not a marketplace and that users are not customers, which is a plain support-drift warning once official listing exists.
- Minecraft's Realms agreement says publication does not mean the platform reviewed or approved a map for compliance, may not guarantee attribution, and can still leave complaints/support issues with the creator.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` These failures and warnings all cut against a blended core library where official, partner, reviewed, and unsupported items share near-identical product language. The more the listing looks official, the more users infer endorsement, support, and durability.

## Findings

### 1. The strongest carry-forward identity is `guest editor / editorial activation`, not `partner brand`

`[evidenced:cited:internal+external-direct]` The remediation packet already preserved `invited / partner authors with editorial activation` as the main later middle-space branch. Direct external comparison makes that identity narrower:

- Minecraft Realms says its content library is curated, the team selects the most functional and fun submissions, and creators can be featured on Minecraft.net.
- Realms promotional pages can visibly credit creators while still keeping the official Realms surface in charge.
- This is materially closer to `guest editor inside an official Prix library` than to `creator brand as a parallel product identity`.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` For Prix Guesser, later approved outside packs should default toward:

- Prix-controlled editorial activation
- visible contributor credit
- official discovery staying Prix-curated

not toward:

- creator-profile-led discovery
- creator storefronts
- creator-follow becoming a main product identity

### 2. `Partner brand` is a real pattern, but it drags in heavier public-platform semantics than this branch can honestly carry

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` Minecraft Marketplace partners and Kahoot verified creators both push the contributor identity forward:

- public creator/partner identity is part of the value proposition
- the catalog/discover surface teaches users to navigate by creator
- submissions or offerings sit inside a broader marketplace/discovery system

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` That pattern is not forbidden forever, but it is semantically too heavy for current Prix doctrine because it tends to imply:

- public creator discovery as a meaningful destination
- stronger endorsement by association
- stronger moderation and profile management obligations
- easier drift toward `marketplace` thinking

### 3. `Prix-curated copy` is better understood as a selective maintenance pattern than as the primary outside-pack identity

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` The strongest direct support here comes from the Realms publishing agreement, not from a mainstream public-facing product pattern:

- the platform reserves broad rights to use, modify, distribute, and market submitted maps
- attribution is attempted but not guaranteed
- publication does not itself prove broad approval/control in the durable sense users may infer

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` That makes `Prix-curated copy` defensible as a later fallback when:

- Prix needs to repair, adapt, or maintain a strong outside contribution
- the creator relationship is limited or time-boxed
- the product wants to absorb a pack into a stable curated library

It does **not** make `Prix-curated copy` the best default user-facing identity for later approved outside packs. As a default it would over-hide contributor identity and understate the relationship work implied by invited outside authorship.

### 4. Unsupported listings fit advanced/admin/self-host channels much better than the core guest-facing library

`[evidenced:cited:internal+external-direct]` This is where the external comparison is sharpest:

- HACS custom repositories are added manually from an advanced menu, while default-directory inclusion is slower and more governed.
- HACS also states plainly that custom elements are at the user's own risk and are not supported by the Home Assistant project.
- Obsidian keeps community plugins behind Settings and Restricted Mode, forcing an explicit opt-in before users browse or enable third-party additions.
- WordPress shows that once items are listed inside an official directory, users still arrive with support expectations even when the host tries to police those expectations aggressively.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` The carry-forward implication is strong:

- if Prix ever preserves `directory-listed but unsupported`, it should default to `advanced/admin/self-host surfaces`
- it should not default to `main guest-facing library`

This is the cleanest way to preserve the seam without silently changing the product's official endorsement posture.

### 5. `Reviewed` and `approved` are not stable synonyms for endorsement

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` The directly engaged sources use these terms differently:

- Obsidian: `initial review` means a first gate into the plugin store, not ongoing review of every release
- Kahoot: a flagged public kahoot can later be `approved for public access`, which is a moderation-state result, not a permanent quality/endorsement badge
- Minecraft Marketplace: every new submission is reviewed for quality and suitability before release
- Minecraft Realms agreement: publication does not mean the platform reviewed or approved the map for compliance in the strong sense a creator might infer

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` For Prix this means:

- `reviewed submission` should mean `queue-processed and decisioned`, not `officially blessed forever`
- `approved` should be treated as narrower than `curated`
- `curated` should be reserved for material Prix editorial activation into an official surface

### 6. `Marketplace` is the most semantically dangerous borrowed term in this chunk

`[evidenced:cited:internal+external-direct]` In the directly engaged references, `marketplace` is not a neutral synonym for `later contribution surface`:

- Kahoot Marketplace includes verified creator profiles, seller access, public discoverability, and paid offerings
- Minecraft Marketplace includes official partners, creator businesses, public release, and in-game purchasing
- WordPress explicitly has to say its official directory is *not* a marketplace because listing alone creates commercial/support confusion

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` If Prix uses `marketplace` loosely in later docs, it will silently import:

- public discovery assumptions
- creator-economy assumptions
- stronger support and quality inference
- more public-facing moderation burden

For this project, `marketplace` should remain a reversal-sensitive term, not a casual label for the later contribution seam.

## What got tighter

`[projected:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` This chunk materially tightens the carry-forward in four ways:

1. The default later approved-outside-pack identity is now best described as `guest editor / editorial activation inside a Prix-curated library`.
2. `Partner brand / creator profile` remains alive only as a later, challengeable branch that would require a much stronger public discovery posture.
3. `Prix-curated copy` becomes a selective maintenance/forking pattern, not the default identity for approved outside packs.
4. `Directory-listed but unsupported` now has a stronger preferred placement: `advanced/admin/self-host surfaces`, not the core guest-facing library.

## What remains challengeable

`[open:reasoned:internal]` The following should remain explicitly challengeable:

- whether later solo ritual or public-light wrappers eventually justify stronger creator-follow surfaces
- whether certain trusted collaborators deserve limited co-branding rather than only guest-editor credit
- whether some approved outside packs should eventually graduate from guest-editor identity into stronger Prix-curated copies
- whether reviewed submissions, if ever activated, should feed only editorial activation or should also feed a segregated unsupported operator catalog
- exact contributor namespace language such as `guest editor`, `featured author`, `contributing curator`, or similar

## Trigger conditions

- `Guest editors / editorial activation`
  - Prix has stable preview/playtest and editorial review flows
  - contributor rights provenance and attribution rules are explicit
  - pack metadata supports contributor credit, versioning, compatibility, and retirement
  - official discovery can show outside contribution without weakening the core Prix-curated trust surface

- `Partner brands / creator profiles`
  - the product intentionally wants users to discover by creator, not only by Prix editorial curation
  - public or public-light discovery has become a deliberate branch, not an accidental side effect
  - moderation, reporting, profile governance, and contributor-status changes are operationally owned
  - support language can distinguish `creator identity`, `Prix curation`, and `Prix support` without ambiguity

- `Prix-curated copies`
  - Prix has rights to adapt/maintain the pack
  - pack maintenance and calibration require stronger Prix ownership than the contributor can provide
  - creator credit/attribution policy is explicit even if the pack enters a stronger Prix namespace
  - the project wants long-term evergreen reliability more than a strong creator-front identity for that pack

- `Reviewed external submissions`
  - there is owned queue capacity
  - response-time honesty exists
  - rejection, revision, and removal states are defined
  - reviewed items have a known downstream path: either editorial activation or segregated listing

- `Directory-listed but unsupported`
  - advanced/admin/self-host surfaces exist as a distinct operator context
  - official and unsupported surfaces are visually and verbally separated
  - origin, compatibility, version, and deprecation labels are visible before activation/import
  - support docs, reporting, and removal paths exist without implying official pack support

## Recommended carry-forward

`[projected:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Recommended contribution/discovery identity ladder:

1. Keep `official Prix-curated packs` as the main library and main trust surface.
2. Keep `trusted-private sharing / import` as the first non-internal seam.
3. Narrow the later approved-outside-pack default toward `guest editors / editorial activation` rather than `partner brands`.
4. Keep `partner brands / creator profiles` alive only as a later, challengeable branch that would require a deliberate move toward stronger creator discovery.
5. Keep `Prix-curated copies` as a selective adaptation/maintenance pattern, not the default later identity.
6. Keep `reviewed external submissions` later and conditional, with the expectation that review feeds either editorial activation or a sharply segregated later surface.
7. Keep `directory-listed but unsupported` as a narrow advanced/admin/self-host possibility only.
8. Keep `open marketplace / public publishing` rejected-for-now.

`[projected:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Discovery carry-forward:

- core product discovery should continue to mean `official Prix-curated`, with later room for `guest-editor activated`
- `unlisted/share-by-link` remains a meaningful earlier seam than browseable community discovery
- unsupported discovery, if ever real, should default to operator-facing surfaces rather than the guest-facing library

## Explicit deferrals

- public creator profiles as a near-term commitment
- public creator storefronts or creator-economy mechanics
- broad searchable community catalog inside the main library
- self-service publishing
- creator ratings/reviews
- final namespace and label copy for later contributor surfaces
- exact moderation workflow for any future reviewed-submission queue

## What should remain open

- whether some later mature-product wrapper makes creator-follow more valuable than room-first trust clarity
- whether a small set of long-running collaborators should eventually appear more like `partners` than `guest editors`
- whether certain imported packs should later graduate into Prix-maintained curated copies
- whether a reviewed-submissions branch, if ever activated, should publish as outside-owned entries, Prix-maintained forks, or both
- exact contributor status vocabulary and visual language

## Sensitivity implications

`[governing:reasoned:internal]` A later sensitivity pass may now assume:

- the core official library remains Prix-curated
- `guest editor / editorial activation` is the preferred later middle-space identity
- `partner brand / creator profile` is not the default carry-forward
- `directory-listed but unsupported` should be tested mainly as an advanced/admin/self-host seam
- `marketplace` remains a reversal-sensitive term

`[governing:reasoned:internal]` A later sensitivity pass should **not** assume:

- that partner-brand surfaces are fully ruled out forever
- that guest-editor naming is finalized
- that Prix-curated copies are the default fate of approved outside work
- that reviewed submissions are ready to activate
- that unsupported listings belong in the main guest-facing library

`[governing:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Sensitivity should specifically watch for semantic drift:

- `reviewed` being used as if it meant `officially endorsed`
- `approved` being used as if it meant `curated`
- `curated` being used for mere queue passage
- `unsupported` being used without a separate surface and stronger friction
- `marketplace` being used as a casual synonym for `later contribution seam`

## Semantic drift register

`[evidenced:cited:internal+external-direct]` The most important term corrections from this chunk are:

- `reviewed`
  - should mean `passed through a review step`
  - should not mean `fully endorsed`, `permanently safe`, or `ongoing quality guarantee`

- `approved`
  - should mean `allowed into a surface or restored after review`
  - should not automatically mean `officially curated`

- `curated`
  - should mean `Prix materially selected, activated, and stands behind the item's presence in an official surface`
  - should not be used for mere submission eligibility or technical compliance

- `unsupported`
  - should mean `separate trust tier with explicit operator/user burden`
  - should not share near-identical discovery language with official packs

- `marketplace`
  - in the external references, this means public creator-discovery and/or sales infrastructure
  - in Prix docs, using it loosely would materially overstate the intended later branch
