# 05 Sensitivity Docket B1: Later Money-Family Ordering Output

## Docket framing

- Mode: `synthesis`
- Classification: `initial architecture research/planning`
- Question: how should the core planning docs preserve the challenge-rounded reading where `editorial/content` is the current stress-tested default later money family, while `hosted convenience` remains a serious rival rather than a minor footnote?
- Scope:
  - `PROJECT.md`, `LONG-ARC.md`, `ROADMAP.md`, and `REQUIREMENTS.md`
  - feature-to-doc implications, not canon edits
  - ordering pressure, not detailed perk or pricing design
- Non-goals:
  - exact prices or supporter benefits
  - a final monetization roadmap
  - choosing a winner
  - re-running the `paid guaranteed participation` or hosting-identity packets
- Stop condition: each affected planning surface has a clear map of what it may safely imply, what would overcommit, what would silently foreclose, and what must stay explicit/open

## Target feature pressure

- `editorial/content` survives as the current stress-tested later-family default, but only in the narrowed sense used by the challenge round: the more doctrine-compatible cross-archetype default, not a committed winner.
- `hosted convenience` survives as the strongest live rival and becomes especially important under recurring private remote play, operator absence, persistent access, and convenience-heavy coordination pain.
- `paid guaranteed participation` remains outside the ordinary later-money ladder and should not be allowed to distort this ordering through softer wording. That boundary is adjacent rather than primary here.
- The practical pressure is not "which money family exists?" It is "which family the core docs quietly make sound inevitable."

## Current gate classification

- Primary classification: `bounded-open`
- Evidence:
  - `05-sensitivity-control-note.md` says sensitivity may test accidental hard-coding for bounded-open questions, but may not close the ranking.
  - `05-pre-sensitivity-challenge-converged-synthesis-output.md` says sensitivity may assume `editorial/content` as the current stress-tested default and `hosted convenience` as a serious rival, but still may not assume a final winner.
  - `05-pre-sensitivity-challenge-b-money-service-output.md` narrows the verdict to: `editorial/content` is the stronger general family only as the doctrine-compatible cross-archetype default, while `hosted convenience` may outrank it for specific remote/private recurring archetypes.
- Secondary watchpoint: `reversal-sensitive` language can still appear at the edges if a doc quietly reintroduces `paid guaranteed participation`, or treats `hosted convenience` as the chosen official future rather than the strongest rival.

## Current survivor and strongest rival(s)

- Current survivor: `editorial/content`
  - Narrow reading: the cleaner general later family if "general" means "least likely to sell room fairness or immediate service obligation across still-open wrappers."
  - Load-bearing caveat: this default depends on the project being able to sustain a real later content/programming supply.
- Strongest rival: `hosted convenience`
  - Narrow reading: not a minor branch, but a real rival whenever recurring private remote play makes easier room start, persistence, backups, remote access, or always-on coordination more valuable than new content drops.
  - Load-bearing caveat: the rival matters most when the remote/private archetype becomes the dominant real use pattern, not just a hypothetical later edge.
- Boundary item, not co-equal rival: `paid guaranteed participation`
  - It remains outside the ordinary later-family ladder and should not be used as a backdoor tie-breaker between `editorial/content` and `hosted convenience`.

## Why this pressure matters

- Evidence:
  - `PROJECT.md:98-111` already holds both sides of the pressure in Milestone 2: sustainable content operations and richer pack/history surfaces on one side, with private-host durability and more persistent remote access on the other.
  - `PROJECT.md:142-146` and `LONG-ARC.md:60-66,81-129` already separate cadence, memory, hosting shape, and support/service obligation.
  - `ROADMAP.md:79-100` preserves the private-host durability seam in Phase 3, while `ROADMAP.md:170-188` preserves content-calibration and cadence seams in Phase 6.
  - `REQUIREMENTS.md:87-118,126-144` already splits future wrappers, distribution, content scaling, and explicit deferrals rather than flattening them into one future-premium bucket.
- Inference:
  - The biggest risk is not total omission. It is asymmetric wording that quietly makes one family feel like the only "serious" mature-product path.
  - If the canon talks only in editorial cadence/programming language, `hosted convenience` slides back to a footnote.
  - If the canon talks only in durable remote/private access language, `hosted convenience` starts looking like the chosen official future rather than the strongest unresolved rival.

## What each doc may safely imply

- `PROJECT.md` may imply that the shared substrate could later support both richer authored/programmed value and easier recurring remote/private convenience, and that Milestone 2 is where those pressures start to matter without being chosen.
- `LONG-ARC.md` may imply that later content/programming value and later convenience/service value are separate doctrine-relevant families, and that the project intentionally preserves both seams while keeping support, access, and service promises distinct.
- `ROADMAP.md` may imply that Phase 3 protects the convenience-bearing seam and Phase 6 protects the content-bearing seam, but only as seam protection for later choice rather than as roadmap commitments.
- `REQUIREMENTS.md` may imply that future capability families stay split across wrappers/retention, distribution/durability, and content operations/history, with no requirement yet that turns those into one monetized membership posture.

## What each doc must not imply

- `PROJECT.md` must not imply that `editorial/content` has already won the long-arc ranking, and must not relegate `hosted convenience` to a mere implementation detail under remote/private recurrence.
- `LONG-ARC.md` must not imply that the hosting ladder already chooses official convenience as the mature-product identity, and must not turn layered cadence into evidence that editorial/programming is the settled money doctrine.
- `ROADMAP.md` must not imply that current phases are proto-commitments to content monetization or official hosted service, and must not convert seam-preservation work into a hidden mature-product order.
- `REQUIREMENTS.md` must not imply a generic premium/membership future, must not attach access rights or service promises to future requirements by default, and must not let recurring-content or always-on-host wording act as a stealth winner-selection mechanism.

## What must remain explicit

- `editorial/content` is the current stress-tested default only in the narrow challenge-round sense: the more doctrine-compatible cross-archetype family, not a closed winner.
- `hosted convenience` is a serious rival, especially for recurring private remote play, and should not be demoted back to a minor footnote.
- `content cadence/programming` and `service-bearing convenience` are separate future families with different obligation profiles.
- `paid guaranteed participation` remains outside the ordinary ladder and should not be used to settle this ordering indirectly.
- The ranking could still change if remote/private recurrence becomes dominant, or if the project cannot sustain enough authored/programmed content supply.

## Semantic drift watchpoints

- `premium`
  - Too vague. It can silently collapse content/programming, convenience/service, and participation-rights branches into one word.
- `membership`
  - Risks smuggling recurring benefit obligations and entitlement language into docs that are supposed to preserve open family ordering.
- `support`
  - Must stay distinct from room access, service obligation, and guaranteed participation.
- `hosted`
  - Must distinguish privately hosted remote rooms, trusted operators, provider/partner hosts, and official hosted convenience. Not every use of `hosted` means the convenience family has been chosen.
- `content cadence`, `daily challenge`, `programming`, `library growth`
  - These should not be treated as automatic proof that `editorial/content` has won.
- `persistent remote access`, `always-on`, `operator convenience`, `easier room start`
  - These should not be treated as automatic proof that `hosted convenience` has won.

## Sensitivity lane routing

- Primary: `05-sensitivity-lane-i-canon-doctrine`
  - Use this docket to test whether `PROJECT.md`, `LONG-ARC.md`, and `REQUIREMENTS.md` preserve the rivalry without hard-coding a winner.
- Primary: `05-sensitivity-lane-ii-roadmap-rerun`
  - Use this docket to test whether `ROADMAP.md` preserves both Phase 3 and Phase 6 as seam-bearing work rather than as disguised money-family commitments.
- Coordination edge:
  - If the real pressure comes from `premium access`, `priority`, `reserved participation`, or other promise grammar, route that to docket `B2` rather than letting B1 absorb service-identity logic.
  - If the real pressure comes from `official hosted convenience` versus `provider/partner host` identity, route that to docket `C1` rather than treating B1 as the whole hosting answer.

## What remains open after this docket

- The exact archetype breakpoint where recurring private remote play makes `hosted convenience` outrank `editorial/content`.
- Whether the project can sustain enough authored/programmed content supply for `editorial/content` to keep its current default status.
- Whether the first real later value surface would be content/programming, convenience/service, or a narrower hybrid that still preserves the family split.
- The exact benefit language, support promises, and service-boundary wording that could quietly re-rank the families. That remains for docket `B2`.
- The exact hosting-identity branching inside `hosted convenience`, especially official vs provider/partner-host forms. That remains for docket `C1`.
- Any roadmap commitment. This docket only bounds the doctrine and wording discipline; it does not pick the later winner.
