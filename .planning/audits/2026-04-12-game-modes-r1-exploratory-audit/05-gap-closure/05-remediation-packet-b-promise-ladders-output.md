# 05 Remediation Packet B: Promise Grammar And Economic Ladders

## Packet framing

- Mode: `synthesis`
- Classification: `initial architecture research/planning`
- Question: what is the honest early support posture for Prix Guesser, which later money families should remain preserved, and where does payment cross into a stronger service obligation?
- Scope:
  - support-only posture
  - support-plus-beta windows
  - queue / capacity honesty tools
  - hosted convenience
  - editorial programming / content families
  - what paying should and should not buy
- Non-goals:
  - exact prices or tier counts
  - final payment mechanics
  - final host-ecology ranking beyond the burden threshold this packet depends on
  - public-commercial launch planning
- Stop condition: enough direct outside-repo grounding to compare the live families without treating earlier repo syntheses as if they were direct external evidence

`[governing:cited]` This packet inherits the repo's private-first center, explicit visibility states, and support/access/service-obligation separation from `PROJECT.md:136-147`, `LONG-ARC.md:81-129`, and `REQUIREMENTS.md:137-144`.

`[assumed:reasoned]` The key mistake to repair is not "we chose the wrong price model." It is "we moved too quickly from a useful promise grammar to a ranked later-money thesis."

## What is already settled

`[governing:cited]` Already settled canon, not reopened here:

- Prix Guesser is private-first, watchable, and browser-first; broader public discovery is a later deliberate obligation step, not the default center (`PROJECT.md:138-146`).
- Visibility state, hosting shape, support promise, and service obligation are separate axes and should stay separate (`LONG-ARC.md:81-102`, `116-129`).
- Optional support can exist without changing room access; paid guaranteed access is a later-stage posture, not a Milestone 1 or default Milestone 2 assumption (`LONG-ARC.md:122-129`).
- Public trust, moderation, public uptime, and commercialization hardening remain deferred (`LONG-ARC.md:155-166`; `REQUIREMENTS.md:139-144`).
- Round 2A's archetypes are live steering context: local recurring party, private online sync, solo async ritual, and bounded public/event shell all produce different value and burden profiles (`round-2a-experience-archetypes-output.md:63-184`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` What was not yet settled:

- whether the earliest honest support posture should be pure support-only or support-plus-some-access layer
- whether hosted convenience or editorial/content should already be treated as the dominant later money family
- which burdens are archetype-specific versus broadly shared

## Reference translation

`[assumed:reasoned]` Translation discipline for the main references:

| Reference | What is analogous | What is not analogous | Borrowed as concern vs solution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Patreon | direct support, thin tiers, benefit tracking, billing confusion, creator-operated fulfillment | creator membership is not the same as a multiplayer room service; Patreon communities are often audience-first rather than private-session-first | borrow mostly as `concern`: vague benefits create inbox load and entitlement; borrow only a narrow `solution`: support-only or extremely thin benefits can stay low-obligation |
| Steam Playtest / Keys / Early Access | limited access by capacity, clear "playable now" threshold, warning against future promises | Steam is public storefront infrastructure at much larger scale and assumes public customers rather than trusted rooms | borrow as `concern`: paid unfinished access creates expectation debt fast; borrow as `solution`: free limited signups, capped beta windows, and explicit current-state language |
| Home Assistant / Nabu Casa | local-first or self-hosted core with paid convenience on top | smart-home automation is not a social game and its moderation burden is structurally different | borrow both: `solution` for "core remains local/private while convenience is sold later"; `concern` for how quickly remote convenience creates support/security duties |
| Foundry VTT | self-hosted core, managed hosting partners, premium content, creator-linked content access | TTRPG infrastructure has longer sessions, heavier operator role, and a mature creator ecosystem | borrow both: `solution` for separating core software, hosting convenience, and content purchases; `concern` that each family creates different operational burdens and should not be collapsed into one premium ladder |

## Negative-case / failure evidence note

`[evidenced:cited]` Negative evidence sampled for this packet:

- Patreon member access is easy to misread even on a platform designed for memberships. Patrons are told to inspect tier descriptions, contact creators when benefits are unclear, and negotiate refunds directly with creators if promised benefits are not delivered ([Access my benefits](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/204606265-Access-my-benefits)).
- Patreon explicitly ships a benefit-tracking workflow because recurring benefits become owed work: due dates, monthly cadence, member lists, shipping details, and completion states all become part of operations ([Track delivery with Benefits feature](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/360004710212-Track-delivery-with-Benefits-feature)).
- Patreon also documents a concrete creator case where ambiguous billing grammar produced hundreds of confused messages every month, 30-40% early churn, and clear operator dread until the billing model was simplified ([WhatchaGot2Say subscription billing case study](https://creatorhub.patreon.com/articles/how-whatcha-got2-say-keeps-members-around-longer-with-subscription-billing)).
- Steam warns developers not to ask customers to bet on the future, not to use Early Access as crowdfunding, and not to charge for Playtests. That is direct negative evidence that once money and unfinished access mix, the obligation surface changes immediately ([Steam Early Access](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/earlyaccess?l=english), [Steam Playtest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest)).
- Steam Playtest also warns that removing access without communication may frustrate or disappoint the community. Even free limited-access programs create expectation debt if their capacity grammar is vague ([Steam Playtest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest)).
- Nabu Casa's troubleshooting and insecure-instance restrictions show that hosted convenience creates a new support and safety burden around certificates, auth settings, bans, and secure versions that a purely local-first surface can avoid ([About remote access](https://support.nabucasa.com/hc/en-us/articles/26469707849629-About-Home-Assistant-remote-access), [403 forbidden](https://support.nabucasa.com/hc/en-us/articles/26203603012509-Unable-to-reach-Home-Assistant-Cloud-403-forbidden), [Insecure instance detected](https://www.nabucasa.com/more-info/insecure-instance/)).

`[assumed:reasoned]` Negative-case implication:

- unclear payment grammar does not merely create mild confusion
- it creates direct support load, churn, resentment, and operator avoidance
- therefore early money posture should optimize for low ambiguity before it optimizes for monetization breadth

## Live alternatives

`[assumed:reasoned]` The live comparison space after direct external grounding is:

| Alternative | What money buys | Access effect | Main burden created | Current packet judgment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Support-only posture` | gratitude, continuity, optional recognition | none | low; messaging and light supporter admin only | strongest early rung |
| `Support-plus-beta windows` | support plus time-bounded preview or test access | bounded, temporary, explicitly not full service | medium; expectation handling, test-ops, selection fairness | keep alive narrowly, do not default to it |
| `Queue / capacity honesty tools` | not primarily a money family; a truth-telling control surface | explicit waitlist, capped signup, access batches, maybe sold-out slots | medium; communication discipline, but healthier than silent favoritism | should arrive before any monetized access priority |
| `Hosted convenience` | easier official remote access, persistence, backup, account-linked convenience | real convenience change, not necessarily fairness change | medium-high; status, support, security, and incident communication | preserve as a later family, not first-value |
| `Editorial programming / content` | curated drops, premium packs, event-like authored content, partner content | changes content/program access more than room fairness | medium; cadence, curation, fulfillment, library coherence | preserve as a later family, especially for solo/editorial return |
| `Paid guaranteed access / priority` | dependable participation rights, better slot rights, faster service | yes, directly | high; customers, refunds, uptime, support, queue legitimacy | explicit non-live threshold, remain deferred |

## Findings

### 1. The strongest early posture is `support-only`, not `support-plus-access`

`[evidenced:cited]` Patreon explicitly supports general-support tiers, non-benefit support motivation, and paying more without upgrading access or perks ([tiers and benefits](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/203913559-I-m-a-creator-what-are-tiers-and-benefits-), [advanced tax settings](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043191232-About-Advanced-Sales-Tax-Settings), [increasing payment amount](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/27288560659981-Increasing-your-payment-amount-not-upgrading-tier)).

`[assumed:reasoned]` That matters because the repo's current center already assumes the core value is private-first play, not gated service access. Early support can therefore be honest if it is framed as:

- backing the project
- helping cover development or hosting cost
- not buying better room rights
- not buying uptime or issue-resolution guarantees

### 2. `Queue / capacity honesty` is first-value, but it is not the same thing as monetization

`[evidenced:cited]` Steam Playtest's default limited-signup model is explicit: request access, grant in batches as capacity allows, communicate changes, and do not charge for the playtest ([Steam Playtest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest)).

`[assumed:reasoned]` This makes queue/capacity honesty a high-value transitional tool for Prix Guesser if demand ever exceeds what the current operator posture can support. The right early move is:

- clear waitlist or signup language
- sold-out or capped surfaces when real capacity exists
- explicit notes about why access is limited
- no fake neutrality where support money quietly changes admission odds

### 3. `Support-plus-beta windows` are real, but expectation-heavy

`[evidenced:cited]` Steam allows small pre-release beta-key programs for backers, but caps them and explicitly forbids turning them into open-ended ongoing crowdfunding access; Steam Early Access separately insists customers buy based on current value, not future promises ([Steam Keys](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/keys), [Steam Early Access](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/earlyaccess?l=english)).

`[assumed:reasoned]` The closest useful translation is not "sell early room rights." It is:

- if a test surface exists, make it a bounded learning program
- say what participants get now
- say what they do not get
- avoid implying that support payment grants durable participation priority

`[assumed:reasoned]` So `support-plus-beta` remains live only as a narrow transitional instrument, not as the default ethical early posture.

### 4. `Hosted convenience` is a strong later family precisely because it changes the obligation profile

`[evidenced:cited]` Home Assistant and Nabu Casa separate the local-first core from cloud convenience, then sell remote access, backups, and voice integrations while funding ongoing project work ([Home Assistant](https://www.home-assistant.io/), [Nabu Casa](https://www.nabucasa.com/)). Foundry similarly keeps a self-hosted core and layers managed hosting partners that sell always-on access, backups, and low-friction hosting ([Hosting Options Guide](https://foundryvtt.com/article/hosting/), [Partnerships](https://foundryvtt.com/article/partnerships/)).

`[evidenced:cited]` Those same ecosystems also expose the support cost: remote-access troubleshooting, insecure-instance blocking, certificate issues, backup/recovery expectations, and status/support burden all become part of the promise surface ([Nabu Casa remote-access docs](https://support.nabucasa.com/hc/en-us/articles/26469707849629-About-Home-Assistant-remote-access)).

`[assumed:reasoned]` This strengthens `hosted convenience` as a preserved later family while weakening any claim that it should already be the dominant mature-product money story.

### 5. `Editorial programming / content` is also real, but it is a different burden family

`[evidenced:cited]` Foundry's premium-content system and marketplace show a clear content family distinct from hosting: users can buy or subscribe to modules, adventures, and assets without changing who hosts the game or how session access is governed ([Premium Content](https://foundryvtt.com/article/premium-content/), [Partnerships](https://foundryvtt.com/article/partnerships/)).

`[assumed:reasoned]` This strengthens the repo's instinct that later money does not have to be a service subscription. But it also means the burden moves to:

- editorial cadence
- content quality control
- curation and packaging
- compatibility and activation support

`[assumed:reasoned]` In Prix Guesser terms, this fits later authored drops, premium packs, or event-programming better than early-room access monetization.

### 6. The real posture-change line is still `dependable paid participation`

`[evidenced:cited]` Steam's rules and Nabu Casa's support surface converge on the same principle: the moment money is tied to current usable access, the operator owes clear current-state communication, support handling, and fair treatment ([Steam Early Access](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/earlyaccess?l=english), [Nabu Casa](https://www.nabucasa.com/pricing/)).

`[assumed:reasoned]` For Prix Guesser, the threshold remains:

- payment buys guaranteed room slots
- or queue priority
- or faster issue handling
- or a stronger uptime promise

At that point the project is no longer merely accepting support. It is selling dependable service.

## First-value vs later-value

`[assumed:reasoned]` Comparative split:

| Family | First-value | Later-value | Preserve-without-prioritizing | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Support-only posture` | high | medium | no; this is the likely baseline | it lets people back the project without creating access or service debt |
| `Support-plus-beta windows` | low-medium | medium | yes | useful only when a bounded learning surface exists and expectations are tightly managed |
| `Queue / capacity honesty tools` | high | high | not a premium family; a required discipline | capacity truth-telling is valuable before and after any future charging |
| `Hosted convenience` | low | high | yes | strong later value for remote coordination, but it creates support/security/status obligations too early if prioritized now |
| `Editorial programming / content` | medium | high | yes | already aligned with the solo/editorial branch, but depends on proven cadence and curation capacity |
| `Paid guaranteed access / priority` | none | possible but heavily conditional | explicit deferral | it marks a service-posture change rather than a light support posture |

`[assumed:reasoned]` Main packet judgment:

- `support-only` has the strongest first-value
- `hosted convenience` and `editorial/content` have the strongest later-value
- both later families should be preserved without being chosen now
- `support-plus-beta` is a narrow transitional tool, not a preferred stable answer

## Archetype-to-burden translation

`[assumed:reasoned]` Translation across Round 2A archetypes:

| Archetype | Best early fit | Burden if support-only | Burden if support-plus-beta | Burden if hosted convenience | Burden if editorial/content | Main caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Local recurring party` | support-only | low; the core value already exists privately | medium; beta windows can distort a house-night ritual into access management | low-medium later; not much first-value unless remote coordination becomes common | medium later; packs or drops can matter, but the social loop does not require monetized access | do not turn trusted-room fairness into donor privilege |
| `Private online sync` | support-only plus capacity honesty | low-medium; remote users may want to contribute to hosting costs | medium-high; test access can be useful, but fairness and scheduling optics matter | high later-value; remote hosting convenience clearly helps here, but brings uptime/support burden | medium later; content helps, but transport and coordination pain may dominate first | hosted convenience belongs here more than in local play, but still should not be promised before the support posture is ready |
| `Solo async ritual` | support-only or later editorial/content | low | low-medium; limited previews can work if framed as editorial preview, not service access | low-medium; hosted convenience matters less if solo play works without it | high later-value; authored drops, evergreen libraries, and premium packs map naturally to solo ritual return | do not let solo-friendly content cadence force a full live-service subscription posture |
| `Bounded public/event shell` | capacity honesty first | medium; support can exist, but public-facing audiences will read payment as a promise faster | high; supporter access to events quickly reads as entitlement or reservation rights | high; event shells make status, moderation, and incident handling matter earlier | medium-high; event programming can fit, but it also creates schedule and editorial obligations | this archetype is exactly why public-facing money posture should not drive the early default |

`[assumed:reasoned]` Cross-archetype synthesis:

- a money family that feels harmless for trusted recurring groups can become entitlement-heavy in bounded event shells
- hosted convenience becomes more attractive as private online sync matters more
- editorial/content becomes more attractive as solo ritual and authored return matter more
- this is why the packet cannot name one universal later winner yet

## Trigger conditions

`[assumed:reasoned]` Concrete conditions that would move a branch up or down:

| Branch | Promote when | Demote when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Support-only posture` | players actively ask how to support; support language can stay simple; no recurring perk debt is required | supporters immediately expect better room rights, faster fixes, or private support promises |
| `Support-plus-beta windows` | a real test surface exists; capacity is limited for legitimate learning reasons; the project can explain scope and duration clearly | supporters start reading payment as a durable entitlement or general room priority |
| `Queue / capacity honesty tools` | signups exceed available rooms, operator time, or test slots; event participation needs explicit caps | there is no actual scarcity and the project is inventing queues for FOMO |
| `Hosted convenience` | remote-private play becomes common; self-host friction is blocking adoption; the project can support account-linked convenience and basic incident/status communication | usage remains mostly local/private-hosted and the convenience layer would create more support work than play value |
| `Editorial programming / content` | authored cadence proves itself; premium packs or event programming clearly strengthen repeat return; content support can stay bounded | cadence remains fragile and premium content would overcommit the creator to production promises |
| `Paid guaranteed access / priority` | only if the project deliberately chooses a customer-service posture with uptime, support, and refund clarity | any time the project still wants to remain hobby-grade, private-first, and low-obligation |

## Recommended carry-forward

`[projected:reasoned]` Carry-forward doctrine after this packet:

1. Early support posture should be `optional support first`, with payment explicitly decoupled from room fairness, queue priority, or guaranteed participation.
2. If scarcity appears, the first honest response should be `queue / capacity honesty`, not monetized priority.
3. `Support-plus-beta` should remain a narrow transition tool only for bounded learning surfaces, not a default membership story.
4. Preserve two later money families without ranking them yet:
   - `hosted convenience`
   - `editorial programming / content`
5. Keep the hard threshold explicit: once payment buys dependable participation, the project has crossed into a stronger service posture.

`[projected:reasoned]` Plain-language version for later canon work:

- people may be able to support Prix Guesser before they can buy anything meaningful
- if they later buy anything, the healthiest families are likely convenience or content, not fairness privilege
- the project should not quietly become a customer service business through ambiguous supporter perks

## Explicit deferrals

`[governing:cited]` Still deferred after this packet:

- exact prices, tiers, and packaging
- final choice of payment rail or storefront
- paid guaranteed access, queue priority, or reservation rights
- public uptime and incident-response promises
- any benefit that changes room fairness or moderator authority
- any claim that hosted convenience already outranks editorial/content, or vice versa
- any claim that host-ecology ranking is settled by this packet alone

## What should remain open

`[open:reasoned]` Still genuinely open:

- whether `hosted convenience` or `editorial/content` becomes the stronger later family after more mature-product synthesis
