# 05 Sensitivity Docket A1: Low-Burden Wrapper Ordering Output

## 1. Docket framing

- Mode: `feature-to-doc map`
- Classification: `initial architecture research/planning`
- Purpose: translate the challenge-round result for low-burden wrapper ordering into doc-level guardrails for sensitivity.
- This docket does not choose a winner.
- It exists to keep the docs from silently converting a two-candidate contest into a settled sequence.

## 2. Target feature pressure

- `showcase aftermath` and `share-by-link / challenge` remain the two main low-burden first-wrapper candidates after private rooms.
- No final winner exists yet.
- Both candidates are still bounded-open for the current private-first product center.
- `bounded live audience` remains a real rival pressure for bounded-event-shell archetypes, but it is not the same burden class and should not be smuggled in as the answer to this docket.
- `daily/programmed challenge` matters here only as later cadence pressure that can distort wording around `challenge`; it is not a peer winner in this docket.

## 3. Current gate classification

- Primary classification: `bounded-open`
- Embedded doctrine sensitivity may protect:
  - `showcase aftermath`, `share-by-link / challenge`, and `bounded live audience` are distinct families
  - the low-burden contest is still mostly between `showcase aftermath` and `share-by-link / challenge`
  - `bounded live audience` is heavier and archetype-conditional, not a cheap synonym for either low-burden candidate
- Embedded inquiry debt that must remain open:
  - the exact order between `showcase aftermath` and `share-by-link / challenge`
  - whether archetype differences change that order
  - where in-room summary/replay stops and a true aftermath wrapper begins

## 4. Current survivor and strongest rival(s)

- The surviving logic is a two-candidate frame, not a single winner: for the current private-first center, the first low-burden extension is still mostly `showcase aftermath` versus `share-by-link / challenge`.
- Inside that frame, each candidate is the other's strongest rival.
- The strongest external rival pressure is `bounded live audience` for hosted-night or bounded-event-shell conditions; docs must preserve that exception without letting it rewrite the low-burden contest into a universal live-audience answer.
- `daily/programmed challenge` remains secondary pressure only. It can strengthen later `challenge` language, but it does not settle this ordering question.

## 5. Why this pressure matters

- The canon already names `challenge`, `share-by-link`, `spectator`, `showcase`, `summary`, and `replay` in different contexts.
- Without a docket, those terms can accidentally imply a winner just because one branch is named more often than the other.
- The main risk is semantic drift, not lack of options:
  - `showcase` can mean low-burden read-after recap or a heavier spectator/public shell
  - `challenge` can mean frozen friend-to-friend play, recurring official cadence, or a broader competition surface
  - `summary` and `replay` can mean in-room closeout or a true aftermath wrapper
- If that drift is not controlled, sensitivity will protect the wrong thing and hard-code ordering by accident.

## 8. What each doc may safely imply

- The only committed wrapper today is the live private-room ritual.
- The next low-burden extension is still mostly a two-way contest between `showcase aftermath` and `share-by-link / challenge`.
- `bounded live audience` is real, but it is heavier and more conditional than either low-burden candidate.
- A doc may name async challenge, share-by-link, recap, summary, replay, spectator, or showcase examples if the wording does not convert an example into winner-proof.
- `daily/programmed challenge` may appear only as recurrence or cadence pressure unless event-object traits are named separately.

## 9. What each doc must not imply

- a final winner between `showcase aftermath` and `share-by-link / challenge`
- that Milestone 2 or a visibility-ladder rung already decides the wrapper order
- that `showcase` always means a later spectator/public shell and therefore cannot be a low-burden aftermath surface
- that `challenge` always names the same thing across friend-to-friend sharing, recurring official cadence, and broader competition shells
- that Phase 5 `summary/replay` or `OPS-V2-03` sharing/export already closes the wrapper question
- that `daily`, `featured`, `programmed`, or `weekend` language alone proves a new shell or an ordering decision

## 10. What must remain explicit

- The low-burden order remains unresolved.
- The challenge-round result was not `pick one`; it was `protect the two-candidate frame`.
- `showcase aftermath` means a read-after recap/results/share surface here, not automatically a public discoverable feed or a full spectator shell.
- `share-by-link / challenge` means time-shifted participation or replay surface here, not automatically official recurring challenge cadence.
- In-room summary/replay, outward aftermath, recurring challenge, and true event shell are separate layers.
- Phase 1 and the v1 roadmap preserve seams for both candidates without choosing either.

## 11. Semantic drift watchpoints

- `showcase`
  - in the canon it often means spectator/showcase shell
  - in this docket it includes low-burden read-after aftermath
- `challenge`
  - in the canon it can mean frozen friend-to-friend play, future async reuse, or recurring official challenge
  - these are related but not interchangeable
- `share`
  - pack export/import sharing is not the same thing as a playable share-by-link wrapper
- `summary`, `replay`, `recap`, `aftermath`
  - in-room closeout is not automatically the same as an outward aftermath surface
- `audience`, `spectator`
  - bounded passive viewers, interactive audience rights, and broader public/community participation are different obligation classes
- `daily`, `featured`, `programmed`, `weekend`
  - cadence markers are not event-shell proof by themselves

## 12. Sensitivity lane routing

- Lane I: `canon doctrine`
  - primary consumer for `PROJECT.md`, `LONG-ARC.md`, and `REQUIREMENTS.md`
  - test whether named examples stay illustrative rather than order-setting
- Lane II: `roadmap and rerun`
  - primary consumer for `ROADMAP.md` and `01-CONTEXT.md`
  - test whether v1 phase language preserves the seam without letting Phase 5 summary/replay or Phase 1 future-awareness pick a winner
  - `STATE.md` is only a relay surface here; the safe relay is simply that the ordering remains open
- Lane IV: `inquiry debt and anti-hard-coding`
  - primary consumer for the exact ordering question
  - flag any prose that converts repeated naming into a settled sequence
- Lane III: `preserve-only and reversal-sensitive futures`
  - no primary routing from this docket
  - only note spillover if low-burden language drifts into public/community or official-winner claims

## 13. What remains open after this docket

- the exact order between `showcase aftermath` and `share-by-link / challenge`
- whether recurring private groups, private online sync, and solo ritual want different first low-burden extensions
- where the boundary sits between in-room summary/replay and a true aftermath wrapper
- whether future canon eventually needs a more explicit neutral placeholder for aftermath so challenge-only naming does not dominate by accident
- whether official programmed challenge ever becomes early enough to pressure this ordering harder than it does now
