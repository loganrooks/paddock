# 05 Residual Gap Chunk B: Money-Family Ranking, Supporter Benefits, And Paid-Participation Reversal

## Chunk framing

- Mode: `synthesis`
- Classification: `initial architecture research/planning`
- Question: after packet B, the converged synthesis, and the deferral/rejection review, what later money-family shape is actually tighter, what supporter-benefit classes tacitly choose a branch, and whether `paid guaranteed participation` still belongs inside normal later-money language at all
- Scope:
  - later differentiation among `optional support`, `support-plus-beta`, `hosted convenience`, `editorial/content`, and `paid guaranteed participation`
  - benefit-to-branch translation rather than exact perk design

## What is already settled

`[governing:cited:internal]` Already settled carry-forward:

- `optional support` can exist without changing room access or service obligation
- `queue / capacity honesty` should arrive before monetized priority
- `hosted convenience` and `editorial/content` remain preserved as later live families
- `exact supporter benefits` are still open and branch-committing in practice
- `paid guaranteed participation` is already near the line where support becomes dependable service

`[evidenced:cited:internal]` The review tightened one important classification that this chunk keeps:

- `paid guaranteed participation / priority access` should not be treated as a normal later option inside the same bucket as support perks
- it is a `rejected-for-now` reversal-sensitive proposition because it changes fairness, refunds, support posture, and customer-service expectations

## What remains live

`[open:reasoned:internal]` The live space after the review is not a flat ladder. It is:

- `optional support first` as settled early posture
- `queue / capacity honesty` as a truth-telling control surface, not a money family
- `support-plus-beta` as a narrow transitional instrument, not a stable later family
- `editorial/content` as a later family aligned with authored-material value
- `hosted convenience` as a later family aligned with ongoing operational convenience and remote-service burden
- `paid guaranteed participation` as a reversal-sensitive branch that sits outside the ordinary later-family comparison
- `exact supporter benefits` as still-open parameter work that can silently pick any of the branches above

## Negative-case / failure evidence note

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` Negative evidence sampled in this chunk:

- Patreon members are expected to read tier descriptions, message creators for clarification, and often negotiate refunds directly with the creator when benefits do not match expectations.
- Patreon's benefits tooling turns recurring perks into tracked obligations with due states, completion states, addresses, payment history, and direct member messaging.
- Patreon documents a concrete case where billing grammar produced hundreds of confused messages per month and 30-40% early churn until the billing model was simplified.
- Steam warns developers not to make specific future promises in Early Access and notes that customers should buy based on present value, not hoped-for future value.
- Steam Playtest warns that removing access without communication may frustrate or disappoint the community even when the playtest is free.
- Nabu Casa support docs show that paid remote access produces real support and security incidents, not just nice convenience copy.
- StartPlaying's payment, cancellation, refund, and support flow shows that once payment buys a place in a session, operational dispute handling is part of the product.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Negative-case implication:

- `support burden`, `entitlement drift`, and `promise misread` are not secondary polish problems
- they are the main reason perk design cannot be separated from branch-family selection
- therefore the safe default is not `let benefits stay open because they are minor`; it is `keep benefits narrow until the family question is bounded`

## Findings

### 1. The later money space is not one `premium` ladder

`[evidenced:cited:internal+external-direct]` Packet B productively preserved several later money branches, but direct external comparison makes the family split sharper:

- `editorial/content` monetizes authored material and repeat-return surfaces
- `hosted convenience` monetizes an operational service layer
- `paid guaranteed participation` monetizes participation rights themselves

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Those are not small variations on one `premium` idea. They are different obligation families and should no longer be spoken about as one generic later rung.

### 2. `Editorial/content` now looks like the stronger general later-value family, while `hosted convenience` looks more conditional

`[evidenced:cited:internal+external-direct]` Foundry's premium-content flow shows that users can buy content without changing who hosts or how session authority works. Home Assistant / Nabu Casa and Foundry hosting show the opposite pattern for convenience: once the provider sells remote access or managed hosting, it inherits ongoing setup, status, and troubleshooting burdens.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` This does not justify a final global winner, but it does tighten the rough ranking:

- `editorial/content` is the more doctrine-aligned general mature-product family because it monetizes the authored substrate and repeat-return without selling room fairness
- `hosted convenience` is still live, but it now reads more clearly as an archetype- and trigger-dependent service branch, especially for `private online sync`

### 3. Supporter benefits are branch selectors, not harmless parameter detail

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` Patreon requires benefits to be listed and valued even for `General support`, and its tooling turns benefits into tracked deliverables. StartPlaying and Steam show that access-bearing perks create explicit entitlement and support surfaces quickly.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Benefit classes therefore map to branches like this:

| Benefit class | Branch it tacitly selects | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| thanks, recognition, optional credit, lightweight process notes | can still fit `optional support first` | does not change room rights, queue position, or official-service expectations if kept narrow |
| bounded previews, closed tests, temporary feature access | `support-plus-beta` | creates entry/exit expectations and fairness questions immediately |
| easier official room start, persistent remote access, backups, account-linked convenience | `hosted convenience` | sells operational convenience and therefore a real support surface |
| premium packs, recurring drops, subscriber-only authored material, event-programming access | `editorial/content` | sells authored substance and cadence rather than room fairness |
| reserved seats, queue priority, guaranteed event participation, faster support or issue response | `paid guaranteed participation` | sells participation rights or service priority directly |

### 4. `Support-plus-beta` should not be treated as a normal later money family

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` Steam Playtest, Steam Keys, and Steam Early Access all treat limited testing as a bounded, expectation-heavy instrument. Access must be reversible, current-state communication must be explicit, and future promises must be constrained.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` That means `support-plus-beta` belongs in a narrow transition lane only:

- only if a real test surface exists
- only if entry, exit, scope, and current-state language are explicit
- never as a disguised `membership` default

### 5. `Paid guaranteed participation` is more like booking/ticketing than like support

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` StartPlaying's player flow is priced `per player, per session`, has an Instant Book path, a 24-hour cancellation window, refund handling, and support verification. Steam Early Access and Nabu Casa similarly show that money tied to present usable access creates an obligation to communicate current state and treat purchasers fairly.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` This is stronger than packet B's earlier phrasing. `Paid guaranteed participation` should now be treated as:

- outside the ordinary later-money family ladder
- a reversal-sensitive move into booking/service semantics
- something that should not reappear through perk design, queue wording, or `premium` shorthand

### 6. Semantic drift check

`[evidenced:cited:internal+external-direct]` The loaded terms did drift:

| Term | Did it drift? | Drift shape | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `support` | yes | canon and synthesis use it as voluntary backing without access effect; Patreon-derived packet language sometimes leans on tier/membership structures where support is still represented as a benefit | keep `support` reserved for non-rights-altering backing in carry-forward prose |
| `premium` | yes | packet B and older lane names use it as an umbrella for multiple later families; Foundry uses it specifically for content; the review largely stops using it | treat `premium` as too compressive unless explicitly unpacked |
| `guaranteed` | yes, but productively sharpened | packet B said `paid guaranteed access / priority`; the synthesis said `dependable paid participation`; the review recast it as a rejected-for-now service-threshold proposition | use `paid participation` or `booking/service` language, not vague `premium access` language |
| `membership` | yes | Patreon references made `support-only membership` sound cleaner than it is; Patreon itself structurally models support as benefit-bearing membership tiers | avoid importing `membership` into product-facing doctrine unless recurring benefits and membership accountability are truly intended |

`[assumed:reasoned:internal]` The drift is load-bearing for `support`, `premium`, `guaranteed`, and `membership`, not just stylistic.

## What got tighter

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` This chunk tightened five things:

1. `Editorial/content` versus `hosted convenience` is no longer just an open tie. The families now have clearer default roles:
   - content is more general and substrate-aligned
   - convenience is more conditional and service-bearing
2. `Supporter benefits` now have a family-selection map rather than staying a vague open parameter bucket.
3. `Support-plus-beta` is now better bounded as a narrow transitional instrument instead of a stable later family.
4. `Paid guaranteed participation` is now more clearly outside the ordinary later ladder and should be treated as a reversal-sensitive booking/service branch.
5. `Premium` and `membership` are now less trustworthy umbrella terms for later canon or sensitivity work.

## What remains challengeable

`[open:reasoned:internal]` The following still remain challengeable:

- whether `editorial/content` should ever be treated as the global later winner, rather than only the stronger general default
- whether `hosted convenience` could outrank `editorial/content` if `private online sync` becomes the dominant center
- whether there is any Prix-specific `support-plus-beta` lane worth operating at all
- which exact recognition or low-obligation supporter benefits remain safe enough to use
- how room/group/event memory should shape supporter recognition without collapsing memory layers or creating a flat membership profile

## Archetype-to-burden translation

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Archetype translation after the residual pass:

| Archetype | First money posture | Later family with strongest fit | Main burden warning | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Trigger conditions

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Promotion conditions after this chunk:

| Branch | Promote when | Demote when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Optional support first` | support language can stay plainly decoupled from room rights and support response | supporters begin to expect better room access, faster fixes, or recurring deliverables |
| `Support-plus-beta` | a real bounded test lane exists and the project can state what participants get now, how long it lasts, and how access ends | supporters start reading it as durable priority or membership access |
| `Editorial/content` | authored cadence, evergreen-library value, and recap/review return loops become real enough to support premium material honestly | cadence is still fragile and premium material would become obligation theater |
| `Hosted convenience` | remote-private play is frequent enough that self-host friction, always-on availability, or room-start reliability becomes a dominant pain | usage remains mostly local/trusted-private and convenience would create more support debt than play value |
| `Paid guaranteed participation` | only if the project deliberately wants booking/ticketing semantics with refund, cancellation, queue, and support-response clarity | any time the project still wants support-first, private-first, low-obligation posture |

## Recommended carry-forward

`[projected:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Recommended carry-forward after this chunk:

1. Keep `optional support first` as the only stable early posture.
2. Treat `queue / capacity honesty` as the first answer to scarcity, not as a premium family.
3. Treat `editorial/content` as the stronger general later-value family for mature-product preservation.
4. Treat `hosted convenience` as a separate conditional family whose promotion requires stronger remote-private demand and willingness to run an official service surface.
5. Treat `support-plus-beta` as a bounded test instrument only, never as a default `membership` story.
6. Treat `paid guaranteed participation` as outside the ordinary later ladder and as a reversal-sensitive booking/service branch.
7. Avoid umbrella shorthand like `premium membership` in later canon or sensitivity work. Prefer explicit family terms:
   - `support`
   - `content`
   - `hosted convenience`
   - `paid participation`

- `paid guaranteed participation` is reversal-sensitive and should not be treated as an ordinary premium option

`[projected:reasoned:internal]` Later sensitivity should test for:

- any `supporter access`, `priority`, `reservation`, or `guaranteed slot` phrasing
- any blurred use of `premium` that collapses content, convenience, and participation into one family

`[projected:reasoned:internal]` Later sensitivity must not:

- force a final global winner between `editorial/content` and `hosted convenience`
