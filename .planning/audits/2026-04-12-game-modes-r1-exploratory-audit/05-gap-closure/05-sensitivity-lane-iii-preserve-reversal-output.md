# 05 Sensitivity Lane III: Preserve-Only And Reversal-Sensitive Futures Output

## 1. Lane framing

- Mode: `sensitivity lane`
- Classification: `replanning/revision/gap-filling`
- Owned burden:
  - preserve-only seam protection
  - reversal-sensitive boundary protection
  - cross-docket term-drift checks where preserve-only or reversal-sensitive pressure can be distorted by adjacent hosting, money-family, wrapper, or discovery language
- Coordination note:
  - this lane is the authority on `preserve-only` and `reversal-sensitive` classification for the assigned dockets
  - it is not the sole owner of planning-surface wording
  - `ROADMAP.md` and `01-CONTEXT.md` are inspected here to detect relay or distortion, but roadmap/rerun carry-forward work still belongs to `Lane II`
- Scope discipline:
  - no winner selection
  - no canon patching in this lane
  - no attempt to convert preserve-only seams into promoted roadmap branches

## 2. Docs inspected

- Canon doctrine surfaces:
  - `PROJECT.md`
  - `LONG-ARC.md`
  - `REQUIREMENTS.md`
- Planning-surface relay docs:
  - `ROADMAP.md`
  - `01-CONTEXT.md`
- Rerun-status note:
  - `01-CONTEXT.md` was inspected as a pre-rerun steering snapshot, not as execution-approved canon

## 3. Dockets consumed

- Primary lane inputs:
  - `B2` service promises and paid participation
  - `C2` collectives, plural hosts, and hybrid risks
  - `D1` contribution identity
  - `D2` discovery and unsupported listings
- Cross-docket drift references used to avoid false harmonization:
  - `B1` later money-family ordering
  - `C1` hosting identity branches
- Shared drift checks applied:
  - `challenge` / `showcase` / `spectator` / `audience`
  - `hosted` / `operator` / `hybrid`
  - `event` / `featured` / `weekend`
  - `support` / `premium` / `membership` / `priority` / `reserved`
  - `creator` / `partner` / `reviewed` / `unsupported` / `marketplace`

## 4. Direct doctrine protections

- `PROJECT.md`, `LONG-ARC.md`, `REQUIREMENTS.md`, and `01-CONTEXT.md` all preserve the main `B2` reversal boundary:
  - no paid guaranteed access promise now
  - support, access, and service obligation stay distinct
  - `DEF-03` keeps paid guaranteed access and commercialization hardening deferred
- `LONG-ARC.md` preserves the core `C2` non-foreclosure doctrine more clearly than any other canon file:
  - broad community-host governance is deferred
  - the deferral is explicitly non-final
  - hosting, support, visibility, and service obligation remain split rather than folded into one maturity story
- `PROJECT.md`, `LONG-ARC.md`, and `REQUIREMENTS.md` keep the official curated trust anchor intact for `D1` and `D2`:
  - curated packs are the live baseline
  - public discovery and open creator publishing remain deferred
  - no doc currently promotes a creator-platform or marketplace identity as the expected next layer
- `ROADMAP.md` and `01-CONTEXT.md` currently reinforce, rather than weaken, the main reversal guardrails:
  - no `premium`, `membership`, `priority`, `reserved-slot`, or `ticket` language appears as positive planning doctrine
  - no planning surface currently puts unsupported listings into the main guest-facing library
  - no planning surface currently treats hybrid official-plus-private hosting as already-clean doctrine

## 5. Bounded-open anti-hard-coding findings

- The owned preserve/reversal branches are safest where adjacent bounded-open families are still kept distinct:
  - `B1`: content/programming value and hosted convenience still read as separate later money families
  - `C1`: trusted operators, official hosted convenience, provider/partner hosts, and unsupported private operators are not collapsed into one `hosted` story
- `ROADMAP.md` stays mostly capability-oriented:
  - Phase 3 protects operator flow and private-host parity
  - Phase 6 protects curation and calibration seams
  - this avoids hard-coding later money, host, or discovery winners through phase prose
- The contribution/discovery branch remains protected against hard-coding mostly by deferral and neutrality, not by explicit positive naming:
  - that is safe against premature closure
  - it is weaker than direct explicit branch-preservation language

## 6. Preserve-only seam findings

- `C2: collective / mirror governance`
  - Canonical branch protection:
    - `LONG-ARC.md` keeps `broad community-host governance` deferred under an explicit `not never` rule, which is enough to stop the seam from being honestly classified as dead.
    - `PROJECT.md` and `REQUIREMENTS.md` remain host-identity-neutral enough that they do not contradict that preserved seam.
  - Planning-surface drift handoff to `Lane II`:
    - `ROADMAP.md` currently talks about operator-run local or privately hosted deployment, not plural-host doctrine.
    - That is safe now, but later rerun wording must keep `hosted` and `operator` capability-oriented so future planning does not collapse all plurality into `official + private` shorthand.
- `D1: partner brand / creator profile`
  - Canonical branch protection:
    - the canon keeps official curation as the trust anchor and defers broad creator ecosystems, so the preserve-only rival is not being promoted prematurely.
  - Canonical weakness:
    - the preserve-only creator-profile seam is still mostly implicit.
    - the docs currently read more like `official curation now, broad creator ecosystem later` than like `official curation now, with a preserve-only creator-identity rival still alive`.
  - Planning-surface drift handoff to `Lane II`:
    - `ROADMAP.md` Phase 6 language about broader pack libraries stays safe only while it remains library-neutral.
    - if later rerun notes make pack growth sound guest-facing by default, they will erase this preserve-only branch by omission.
- `D2: reviewed submissions and segregated unsupported listings`
  - Canonical branch protection:
    - curated official packs remain the guest-facing baseline.
    - `DEF-05` keeps open creator publishing deferred.
  - Canonical weakness:
    - no current canon file explicitly says that reviewed submissions are preserve-only or that unsupported listings belong in a segregated operator/admin surface rather than the main guest-facing library.
    - the seam survives through trust-anchor and deferral logic, not through explicit status-language doctrine.
  - Planning-surface drift handoff to `Lane II`:
    - `ROADMAP.md` does not currently violate this boundary.
    - future roadmap or rerun language around `broader pack libraries`, `sharing`, or `outside packs` would need explicit status separation to avoid turning preserved seams into implied guest-facing discovery.

## 7. Reversal-sensitive findings

- `B2: paid guaranteed participation / priority / reserved-slot`
  - Current docs are strongly aligned against reversal:
    - `PROJECT.md` says there is `no paid guaranteed access promise`
    - `LONG-ARC.md` keeps paid guaranteed access as a later-stage posture, not current doctrine
    - `REQUIREMENTS.md` defers paid guaranteed access and commercialization hardening
    - `01-CONTEXT.md` preserves `no paid-access obligation`
    - `ROADMAP.md` explicitly says later phases do not decide paid access or SLA-like uptime guarantees
  - Planning-surface drift handoff to `Lane II`:
    - the one nearby risky term is `seat claim` in Phase 4 carry-forward constraints
    - current usage is safe because it sits beside `join` and `rejoin`, which frames it as transient live-room state ownership rather than reservation or admission
    - future rerun wording must keep that term temporal and room-local
- `C2: hybrid official-plus-private hosting is naturally clean`
  - No current doc asserts this reversal-sensitive claim.
  - The real risk is semantic reuse:
    - `PROJECT.md` uses `hybrid sessions`
    - `01-CONTEXT.md` uses `hybrid` for media/fallback structure
    - neither usage is about host ecology
  - Planning-surface drift handoff to `Lane II`:
    - if later planning uses `hybrid` for hosting without explicit host-type and support-owner qualifiers, it will create false calm around official-plus-private coexistence
- `D2: marketplace / public publishing re-entry`
  - The current docs keep public discovery, public publishing, and marketplace posture clearly deferred.
  - No planning surface currently reintroduces creator-platform language through softer `library` or `discover` wording.
  - Planning-surface drift handoff to `Lane II`:
    - Phase 6 is the main future risk surface because content-library growth language sits there already
    - if later reruns add `reviewed`, `community`, or `outside pack` language without status qualifiers, the reversal boundary will weaken quickly

## 8. Inquiry-debt protection findings

- The docs still protect the open questions that should remain open:
  - what later trigger, if any, would justify a true paid event/service identity rather than the current private-support posture
  - whether collective or mirror governance ever rises above preserve-only
  - whether later outside contributions stay inside Prix-controlled editorial activation or climb toward stronger creator-profile identity
  - whether reviewed submissions, if ever activated, feed only official editorial activation or also a segregated unsupported/operator catalog
- `01-CONTEXT.md` stays appropriately narrow for these questions:
  - it preserves future wrappers and layered identities
  - it does not try to settle contribution, discovery, event-memory, or paid-access doctrine inside Phase 1
- `ROADMAP.md` also holds the line:
  - current phases preserve technical and content seams without pretending those seams already answer later governance, discovery, or paid-access questions

## 9. Semantic drift findings

- `hosted` / `operator` / `hybrid`
  - This is the sharpest cross-docket drift zone.
  - `hosted` can mean privately hosted remote capability, later official convenience, or later provider-host shape.
  - `operator` can mean room runner rather than support owner.
  - `hybrid` currently means play topology or content-shape mixture, not host ecology.
- `support` / `premium` / `membership` / `priority` / `reserved`
  - Current docs are clean here because only `support` and `service obligation` appear directly.
  - No positive `premium` or `membership` doctrine exists.
  - `seat claim` is the only nearby term that could drift toward reservation semantics if later planning loosens its context.
- `creator` / `partner` / `reviewed` / `unsupported` / `marketplace`
  - The canon defers open creator ecosystems and marketplace posture, which is good reversal protection.
  - It does not yet provide a stable status ladder for `reviewed`, `approved`, `curated`, and `unsupported`.
  - That means future planning prose could drift simply by using intuitive language without explicit status definitions.
- `challenge` / `showcase` / `spectator` / `audience`
  - Current docs use these mainly as wrapper and legibility terms.
  - No current doc links them to paid admission, member rights, or creator-discovery expectations.
  - That separation should be kept explicit because these wrapper terms are the easiest place for `B2` or `D2` drift to hide later.
- `event` / `featured` / `weekend`
  - Current uses remain theme, cadence, or wrapper-context language rather than proof of event-service doctrine.
  - No present drift was found here for the owned lane, but later event-shell phrasing could still become a backdoor for paid-entry or public-library assumptions.

## 10. Concrete doc adjustments or carry-forward notes

- No direct canon edit is justified from this lane alone.
  - The main reversal-sensitive boundaries already hold.
  - The main preserve-only problem is visibility, not active contradiction.
- Carry forward to `Lane II`:
  - keep `seat claim` explicitly tied to transient live-room state ownership
  - keep `hosted`, `private host`, and `operator` capability-oriented unless host type and support ownership are named explicitly
  - do not let `hybrid` switch from topology language to host-ecology doctrine without qualification
  - if roadmap/rerun text expands Phase 6 content-library language, add explicit separation between:
    - official curated library
    - reviewed intake
    - segregated unsupported/operator listings
  - keep `challenge`, `showcase`, `event`, and `featured` separate from paid-access or creator-platform assumptions
- Carry forward to converged synthesis:
  - decide whether preserve-only seams should stay protected only by deferral/neutrality or receive light explicit non-foreclosure language in canon later
  - if explicit language is desired, keep it non-promotional and status-aware rather than turning preserved seams into roadmap candidates

## 11. What still requires converged synthesis

- whether the canon needs a light explicit non-foreclosure sentence for:
  - collective / mirror governance
  - partner-brand / creator-profile identity
  - reviewed submissions and segregated unsupported listings
- whether the current negative-space protection is enough, or whether it leaves preserved seams too dependent on audit memory
- the exact later threshold where a true event/service posture would reopen participant-paid access explicitly rather than by drift
- the exact host-type, support-owner, and pack-status metadata needed if multiple host or library states ever become visible
- how `D1` middle-space editorial activation and `D2` reviewed/unsupported status language should fit together without collapsing into either house-only authorship or marketplace posture
