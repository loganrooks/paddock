# Lane E: Grassroots Transition Hosting

## Lane framing

`[governing:cited]` This is a `synthesis` lane, not a blank ideation lane. The task is to close the transition-hosting question inside the mature-product gap while preserving the already-settled private-first center, substrate-plus-wrappers posture, layered memory/cadence doctrine, staged visibility, and Round 2B non-foreclosure seams (`05-gap-closure-lane-e-grassroots-transition-hosting-task-spec.md:25-45`, `05-gap-closure-lane-common-scaffold.md:18-27`, `05-gap-closure-lane-common-scaffold.md:46-54`).

`[governing:cited]` The lane question is narrow: how Prix Guesser could move from `I host it myself` toward online play and modest broader use while reducing sole-operator burden, keeping guest join browser-first, and staying explicitly distinct from full browser peer-to-peer room authority (`05-gap-closure-lane-e-grassroots-transition-hosting-task-spec.md:27-38`, `05-gap-closure-synthesis.md:61-67`, `05-gap-closure-synthesis.md:108-110`).

`[governing:cited]` Non-goals are equally important. This lane is not deciding exact pricing or packaging, not reopening whether browser P2P is "cool," not treating earlier repo research as closure proof, and not collapsing into Lane D's support/premium/obligation ladder (`05-gap-closure-lane-e-grassroots-transition-hosting-task-spec.md:40-45`, `05-gap-closure-synthesis.md:158-159`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` Good stopping point for this lane: a planning-ready answer about which transition-hosting shapes remain alive, which one(s) should carry forward as the main grassroots branch, what must stay architecturally open, and what still needs later testing or separate lane treatment.

## What is already settled

`[evidenced:cited]` The project center is already settled as private-first, watchable, and browser-first for guests across local, LAN, and privately hosted remote play (`PROJECT.md:136-146`).

`[evidenced:cited]` The long-arc doctrine already defines the main hosting ladder as:

1. local or LAN operator flow
2. privately hosted remote rooms with standard browser access
3. modest hosted operation if later needed

and it explicitly says full peer-to-peer room authority is not the primary path (`LONG-ARC.md:104-114`).

`[evidenced:cited]` The doctrine already separates support, access, and service obligation, and keeps paid guaranteed access deferred (`LONG-ARC.md:116-129`, `LONG-ARC.md:155-166`).

`[evidenced:cited]` Phase 3 already assumes authoritative private rooms with one repeatable local or privately hosted operator flow, standard browser guest access, and no guest requirement to install operator networking tools (`ROADMAP.md:80-101`, `REQUIREMENTS.md:60-69`).

`[evidenced:cited]` The whole-job closeout already judged the mature-product/business/community thread only partially answered. Lane E is therefore closing a real remaining gap, not inventing a new one (`whole-job-verification-output.md:26-38`, `whole-job-verification-output.md:94-101`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` What remains open is not the baseline doctrine. What remains open is the transition answer sitting on top of that doctrine: when Logan should remain the only operator, when trusted others can carry whole authoritative rooms, when plural hosts become governance-heavy enough to count as a different product posture, and how supporter-funded official hosting relates to those steps.

## Path of inquiry

- Entry point: the synthesis explicitly elevated grassroots transition hosting to a first-class closure target and required a clean distinction from full browser peer-to-peer room authority (`05-gap-closure-synthesis.md:61-67`, `05-gap-closure-synthesis.md:108-110`).
- Branches considered:
  - Logan-only self-host from personal hardware
  - trusted volunteer-operated authoritative hosts
  - sanctioned mirrors or small host collectives
  - supporter-funded official hosted convenience
  - full browser P2P authority
  - broader public/federated community-host governance
- Branches pursued:
  - the first four as live transition shapes
  - browser P2P only as a contrast case, to prevent scope drift and category confusion
- Branches deferred or abandoned:
  - full browser P2P authority as the main transition answer
  - exact pricing, queue priority, or supporter entitlements
  - open federation or broad community-host governance
  - pack-signing, mirror verification, and legal/policy implementation detail
- Reframing:
  - the decisive question is not `centralized vs decentralized`
  - the decisive question is `who owns canonical room authority, who carries operator burden, and how invisible can that be kept for guests`

## Core closure questions

1. Which transition-hosting shapes reduce sole-operator burden without putting canonical room authority on guest devices?
2. What can a casual invited guest reasonably be asked to do, versus a supporter, versus a technically confident operator?
3. Which hosting shapes preserve stable browser-first join grammar across LAN, private remote hosting, and later hosted convenience?
4. When do volunteer hosts or sanctioned mirrors stop being a deployment convenience and start becoming a governance change?
5. What must remain architecturally open if the project wants to support trusted-circle operators now without foreclosing later official hosting?
6. What cannot be honestly closed yet because the bundle's targeted external-reference memo is still missing and there is no direct telemetry on operator adoption or room load?

## Findings

### Direct evidence

`[evidenced:cited]` The current canon already favors self-hostable authoritative rooms, browser-first guest join, and staged hosting transitions over full peer-to-peer authority (`LONG-ARC.md:104-114`, `PROJECT.md:138-146`, `ROADMAP.md:80-101`).

`[evidenced:cited]` The earlier hosting-transition research already found one coherent operational ladder: LAN host on one machine, private remote play from personal hardware, then modest hosted operation, while keeping guest join on normal browser HTTPS and treating Tailscale-like tools as operator-side rather than guest-side requirements (`2026-04-10-vision-hosting-wave/findings/02-hosting-transition.md:47-72`, `2026-04-10-vision-hosting-wave/findings/02-hosting-transition.md:119-130`, `2026-04-10-vision-hosting-wave/findings/02-hosting-transition.md:155-166`).

`[evidenced:cited]` The cooperative-scaling comparison pass found that the strongest cooperative branch is still `self-hostable authoritative rooms for trusted operators`, while `trusted-circle cooperative hosting or sanctioned mirrors` are only medium-high technically and much weaker operationally because plural hosts create versioning, accountability, privacy-disclosure, and support-boundary work (`2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:51-57`, `2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:87-93`, `2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:138-145`).

`[evidenced:cited]` The same comparison pass also concluded that only operator-layer cooperation can plausibly be invisible enough for casual users, while guest-side contribution quickly becomes adoption-hostile (`2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:95-101`).

`[evidenced:cited]` The funding and risk lanes both sharpened the same distinction: community hosting is not just another payment option, and it is not just a deployment trick. It changes topology, responsibility, trust labeling, and support ownership (`2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/02-funding-access-and-transparency-models.md:110-119`, `2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/02-funding-access-and-transparency-models.md:155-164`, `2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/04-security-trust-and-operational-risk.md:91-99`, `2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/04-security-trust-and-operational-risk.md:142-149`).

`[evidenced:cited]` Earlier risk work also drew the governance line this lane needs: trusted volunteers running known servers and end users relaying one another's traffic are not the same idea, and sanctioned mirrors look materially safer than peer relays (`2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/04-security-trust-and-operational-risk.md:93-99`, `2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/04-security-trust-and-operational-risk.md:126-127`).

### Inference and closure judgment

`[assumed:reasoned]` Lane E can close one important question now: the project does not need to choose between `Logan hosts forever` and `full official hosted service immediately`. There is a credible middle ground, but it is operator-layer authoritative hosting, not guest-layer P2P.

`[assumed:reasoned]` The strongest grassroots transition path is:

1. keep one canonical self-hostable authoritative bundle
2. let trusted technical supporters run that bundle as whole-room operators
3. only after that, consider either sanctioned mirrors/collectives or official hosted convenience as broader transition branches

This follows the existing doctrine and the older research more closely than any peer-assisted guest model (`LONG-ARC.md:104-114`, `2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:140-165`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` The key distinction is actor burden:

- casual invited guest should only open a normal link, scan a QR code, or enter a room code
- enthusiast supporter may optionally fund hosting continuity or run an operator-grade bundle
- technically confident operator may handle updates, secrets, logs, domains/tunnels, and room recovery

Once that distinction is explicit, most browser-P2P enthusiasm falls away because it asks the wrong actor to absorb the complexity (`2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:97-101`, `2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:103-111`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` `Trusted volunteer-operated authoritative hosts` are therefore a real live option, but only if they run the same canonical authority model as Logan. The lane does not support a separate "lightweight community-host mode" where trust, compatibility, or recovery rules are looser by default.

`[assumed:reasoned]` `Sanctioned mirrors or small host collectives` remain alive, but only as a governance-heavy branch. They are not the next automatic step after Logan's own hardware. They require explicit host identity, version compatibility, pack sync discipline, support boundaries, and probably different user-facing labeling than official hosting (`2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:89-93`, `2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:156-165`, `2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/02-funding-access-and-transparency-models.md:147-164`, `2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/02-funding-access-and-transparency-models.md:252-269`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` `Supporter-funded hosted convenience` is also a live option, but Lane E only closes the topology side of that story: it is the cleanest way to widen access without changing guest burden or diffusing authority, because it preserves one official hosted path. Whether funding should be donation-led, symbolic-tiered, queued, or eventually paid belongs to Lane D (`LONG-ARC.md:118-129`, `2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/02-funding-access-and-transparency-models.md:213-235`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` Full browser peer-to-peer room authority should remain outside the main transition ladder. This is not only because doctrine says so, but because the older cooperative-scaling work re-tested the question and still found P2P to be the worst fit for authoritative timers, reconnect correctness, clear responsibility, and casual guest invisibility (`LONG-ARC.md:114`, `2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:46-49`, `2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:53-57`, `2026-04-10-second-wave-scaling-governance/findings/05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:66-78`).

### Unknowns

`[open]` This lane cannot yet close:

- whether broader transition should prefer `official hosted convenience` before `sanctioned mirrors`, or the reverse, once demand grows beyond a trusted technical circle
- whether trusted friends will actually self-host often enough to matter in practice
- what minimum compatibility/signing/runbook standard a sanctioned mirror would need
- whether real operating costs will justify supporter-funded official hosting before simpler donation-led continuity

`[open]` Those unknowns remain because the targeted external-reference memo for volunteer-host and sanctioned-mirror patterns is still missing, and there is still no direct telemetry on operator adoption, room throughput, or incident load (`05-gap-closure-synthesis.md:118-120`, `05-gap-closure-synthesis.md:161-168`).

## Live options or operating shapes

`[assumed:reasoned]` The option space that remains honestly alive is:

| Operating shape | Guest burden | Operator burden | Trust clarity | Current lane judgment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Logan-operated authoritative hosting from local or personal hardware | lowest | highest on Logan | highest | governing baseline |
| trusted-circle volunteer operators running the same canonical bundle | low | medium, spread across a few technical supporters | medium-high if host mode is explicit | strongest grassroots extension |
| sanctioned mirrors or small host collectives | low | medium per operator, high collective governance | medium-low unless official/unofficial boundaries are explicit | conditional branch, not default next step |
| supporter-funded official hosted convenience | lowest | centralized on one official operator path | high | cleanest later widening path, but funding logic belongs to Lane D |
| browser P2P or guest-side cooperative authority | hidden or high | support/privacy burden becomes diffuse | low | not a carry-forward transition answer |

`[assumed:reasoned]` The important comparative result is not that one of these must be chosen now as the final mature-product answer. It is that only the first four keep canonical authority off guest devices, and only the middle three materially reduce Logan-only operator dependence.

## Recommended carry-forward

`[projected:reasoned]` Carry forward this ladder as the planning-facing answer from Lane E:

1. **Preserve one canonical self-hostable authoritative bundle.**
   This is the backbone that makes every other transition shape coherent.

2. **Treat trusted-circle volunteer operators as the primary grassroots extension.**
   This is the strongest non-solo branch because it reduces Logan-only dependence without changing the guest contract.

3. **Keep sanctioned mirrors / small host collectives alive only as a conditional resilience branch.**
   They require explicit governance and labeling before they should be treated as product-facing transition doctrine.

4. **Keep supporter-funded official hosted convenience alive as the cleanest broader-use branch.**
   But treat it as a separate question from who can host; Lane D must answer what money changes and what it does not.

5. **Do not carry forward full browser P2P authority as the transition answer.**
   If a later wrapper ever justifies a narrower peer experiment, it should reopen as a wrapper-specific exception, not as the mature-product hosting ladder.

`[projected:reasoned]` Architecture and product seams this lane wants protected:

- stable room-code/link/QR join grammar across local, private-hosted, and later official-hosted modes
- explicit `host mode` or `operator mode` metadata so users can tell whether they are in official, self-hosted, or sanctioned-community-hosted play
- pack/version compatibility metadata before plural hosts become real
- clear separation between canonical live room state and any later cacheable/static asset distribution experiments
- operator-facing start/stop, health, and recovery surfaces strong enough that a trusted non-Logan operator could plausibly run the stack

`[projected:reasoned]` If later synthesis agrees, the canon consequence is not "community hosting is now the plan." The canon consequence is narrower: `trusted operator-hosted authoritative rooms` should remain a protected transition seam, while `community-host collective` stays conditional and `browser P2P authority` stays explicitly deferred.

## Explicit deferrals

`[governing:cited]` This lane does **not** settle:

- exact pricing, donations platform, membership tiers, or packaging
- whether support buys queue priority, guaranteed access, or scheduled windows
- public room discovery, stranger participation, or broad public moderation posture
- open federation or broad community-host governance
- pack-signing, mirror verification, legal agreements, or formal trust policy for volunteer hosts
- peer-assisted media seeding or any other guest-side cooperative transport experiment
- the exact threshold where official hosted convenience should outrank sanctioned mirrors

`[assumed:reasoned]` Those are kept deferred because they belong either to Lane D, to the later sensitivity pass, or to a separate governance/implementation pass once real operator adoption and host-mode demand are measured.

## What the later sensitivity pass must test from this lane

`[projected:reasoned]` The later sensitivity pass should test whether the carry-forward from Lane E:

- preserves the private-first center instead of quietly normalizing public or semi-public hosting as the default
- preserves the distinction between `official hosted`, `self-hosted`, and `sanctioned community-hosted` modes
- keeps `host`, `authority`, `presenter`, and later moderation/operator roles separable rather than collapsing them into one permanent actor
- keeps support/access logic separate from hosting topology so Lane D does not accidentally turn hosted convenience into paid guaranteed access
- preserves the current explicit distinction between `grassroots authoritative hosting` and `browser P2P authority`
- introduces any new carry-forward seam for `ROADMAP.md` or `REQUIREMENTS.md`, especially around host-mode metadata, compatibility labeling, or operator-runbook strength
- smuggles in new public-obligation assumptions such as stronger uptime, queue guarantees, or moderation promises before the product has chosen them deliberately

`[projected:reasoned]` If the later synthesis wants to patch canon, the strongest candidate text is likely:

- `LONG-ARC.md`: clarify that trusted operator-hosted authoritative rooms are the first grassroots extension of the hosting ladder
- `ROADMAP.md` / `REQUIREMENTS.md`: preserve stable guest join grammar and make room for explicit host-mode labeling if plural trusted operators later become real

## Coverage note

`[assumed:reasoned]` Lane E closes the main doctrinal question well enough for synthesis: the project can keep a credible middle path between `Logan hosts everything forever` and `official hosted service immediately`, and that middle path is operator-layer authoritative hosting, not browser P2P.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Confidence is:

- high on the negative distinction from full browser P2P authority
- medium-high on trusted-circle volunteer operators as the strongest grassroots branch
- medium on sanctioned mirrors / small host collectives as a conditional later branch
- medium on supporter-funded official hosted convenience as the cleaner broader-use branch, because the topology is clearer than the eventual money model

`[open]` This lane does not by itself fully close the mature-product support/community story. It is meant to feed the converged synthesis alongside Lane D and the other substantive lanes, and it still lacks the planned external-reference memo that could either strengthen or weaken the sanctioned-mirror and hosted-convenience comparisons.
