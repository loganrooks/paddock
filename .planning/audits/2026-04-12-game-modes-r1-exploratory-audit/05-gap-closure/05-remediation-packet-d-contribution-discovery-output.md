# 05 Remediation Packet D: Curated Contribution And Discovery Posture

## Packet framing

- Mode: `solution evaluation`
- Question: what contribution and discovery posture is worth preserving between `internal-only authorship` and `open publishing`, given Prix Guesser's private-first, curated, host-led product center?
- Scope:
  - curated partner or invited authors
  - reviewed outside submissions
  - directory-listed but unsupported additions
  - visibility, activation, removal, and endorsement posture
  - how discovery promises change content-supply assumptions
- Non-goals:
  - designing a public creator marketplace
  - closing the full content-return loop
  - setting creator economics, revenue split, or payout mechanics
  - finalizing later public moderation architecture
- Stop condition: enough direct outside-repo evidence to distinguish viable middle-space postures, separate `first-value` from `later-value`, translate burden by archetype, and state explicit deferrals for later converged synthesis.

`[governing:cited]` This packet is an input to later converged synthesis, not a claim that the full `05-gap-closure` bundle is now closure-grade on its own.

## Gap justification

This packet directly addresses:

- `GCO-00`
  - a mature-product answer here has to stay archetype-aware rather than assuming one default player or one default discovery surface
- `GCO-00A`
  - contribution posture is part of the unresolved content-supply model, not a side question
- `GCO-06`
  - the middle ground between pure internal authorship and open platform behavior is still under-described
- `GCO-08C`
  - the packet must separate what matters first from what may matter later
- `GCO-09`
  - direct outside-repo grounding is required here rather than inherited internal synthesis
- `GCO-09A`
  - reference translation must be explicit so polished examples are not imported wholesale
- `GCO-09B`
  - negative-case evidence is necessary because contribution/discovery systems reliably hide burden behind clean creator pages and app-store style surfaces

These IDs belong together because the unresolved issue is not merely `should community contribute?` It is how contribution intake, activation, discovery, endorsement, removal, and support expectation interact without quietly forcing Prix Guesser into an open-platform identity.

This packet is not trying to close:

- a public creator marketplace
- final long-term monetization of creator content
- the full content flywheel or return-loop ranking
- final public moderation doctrine

## What is already settled

`[governing:cited]` Repo-local doctrine that should constrain this packet rather than be reopened:

- Prix Guesser remains private-first, browser-first, curated, and host-led in its current product center (`PROJECT.md`, `LONG-ARC.md`).
- Visibility is staged. `trusted private`, `unlisted/share-by-link`, and `selectively visible showcase or spectator shells` are distinct states, not one generic publicness switch (`PROJECT.md`, `LONG-ARC.md`, `05-gap-closure-lane-c-community-shells-output.md`).
- Open creator publishing, broad public discovery, and marketplace behavior remain explicitly deferred (`LONG-ARC.md`).
- The content flywheel already leans toward `evergreen library + bounded editorial spotlight + calibration`, but the contribution/discovery seam inside that model remains underclosed (`05-gap-closure-lane-a-content-flywheel-output.md`).
- The 05 bundle's problem is mostly `premature ranking`, not total lack of vocabulary; this packet should preserve useful distinctions while reopening over-strong closure (`05-epistemic-opening-output.md`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` Practical implication: this packet does not need to prove that curation-first is allowed. It needs to determine which contribution postures are worth preserving as future seams, which discovery surfaces arrive earliest if any, and which branches should remain explicitly later.

## Path of inquiry

- Entry point:
  - the task spec and `GCO-06` specifically reopened the space between `internal only` and `open platform`
- Branches considered:
  - official-only editorial authorship
  - trusted-private sharing without broad discovery
  - invited or partner authors under editorial activation
  - broader reviewed submissions
  - directory-listed but unsupported additions
  - open marketplace / public publishing as a boundary case
- Branches pursued:
  - curated partner programs and approved creator libraries
  - staged visibility and verified-creator discovery
  - community directories that are searchable but explicitly unsupported
  - large review queues and support-forum models that expose hidden operator burden
- Branches deferred or abandoned:
  - payout mechanics
  - rating/recommendation algorithms
  - tax/compliance details for creator sales
  - a full DMCA/trademark process for Prix Guesser
- Unexpected branches / reframings:
  - `discovery posture` turned out to be as load-bearing as `submission posture`; the moment a contribution is searchable, users infer quality, support, and endorsement
  - `listed but unsupported` emerged as its own branch rather than merely a weaker version of approval
  - official review is often `initial review only`, which materially changes what a listing promise can honestly mean

## Negative-case / failure evidence note

Direct external grounding:

- HACS states that custom elements are community-created, published for others to use `at their own risk`, are not supported by the Home Assistant project, and are not reviewed or tested by the Home Assistant development team. It also notes that new default-repository additions still take `months` to review and include.
- Obsidian says community plugins undergo an initial review, but the team is too small to manually review every new release. Community plugins can access local files, connect to the internet, and install additional programs, and users are told to perform an independent security audit if sensitive data is involved.
- WordPress reports review queue pressure directly: weekly submissions stabilized around `330` in late 2025, `38.7%` of reviewed plugins received no reply from their authors, the team is made up of `100% volunteers`, and more than `500` submissions can be mid-review at once.
- Kahoot states that moderators cannot scan all published content, relies on community flagging, removes flagged public items from Discover search results during review, allows public items to be duplicated, and says verified status does not guarantee the absence of inappropriate material.

Inference for Prix Guesser:

- `reviewed submissions` are not a light toggle. They create queue ownership, follow-up burden, rejection/removal work, and stale-listing risk.
- `listed but unsupported` is real, but it only works when the platform is willing to tolerate user confusion, ongoing compatibility drift, and some spillover support demand.
- `verified creator` badges reduce discovery friction but do not remove moderation or trust burden; they can increase false endorsement expectations if labels are too soft.
- discovery creates a duplication and catalog-noise problem even when the platform sees duplication as acceptable behavior.

## Live alternatives

| Posture | Shape | Main strengths | Main burdens | Packet status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Official-only editorial authorship` | only Prix-authored packs are activatable and discoverable | strongest quality control, lowest review burden, clearest support boundary | weakest outside-supply seam; risks over-closing useful future contribution space | keep as baseline, but not as the only preserved seam |
| `Trusted-private sharing` | pack links/files/imports shared intentionally, not broadly searchable | best first fit for private-first product; low endorsement burden; preserves contribution without public discovery | limited catalog/discovery value; still needs schema and compatibility discipline | strongest first non-internal branch |
| `Invited / partner authors with editorial activation` | outside authors can submit packs, but Prix controls activation and curated discovery | expands supply without open-platform identity; strong fit with partner-program references | requires review rubric, rights provenance, author relationship management, and removal tools | main later middle-space branch worth preserving |
| `Reviewed external submissions` | broader outside intake, manual approval before activation or listing | broader funnel while keeping a gate | queue burden, moderation/removal work, stale submissions, status communication, report handling | later and conditional, not first-value |
| `Directory-listed but unsupported additions` | searchable or installable third-party packs remain separate from official/partner activation | long-tail variety for advanced users or self-hosters; preserves experimentation without full approval | strong false endorsement risk, compatibility/version labeling burden, support spillover, content-quality inconsistency | possible later adjunct only if sharply segmented |
| `Open marketplace / public publishing` | creators self-publish to public discovery and possibly sales | highest scale potential | highest moderation, duplication, ranking, copyright, and creator-economy burden | explicit defer |

## Findings

### 1. The strongest middle-space branch is `editorial activation of outside authors`, not `open submissions with public discovery`

Direct external grounding:

- Minecraft's partner and Realms programs use portfolio gates, curated libraries, publisher or partner pathways, and per-submission review before content is surfaced to players.
- Kahoot requires a verified-creator approval process before creators can sell on the marketplace and reserves the right to review and remove submitted content.

Inference:

- If Prix Guesser preserves a non-internal contribution seam, the most doctrine-compatible version is `invited / partner-like authors whose packs are editorially activated by Prix`, not `anyone can publish and be discovered`.
- This keeps outside contribution alive without turning discovery itself into the operating center.

### 2. Discovery posture and contribution posture cannot be separated

Direct external grounding:

- Kahoot distinguishes `public`, `private`, `unlisted`, and `organization` visibility. Public content appears in search results; unlisted content can be shared by link without appearing in search.
- HACS distinguishes default repositories from custom repositories outside the default set.
- Obsidian distinguishes community plugins in a community directory from core plugins and still keeps Restricted Mode as the default security posture.

Inference:

- Prix Guesser can preserve contribution earlier than it preserves searchable discovery.
- The first safe contribution seam is likely `private or unlisted share/import`, not `browseable community catalog`.
- If discovery appears later, the product must say what kind of discovery it is: `official`, `partner-curated`, or `third-party at your own risk`.

### 3. `Listed but unsupported` is a real pattern, but it only works if the labels are brutally explicit

Direct external grounding:

- HACS explicitly says community-created custom elements are used at the user's own risk and are not supported, reviewed, or tested by the Home Assistant development team.
- Obsidian says it cannot officially endorse specific community plugins even when it points users to popular ones, and it warns that community plugins run third-party code.
- WordPress says the directory is not a marketplace and makes support boundaries, user treatment, and forum conduct explicit.

Inference:

- If Prix Guesser ever exposes third-party packs that are not editorially activated, they cannot share the same surface language as official or partner-curated packs.
- `Listed but unsupported` would need separate visual treatment, compatibility labels, author identity, and a stronger install/use friction layer than official packs.
- This posture is more plausible for advanced/self-host or admin-facing surfaces than for the main guest-facing library.

### 4. Review burden is not just intake burden; it is ongoing status and removal burden

Direct external grounding:

- HACS notes that additions still take months to review and include.
- Obsidian reviews only the initial plugin version and relies on community reporting for later issues.
- WordPress documents manual review, queue-management limits, volunteer capacity, repeated common issues, and one-at-a-time submission rules introduced to reduce confusion and resource drain.
- Kahoot keeps verified creators and public content subject to moderation and can quarantine, privatize, or remove material after the fact.

Inference:

- `reviewed submissions` should not be treated as a nearly-free extension of partner curation.
- Prix Guesser should only preserve this branch as a later conditional seam, after it has:
  - stable pack schema and validation
  - explicit reviewer ownership
  - honest response-time language
  - removal/deprecation controls
  - clear rules for what happens when a once-approved pack later becomes misleading, broken, or low-quality

### 5. Compatibility and naming debt still matter even when contributions are "just content"

Direct external grounding:

- HACS custom repositories still need a known structure.
- Obsidian notes that community plugins do not auto-update for security reasons and that some community plugin behavior is not supported in Publish.
- WordPress highlights recurring compatibility issues, naming conflicts, trademark restrictions, and update collisions when a private plugin later enters the public directory.

Inference:

- Prix Guesser cannot treat external packs as raw files with no lifecycle.
- Any non-internal contribution seam eventually needs:
  - schema versioning
  - required metadata
  - compatibility labeling
  - activation / deactivation control
  - migration or retirement posture
- This is one reason `trusted-private sharing` can arrive before `directory discovery`: sharing tolerates more rough edges than searchable catalog promises do.

### 6. Public discovery reliably creates duplication, moderation, and false-endorsement pressure earlier than Prix Guesser needs

Direct external grounding:

- Kahoot publicly searchable content can be duplicated by others, and flagged public items are removed from Discover while under review.
- Kahoot's own DSA report says verified status helps highlight quality but does not guarantee the absence of inappropriate material.
- WordPress and HACS both need clear policy text to stop users from reading listing as full official backing.

Inference:

- An open marketplace or broad public submission surface is not just a later-volume branch; it is a product-identity change.
- For Prix Guesser's current doctrine, open discovery is strictly later than the contribution seams worth preserving now.

## First-value vs later-value

| Branch | First-value | Later-value | Preserve without prioritizing? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Official curated library` | very high; necessary for trust, quality, and baseline content identity | remains core even later | no, this is active baseline |
| `Trusted-private sharing / import` | high for private recurring groups and friend-circle testing; useful before public discovery exists | moderate later as a low-obligation side channel | yes, but it is also the first non-internal seam worth protecting |
| `Invited / partner authors with editorial activation` | low to medium in the transition phase; not required before the base library proves itself | high later if Prix needs more supply without open-platform posture | yes, and this is the main later seam to preserve |
| `Reviewed external submissions` | low first-value; too much queue burden early | medium later if tooling, review ownership, and catalog governance mature | yes, but explicitly later and conditional |
| `Directory-listed but unsupported additions` | low first-value for the core product; risks user confusion too early | niche later value for advanced operators or self-hosters | yes, but only as a narrow adjunct possibility |
| `Open marketplace / public publishing` | negative first-value relative to burden | only relevant if product posture shifts toward public-service and creator-economy obligations | no active preservation beyond `do not accidentally foreclose if the whole product changes` |

`[assumed:reasoned]` The key separation is that `trusted-private sharing` has stronger first-value than `partner authors`, while `partner authors` may have stronger later-value than `trusted-private sharing` for supply expansion. Those are different judgments and should stay different.

## Archetype-to-burden translation

| Archetype | First-fitting contribution posture | Why it fits | Main burden if expanded too fast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Private recurring group` | `official curated library` plus `trusted-private sharing` | this archetype benefits from friend-circle experimentation more than from public catalog discovery | searchable community catalog adds very little first-value and creates unnecessary support/quality ambiguity |
| `Private online sync` | `official curated library`, then `trusted-private sharing`, then later `partner-activated drops` | remote groups may value fresh packs, but they still rely on a trusted host and predictable quality | unsupported third-party packs increase host troubleshooting burden and can sour room reliability |
| `Solo ritual` | `official curated library`, later `partner-activated discovery` | solo play can eventually benefit more from browseable variety and author followability | public catalog noise, duplication, and weaker quality signals become more visible here than in trusted-group play |
| `Bounded public / event shell` | `official curated` and possibly `partner-activated` only | hosts and audiences infer endorsement more strongly in event settings; pack quality has reputational impact on the event shell itself | `listed but unsupported` content creates the strongest false-endorsement and event-failure risk here |

`[assumed:reasoned]` Archetype summary: the branch that helps most first is not the same across all later wrappers, but none of the core archetypes make `open community marketplace` look like the right early move.

## Trigger conditions

- `Trusted-private sharing / import`
  - pack schema and import validation are stable enough that broken packs fail clearly
  - packs can be marked `private` or `unlisted` without implying searchable discovery
  - host/operator UI can show pack origin and version
- `Invited / partner authors with editorial activation`
  - Prix has a preview/playtest workflow and a quality rubric
  - rights provenance and licensing are recorded
  - pack metadata supports credit, version, compatibility, and retirement
  - activation and removal can happen without deleting historical traces
- `Reviewed external submissions`
  - there is an explicitly owned submission queue
  - response-time honesty is defined
  - rejection, revision, appeal, and removal posture are written down
  - calibration data exists to help decide whether a reviewed pack stays promoted
- `Directory-listed but unsupported additions`
  - official, partner, and community surfaces are visually and verbally separated
  - compatibility/version labels are visible before activation
  - reporting and deprecation paths exist
  - the install path is gated enough that users understand they are leaving the fully curated surface
- `Open marketplace / public publishing`
  - not a near-term trigger list; this would require a deliberate shift in product posture, public moderation, payments/compliance, ranking/search policy, duplication/copyright handling, and stronger service-language

## Recommended carry-forward

`[projected:reasoned]` Recommended contribution/discovery ladder to carry forward into later converged synthesis:

1. Keep `official curated packs` as the main library and baseline trust surface.
2. Preserve `trusted-private sharing / import` as the first non-internal contribution seam worth protecting.
3. Preserve `invited / partner authors with editorial activation` as the main later middle-space branch.
4. Keep `reviewed external submissions` alive only as a later conditional branch, after preview, calibration, removal, and reviewer-capacity tools exist.
5. Keep `directory-listed but unsupported additions` as a narrow advanced/self-host possibility, not as the main discovery answer.
6. Keep `open marketplace / public publishing` explicitly deferred.

`[projected:reasoned]` Discovery carry-forward:

- early discovery should mean `official` and maybe later `partner-curated`
- `unlisted/share-by-link` can arrive earlier than browseable community discovery
- if third-party discovery ever exists, it should be visibly segregated from official packs and should not silently imply full endorsement or support

`[projected:reasoned]` Main verdict:

- The middle space worth preserving is `curated activation`, not `open publishing`.
- First-value lies in `official curation + trusted-private sharing`.
- Later-value lies in `partner-activated outside authors`.
- `Reviewed submissions` and `listed but unsupported` are real later possibilities, but only if Prix deliberately accepts the associated queue, labeling, and removal burden.

## Explicit deferrals

- public creator marketplace or self-service publishing
- creator ranking, recommendation, and marketplace search algorithms
- creator revenue split, payouts, and tax/compliance design
- ratings/reviews for third-party packs
- full public moderation and copyright-dispute process
- whether unsupported third-party packs would live inside the main guest-facing product or only in advanced/self-host surfaces

## What should remain open

- whether later outside-approved packs should appear as `partner brands`, `guest editors`, or be absorbed into a Prix-curated namespace
- whether `reviewed external submissions` should publish as standalone outside packs or be forked into Prix-maintained curated copies
- whether a later solo wrapper needs more browse/discovery surface than room-first wrappers do
- whether `directory-listed but unsupported` should ever exist inside the core product at all, rather than only in self-host/admin channels
- the exact threshold at which a contributor moves from `trusted-private sharer` to `partner-like author`

## What later converged synthesis must reconcile

- with the `content flywheel` packet:
  - how much future supply is expected from internal authorship versus partner-like outside authors
- with the `wrapper shells` packet:
  - which visibility states arrive first, and whether unlisted share surfaces should precede any browseable discovery
- with the `promise ladders` packet:
  - what support and service promises attach to official, partner, reviewed, and unsupported pack surfaces
- with the `host ecology` packet:
  - whether advanced/self-host operators are the only sensible home for unsupported third-party packs
- with the `content return-loop` packet:
  - how calibration and retirement work for non-internal packs, and whether strong outside packs can graduate into featured or evergreen curated status
