# 05 Canon Uplift And Milestone 2 Steering Proposal

## 1. Response framing

This should be executed as a broader canon uplift proposal, not as a narrow patch-first cleanup.

The sensitivity round was useful as a gate, but the stronger read from the full trail is:

- the repo does not have a blocking contradiction problem
- the repo does have a doctrine-expression problem
- the current canon still leaves too much mature-product doctrine preserved only by negative space, repeated caution, or audit-memory

The correct response is therefore:

- strengthen canon expression across `REQUIREMENTS.md`, `LONG-ARC.md`, `PROJECT.md`, and `ROADMAP.md`
- use the sensitivity classifications as input, not as the endpoint
- make Milestone 2 carry-forward pressure more explicit without turning open rankings into chosen winners
- make farther long-arc doctrine less dependent on audit-memory, asymmetry, or silence
- leave `01-CONTEXT.md` out of the main patch unless a final consistency touch is genuinely needed at rerun time

The proposal should be judged against three horizons at once:

- current canon clarity
- reduced Milestone 2 planning friction
- stronger later-horizon doctrine preservation

## 2. What the previous sensitivity verdict got right

The previous sensitivity verdict was right about four important things:

- there is no active doctrine break that forces a stop
- the next pass must not choose winners for wrappers, money family, host identity, contribution identity, or event-memory surface
- `REQUIREMENTS.md` is the clearest patch-first hotspot because example density there can read like ranking
- preserve-only and reversal-sensitive branches are still mostly intact in the live docs

It also correctly protected the main anti-overreach boundaries:

- do not widen Phase 01 execution scope
- do not create a new milestone or roadmap branch
- do not treat sensitivity classifications as winner selection
- do not smuggle paid participation, official-hosted-winner logic, marketplace/public publishing, or event-object closure back into canon

## 3. What it understated

It understated the amount of mature-product doctrine the round actually earned.

The narrow `safe enough to patch lightly` reading misses that the gap-closure trail now supports stronger canon expression on all of the following:

- wrapper-family separation and the still-open first low-burden ordering
- audience-right thresholding inside bounded live audience
- later money-family rivalry and service-promise boundaries
- host-identity branching beyond generic `hosted` language
- official-library trust anchor plus middle-space contribution posture
- reviewed versus curated versus unsupported discovery status
- recap/report versus challenge-result versus true event-memory layering
- mastery, room/group memory, event memory, and content-history separation

It also understated how much future friction is being carried by asymmetry rather than by explicit doctrine. The current canon still over-relies on:

- `challenge` as the most visible later wrapper noun
- `summary/replay` as if it can safely stand in for recap shell and event-memory questions at once
- `hosted` as if capability and owner model can stay collapsed indefinitely
- `curated`, `reviewed`, and `unsupported` without a stable status ladder

That is a real Milestone 2 problem, not just a wording preference. If left alone, the next planning round will keep inheriting false winners from prose density.

## 4. File-by-file uplift goals

| File | Uplift goal | Why now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `REQUIREMENTS.md` | Make future capability examples explicitly non-ranked, preserve stronger seam/status vocabulary, and stop challenge/replay examples from reading like chosen product order. | This is the most concentrated source of quiet hard-coding. |
| `LONG-ARC.md` | Carry the durable doctrine the round actually earned: wrapper families, audience-right thresholds, host identities, money-family separation, contribution/discovery status, and layered recap/event memory logic. | This is the best file for durable doctrine rather than patch-note caution. |
| `PROJECT.md` | Rebalance the compact product story so Milestone 2 pressure is not narrated only through challenge, spectator, or generic persistence language. | This is the shortest canon surface and therefore the easiest place for repeated asymmetry to distort later planning. |
| `ROADMAP.md` | Convert doctrine into carry-forward constraints that protect later choices without widening the Milestone 01 spine. | This is where Milestone 2 distortion becomes operational planning friction. |
| `01-CONTEXT.md` | Keep as rerun input, not as main canon patch target; touch only if post-uplift vocabulary drift would otherwise corrupt the rerun. | This file is pre-rerun steering, not durable canon. |

## 5. Recommended patch order

1. `REQUIREMENTS.md`
   Stabilize non-ranked future-capability reading and seam vocabulary before touching higher-level prose.

2. `LONG-ARC.md`
   Encode the durable doctrine explicitly while the patch still has full semantic room.

3. `PROJECT.md`
   Restate the compact product and Milestone 2 posture after the doctrine layer is clearer.

4. `ROADMAP.md`
   Propagate the clarified doctrine into phase carry-forward notes without changing roadmap shape.

5. `01-CONTEXT.md` only if needed
   Apply a minimal rerun-time consistency touch only if the canon uplift would otherwise leave the Phase 01 input using stale or misleading vocabulary.

6. One short consistency review
   Check status vocabulary, open-question visibility, and cross-file alignment after the patch set lands.

This is intentionally broader than the Round 2B patch pattern, but it still stays disciplined:

- no new roadmap phase
- no milestone reordering
- no execution-scope widening

## 6. Execution-ready patch operations by file

### `REQUIREMENTS.md`

- Add a short framing note near the `v2 Requirements` block that these later capability families are illustrative and non-ranked, not a chosen wrapper or monetization sequence.
- Rebalance the future-wrapper and retention examples so `WRAP-01`, `RET-01`, and `RET-04` cannot be read together as `challenge first, challenge cadence first, replay equals explicit shell`.
- Make the recap/review family, challenge family, and later event-memory family visibly separate in wording.
- Keep `SEAM-04`, `SEAM-05`, and `SEAM-08` doing explicit non-foreclosure work for wrapper reuse, visibility/access separation, and layered histories rather than leaving that work to audit memory.
- Preserve deferral language around stronger public discovery, public trust, and paid guaranteed access without using umbrella terms such as `premium`, `membership`, or `hosted`.
- If any future content/discovery capability wording is touched, require status distinction between official curated activation, approved-but-not-identical status, and segregated unsupported surfaces.

### `LONG-ARC.md`

- Expand the wrapper doctrine so low-burden aftermath, share-by-link/challenge, bounded live audience, and later ambient community remain visibly different families.
- Make explicit that the visibility ladder is an obligation ladder, not a winner ladder for wrapper order.
- Add or strengthen audience-right doctrine so `watch`, light `react/vote/predict`, and `submit/speak` do not read like one flat audience role.
- Clarify that later money families remain distinct: `editorial/programming/content` and `hosted convenience` are rival families; `paid guaranteed participation` stays outside the ordinary ladder.
- Refine hosting doctrine so `trusted operators`, `official hosted convenience`, `provider/partner hosts`, governed plural-host futures, and unsupported private operators are not compressed under generic `hosted`.
- Strengthen contribution/discovery doctrine so the official curated library remains the trust anchor while middle-space editorial activation with visible outside credit stays legible without promoting creator-platform identity.
- Make recap/report, featured challenge result, and true event memory distinct doctrine surfaces; event memory should stay tied to a real event object rather than to themed cadence words.

### `PROJECT.md`

- Widen the wrapper examples so the compact project story includes low-burden aftermath/recap/report possibilities alongside solo, challenge, and spectator/showcase possibilities.
- Update the Milestone 2 framing so it clearly carries four different pressure families:
  - remote/private convenience and durability
  - layered memory and recurrence
  - sustainable content operations
  - preserved later wrapper and host-identity choice
- Keep the first post-private wrapper question visibly open rather than narrating the future mainly through async challenge and spectator examples.
- Make the official curated library trust anchor and staged discovery posture slightly more explicit without turning this file into a detailed contribution policy document.
- Keep `watchability` tied to shared legibility and reveal payoff, not to one permanent truth surface or one audience-right model.

### `ROADMAP.md`

- Add one overview-level sentence that Milestone 01 preserves wrapper-order, audience-right, host-identity, and layered-memory seams without choosing later winners.
- Expand `Open Decisions Still Visible` so it keeps open:
  - first low-burden post-private wrapper order
  - first audience bundle
  - later money-family ranking
  - later host-identity ranking
  - first explicit shell versus first true event-memory shell
- Strengthen Phase 3 carry-forward notes so `operator-run` and `privately hosted` remain capability language, not owner-model selection.
- Keep Phase 3.1 and Phase 5 watchability language compatible with bounded audience shells and topology-sensitive reveal without implying a chosen audience-right bundle.
- Clarify in Phase 4 that `join`, `rejoin`, `seat claim`, and later `audience-only entry` are distinct lifecycle cases.
- Clarify in Phase 5 that `summary/replay` is room ritual adjacent to later recap/report or challenge-result shells, not closure of the first-shell question.
- Clarify in Phase 6 that calibration history, content history, room/group memory, player mastery history, and event memory are not one ledger, and that later outside-pack discovery status remains unchosen.
- Keep Phase 7 durability language about continuity and recovery, not about stronger service promises.

### `01-CONTEXT.md`

- Prefer no direct edit in the main uplift pass.
- If a touch becomes necessary, limit it to `future_awareness` and `Explicit Non-Decisions`.
- Preserve stable content identity for later recap/report, challenge-result, and event-memory consumers without choosing their object model in Phase 1.
- Keep public challenge surfaces, persistent identity, room/group memory, event memory, contributor identity, discovery status, and paid-access doctrine out of Phase 1 scope.

## 7. Milestone 2 carry-forward implications by file

### `REQUIREMENTS.md`

If this file stays as-is, Milestone 2 planning can keep treating `challenge`, `recurring challenge`, and `replay/review` as if they already define the later product taxonomy.

If uplifted well, it will give Milestone 2 a cleaner starting point:

- later requirements remain capability families, not chosen sequence
- recurrence, review, and event-memory questions stay separate
- host/discovery/status vocabulary stops hard-coding hidden winners

### `LONG-ARC.md`

If this file stays as-is, Milestone 2 planning will keep relying on audit memory to remember which later branches are doctrine, bounded-open, preserve-only, or reversal-sensitive.

If uplifted well, it will materially reduce Milestone 2 friction by making these carry-forward rules durable:

- wrapper order is open, but wrapper families are not blurry
- audience rights are staged, not generic
- later money families are rival families, not one `premium` ladder
- host identity is a real doctrine question, not a deployment footnote
- contribution and discovery status are distinct from one another
- recap/report, challenge-result, and event memory remain layered

### `PROJECT.md`

If this file stays as-is, Milestone 2 can keep inheriting a compact but biased story in which challenge and spectator examples are more legible than aftermath, recap/report, host identity, or status vocabulary.

If uplifted well, it will give Milestone 2 a better steering brief:

- the future problem statement stays balanced across wrappers, hosting, content, and memory
- the first post-private wrapper remains visibly open
- remote/private convenience does not silently become official-hosted-winner doctrine
- the compact product identity stops erasing the strongest mature-product distinctions

### `ROADMAP.md`

If this file stays as-is, Milestone 2 planning can keep over-reading existing phase language:

- Phase 3 as proto-hosted-service doctrine
- Phase 5 summary/replay as first-shell closure
- Phase 6 history/calibration as one future retention ledger

If uplifted well, it will protect M2 planning from those distortions:

- phase sequencing stays Milestone 01-only
- carry-forward constraints become visible enough to prevent silent re-litigation
- M2-oriented seams remain preserved without being promoted into Milestone 01 obligations

### `01-CONTEXT.md`

If this file is left untouched but becomes vocabulary-inconsistent with uplifted canon, the next discuss rerun can silently back-solve M2 questions into Phase 1.

If touched only as a rerun-time consistency pass, it will help by:

- keeping Phase 1 narrow
- aligning the rerun input with the stronger canon vocabulary
- preventing stale pre-uplift terms from reintroducing old asymmetries

## 8. Longer-horizon implications

The uplift should not stop at making Milestone 2 easier to plan. It should also reduce the chance that later milestones inherit distorted doctrine.

The main longer-horizon protections are:

- wrapper families stay visibly plural instead of being back-solved into one winner too early
- visibility/discovery, hosting identity, and contribution/discovery status remain distinct enough that later public or semi-public futures are not accidentally pre-killed
- memory remains layered enough that later group, event, and content-history surfaces do not require doctrine reversal
- `LONG-ARC.md` remains the place where farther-horizon doctrine is carried explicitly, instead of depending on audit-memory or stale shorthand in the other canon docs

## 8. What must stay visibly open

### Bounded-open branches

- first low-burden post-private wrapper winner between `showcase aftermath` and `share-by-link / challenge`
- first audience bundle inside bounded live audience
- later money-family winner between `editorial/programming/content` and `hosted convenience`
- later host-identity winner between official hosted convenience and provider/partner-host style convenience
- first explicit shell winner between `hosted-night recap / session report` and `featured challenge result`
- first mastery read and exact cadence labels

### Preserve-only seams

- ambient community branch
- governed plural-host or collective/mirror branch
- partner-brand or creator-profile discovery branch
- reviewed submissions as later intake seam
- segregated unsupported/operator-only listings
- later true event-memory board or event-object memory layer

### Inquiry debt

- permanence, visibility, and navigation depth for each memory layer
- exact contributor namespace and support/deprecation ownership for activated outside packs
- exact host metadata needed if more than one host type becomes visible
- exact threshold where a themed cadence surface becomes a real event object

The patch pass should make these visibly open, not merely unspoken.

## 9. What must become more explicit

### Direct doctrine

- wrapper families are distinct even when their ranking stays open
- audience rights are staged and not one flat `spectator` noun
- support, access, and service obligation are separate
- official curated library is the trust anchor
- host identity is a doctrine question, not only a deployment question
- memory and cadence are layered
- event memory requires a true event object

### Preserve-only status

- `deferred` must not quietly mean `dead`
- collective/plural-host futures remain preserved without promotion
- creator-profile and reviewed/unsupported discovery branches remain preserved without becoming default path

### Reversal-sensitive boundaries

- no marketplace or open public publishing by softened discovery language
- no paid guaranteed participation by softened supporter language
- no official-hosted-winner reading by generic `hosted` language
- no `hybrid official plus private` calm doctrine by reuse of `hybrid`
- no event-object proof by words such as `featured`, `weekend`, or `event`
- no status collapse among `reviewed`, `approved`, `curated`, and `unsupported`

### Loaded-term discipline

The eventual patch should explicitly tighten the meaning of:

- `challenge`
- `showcase`
- `spectator`
- `audience`
- `hosted`
- `operator`
- `support`
- `premium`
- `membership`
- `curated`
- `reviewed`
- `unsupported`
- `event`
- `featured`
- `weekend`

## 10. What the eventual patch pass must not do

- do not add a new milestone or a new roadmap phase
- do not widen Milestone 01 or Phase 01 execution scope
- do not convert illustrative future capabilities into ranked commitments
- do not promote preserve-only seams into active roadmap candidates
- do not flatten bounded-open, preserve-only, reversal-sensitive, and inquiry-debt categories into one generic `later` bucket
- do not use umbrella terms such as `premium`, `membership`, `hosted`, or `community` without unpacking which branch they mean
- do not treat Phase 5 summary/replay as proof that first-shell or event-memory questions are closed
- do not let `challenge` keep absorbing share-by-link reuse, recurring cadence, result shell, and paid-entry logic under one word
- do not erase the difference between official curated activation, middle-space editorial activation with visible outside credit, reviewed intake, and unsupported listings
- do not patch `01-CONTEXT.md` as if it were durable canon or execution-approved steering
- do not rely on silence to preserve futures that now merit explicit status language

## Bottom line

The strongest next move is a real canon uplift pass, not a light interpretive cleanup.

The patch set should:

- harden the doctrine the audit actually earned
- lower Milestone 2 planning friction materially
- keep still-open rankings visibly open
- keep preserve-only and reversal-sensitive futures alive by explicit status rather than by silence

If executed with that posture, the repo will be in a meaningfully better state for both the eventual canon patch pass and the later Phase 01 rerun.
