# 05 Gap Closure Synthesis

## Verdict

The two upstream inputs **materially agree**.

`05-gap-closure-context-and-plan.md` identifies a real remaining mature-product gap: the repo has strong doctrine about what must stay possible, but still lacks a sufficiently positive operating-model answer for content flywheel, recurrence, community/spectator shells, and support/premium posture.

`05-handoff-gap-review.md` does not contradict that judgment. It explains **why** the gap survived: the original work over-centered mode families and under-centered archetypes, shell ordering, and mature-product operating model. The difference between the two inputs is therefore **emphasis, not direction**:

- verifier-side input says what still needs closure
- handoff-review input says how the closure work must be shaped so it does not recreate the original framing failure

The synthesis consequence is straightforward: keep Round 2A and Round 2B doctrine fixed as governing input, then run a narrow substantive closure bundle that answers the remaining mature-product questions in archetype/shell/obligation terms rather than generic feature-bucket terms.

Important epistemic note:

- some earlier repo-local research outside this audit explored cooperative scaling, self-hosting, and peer-assisted or community-hosted patterns
- that material may be useful as background, but it does **not** by itself close the gap in this audit
- if a question was not properly addressed inside this audit's own closure work, it should still be treated as a live gap here

## Confirmed Closure Targets

These targets are robustly confirmed by both upstream inputs and should define the remaining closure bundle.

1. **Preserve settled doctrine as input, not as a topic to reopen.**
   Round 2A / Round 2B outcomes are already the governing frame: private-first center, substrate-plus-wrappers posture, layered memory and cadence, explicit visibility/access distinctions, and non-foreclosure seams should constrain the closure work rather than be re-litigated.

2. **Close the mature-product gap at operating-model depth.**
   The remaining work is not blank ideation. It is a partial-answer gap. The closure bundle must produce a positive view of how the product could sustain content, return, bounded community growth, and support/premium posture without collapsing into public-platform assumptions.

3. **Answer recurrence in layered terms rather than as one generic retention bucket.**
   Both inputs converge on the need to rank or order room folklore, personal mastery, editorial return, event cadence, and other recurrence layers instead of speaking about "retention" as one flat mechanism.

4. **Answer community and spectator questions in shell-ordering terms.**
   The open question is not "should there be community features" in the abstract. It is which bounded shells belong in the mature-product story, in what order, with what audience rights, and without displacing the private-first center.

5. **Answer support / premium / access in obligation terms.**
   The closure bundle must make a positive doctrine-level statement about plausible support or premium posture while preserving the already-settled deferrals around paid guaranteed access, heavy public uptime promises, and open public obligations.

6. **Keep multiple plausible monetization families alive without forcing exact pricing or packaging.**
   The closure bundle should compare a few promising ladders such as optional support, hosted convenience, premium programming/content, and supporter/community-host contribution paths. The point is to avoid architectural or doctrinal foreclosure, not to prematurely choose one commercial packaging scheme.

7. **Treat transition-phase grassroots hosting and contribution as a first-class closure target.**
   The closure bundle should explicitly compare early transition pathways such as:
   - self-host from personal hardware
   - trusted volunteer-operated authoritative hosts
   - sanctioned mirrors or small host collectives
   - supporter-funded hosted convenience
   These are not the same question as full browser peer-to-peer room authority, and they matter because the project may need ways for eager early adopters to reduce operator risk before dedicated infrastructure is justified.

8. **Make deferrals explicit.**
   Both inputs warn against letting doctrine, underanswer, and intentional deferral blur together again. The closure bundle must say what is intentionally postponed so silence is not mistaken for closure.

7. **Run a separate post-closure sensitivity pass before canon patching or a Phase 01 rerun.**
   Both inputs converge on this sequence. Substantive closure must happen first; ripple testing happens after; rerun comes later.

## Possible But Lower-Confidence Targets

These may deserve treatment inside the closure bundle, but they are not as firmly established as the targets above and should not silently expand scope.

- **A first-ranked post-private wrapper recommendation.**
  The canon keeps `solo practice`, `frozen challenge`, and `spectator-facing` shells open. The closure bundle may need to narrow their order if it materially affects Phase 01 steering, but it should not force a false precision if the evidence stays thin.

- **A bounded community-authoring or contribution posture beyond curation-only.**
  Contribution posture clearly matters, but the strongest current doctrine still defers open creator publishing and marketplace behavior. The closure work may describe a cautious middle ground, but that is lower-confidence than the broader shell-ordering question.

- **A sanctioned volunteer-host or community-operated hardware branch.**
  Earlier repo research distinguishes browser peer-to-peer room authority from trusted operators running whole authoritative room servers or sanctioned mirrors. That earlier distinction is useful background, but because this audit did not yet address the transition-phase version rigorously enough, the branch should still be treated as a live closure target rather than as a solved question.

- **A stronger view on streamer-adjacent importance as a growth lever.**
  Watchability and spectator seams are well-supported. The strategic weight of streamer-adjacent growth is not yet equally supported and should be handled as a bounded scenario question, not as a presumed pillar.

- **A specific dominant return loop.**
  The need to distinguish recurrence layers is confirmed. Which layer should dominate first is still a substantive question for the closure bundle, not a settled precondition.

## Explicit Deferrals

The following should stay deferred unless a later closure lane produces strong evidence that one is unavoidable for rerun-readiness.

- precise pricing, packaging, or commercialization mechanics
- paid guaranteed access, public uptime promises, or other stronger service guarantees
- public room discovery, stranger participation, and open creator publishing
- marketplace logic or broad community governance
- exact durable group noun, full wrapper taxonomy, final cadence taxonomy, or exact future capability bundle
- adjacent-mode portfolio prioritization beyond what is required to preserve substrate and wrapper seams
- large-scale player-count or public-scale operating research

What is **not** deferred:

- comparison of plausible monetization/support families
- comparison of plausible grassroots transition paths that reduce solo operator risk
- explicit distinction between `full peer-to-peer room authority` and `trusted or sanctioned operator-hosted authority`

These are not the remaining mature-product gap. They are later product-shaping questions that would cause closure sprawl if pulled in now.

## Recommended Closure-Lane Topology

The correct remaining topology is:

1. **One narrow external-reference memo**
   Purpose: provide targeted comparative grounding where the repo is weakest, especially bounded public/showcase shells, contribution posture, support/access/service-obligation transitions, and transition-phase volunteer-host or sanctioned-mirror models.

2. **Lane A: content flywheel**
   Purpose: close content supply, evergreen-library posture, curation/contribution posture, and what a sustainable authored-content pipeline actually looks like.

3. **Lane B: recurrence and return loops**
   Purpose: close first-return-loop ordering across room folklore, personal mastery, editorial cadence, replay/review surfaces, and other recurrence layers.

4. **Lane C: community, spectator, and bounded public shells**
   Purpose: close group-memory, audience-shell, showcase, clip/recap, and streamer-adjacent posture questions in archetype/shell terms.

5. **Lane D: support, premium, access, and obligation ladder**
   Purpose: close the positive doctrine for optional support, convenience access, premium possibilities, and the boundary where obligation meaningfully changes.

6. **Lane E: grassroots transition hosting**
   Purpose: explicitly compare the early transition path from:
   - `I host it myself`
   to
   - `trusted others can host authoritative rooms`
   to
   - `sanctioned mirrors or small host collectives`
   to
   - `more formal hosted operation`
   without silently assuming that the only alternatives are solo hosting forever or immediate dedicated servers, and without collapsing this into full browser peer-to-peer room authority.

7. **One converged mature-product synthesis**
   Purpose: reconcile the three substantive lanes into one answer that maps cleanly back to the original handoff questions and the verifier-side remaining gap.

8. **One dedicated sensitivity pass**
   Purpose: test ripple on canon, roadmap posture, and Phase 01 steering before any rerun-input canon patch is treated as final.

This topology should also reject two bad alternatives:

- **not one omnibus closure lane**
  That would recreate the earlier underframing and blur content, recurrence, shells, obligation transitions, and grassroots transition pathways together again.

- **not a separate new doctrine lane**
  Round 2A and Round 2B doctrine are already settled. They should constrain all three substantive lanes rather than become a fourth substantive argument.

- **not a merged `support/premium + grassroots hosting` lane**
  Those questions are related, but not identical. One is about what money or access changes mean. The other is about who can carry infrastructure or operator burden during the transition phase and what trust/support implications that creates.

## External Research: Required Vs Optional

### Required

- comparative patterns for **bounded audience / showcase / spectator shells**
- comparative patterns for **support, premium, access, and obligation transitions** in private-first or niche products
- comparative patterns for **trusted volunteer hosting, sanctioned mirrors, community-operated hardware, or other grassroots transition models** where guest join remains simple and canonical authority is preserved
- targeted comparative grounding for **contribution posture** between fully curated and fully open publishing

### Optional

- reference examples for **editorial cadence tied to seasonal or event calendars**
- reference examples for **lightweight return loops** that do not assume public-account or heavy live-service posture

### Not Required In This Bundle

- broad market-sizing or full business-plan research
- massive-room or public-scale systems research
- generalized social-network or moderation-platform research beyond what bounded-shell and obligation questions directly require

The earlier cooperative-scaling / P2P feasibility research remains suggestive background here, not closure proof. The bundle should not reopen full browser peer-to-peer room authority as a blank question unless new evidence demands it, but it should explicitly evaluate whether trusted volunteer-operated authoritative hosts, sanctioned mirrors, or other grassroots transition models belong in the support/contribution ladder.

The right research shape is narrow and comparative. The repo already has enough doctrine to avoid a broad exploratory reset.

## What The Later Sensitivity Pass Must Test

The future sensitivity pass should test whether the substantive closure bundle:

- preserves the private-first center instead of quietly shifting the product toward public-first assumptions
- preserves layered memory and cadence rather than collapsing them into one identity, one results ledger, or one recurrence model
- preserves staged visibility and audience-right distinctions rather than flattening them into one generic "community" or "public" surface
- preserves Round 2B seam doctrine around wrapper separation, room/event/session layering, and obligation thresholds
- distinguishes `peer-to-peer room authority` from `grassroots authoritative hosting` and tests whether the closure work preserves both the current doctrine and the newly clarified transition-phase risk question
- avoids smuggling paid guarantees, public moderation assumptions, or stronger service promises into Milestone 1 or default Milestone 2 posture
- creates any new carry-forward requirement for `PROJECT.md`, `LONG-ARC.md`, `ROADMAP.md`, `REQUIREMENTS.md`, or Phase 01 steering docs
- materially changes which later wrapper should be protected first after trusted private rooms
- leaves deferrals explicit and coherent rather than reintroducing ambiguity between "open," "deferred," and "already answered"

The sensitivity pass is therefore not a formality. It is the test that the substantive closure bundle actually integrates with existing doctrine instead of merely sounding plausible in isolation.

## Dependency Chain To The Phase 01 Discuss Rerun

1. Produce the targeted external-reference memo.
2. Produce the five substantive closure lanes:
   - content flywheel
   - recurrence / return loops
   - community / spectator / bounded public shells
   - support / premium / access / obligation ladder
   - grassroots transition hosting
3. Produce one converged mature-product synthesis from those lanes.
4. Run the dedicated post-closure sensitivity pass.
5. Patch and normalize canon only after the sensitivity pass confirms the closure does not conflict with settled doctrine.
6. Add one explicit closeout note that reconciles the mature-product gap against both the verifier finding and the handoff-underframing diagnosis.
7. Only then run a fresh Phase 01 discuss rerun, followed by fresh Phase 01 planning.

## Bottom Line

The remaining closure topology is not large, but it is real.

What remains is a **five-lane mature-product operating-model closure bundle, fed by one narrow external-reference memo, followed by one synthesis and one sensitivity pass**.

That is enough to close the verifier-side gap while also honoring the handoff-review lesson: the work must stay archetype-aware, shell-aware, transition-aware, and doctrine-constrained, rather than falling back into a mode-family-first or feature-bucket framing.
