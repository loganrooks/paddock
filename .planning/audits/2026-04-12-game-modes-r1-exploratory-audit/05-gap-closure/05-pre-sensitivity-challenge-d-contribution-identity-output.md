# 05 Pre-Sensitivity Challenge D: Guest-Editor Default And Later Contribution/Discovery Identity

## Challenge framing

- Mode: `hypothesis testing`
- Classification: `initial architecture research/planning`
- Question: does the current stronger default around `guest editor / editorial activation inside a Prix-curated library` actually survive direct comparison against creator-forward and tighter-curation rivals, or is it mostly the safest-looking editorial answer?
- Exact residual claims being stress-tested:
  - `05-residual-gap-converged-synthesis-output.md` says contribution/discovery identity has tightened toward `guest editor / editorial activation`, pushes `partner brand / creator profile` into a later heavier preserve-only branch, and places `directory-listed but unsupported` in advanced/admin/self-host surfaces rather than the core guest-facing library.
  - `05-residual-gap-chunk-d-contribution-identity-output.md` recommends the same ladder more explicitly and treats `guest editors / editorial activation` as the preferred later identity branch.
- Bears directly on:
  - `RGR-04`
  - supporting pressure from `RGR-03`
  - `GCO-06`
  - `GCO-08B`
  - `GCO-08C`
- Non-goals:
  - not reopening `open marketplace / public publishing`
  - not finalizing creator economics, payout, or revenue share
  - not choosing final contributor namespace copy
  - not designing the full moderation or review-queue operating model
- Stop condition:
  - enough direct external comparison exists to say whether `guest editor / editorial activation` survives as the best later default, what archetypes or trigger conditions re-rank it, and what may or may not safely enter sensitivity

## Target hypothesis

`[projected:reasoned:internal]` The current hypothesis is:

- later approved outside packs should primarily inhabit an official Prix library as `editorially activated outside contributions`
- `partner brand / creator profile discovery` should stay later and challengeable rather than become the default later identity
- `directory-listed but unsupported` should remain a separate advanced/admin/self-host seam rather than enter the main guest-facing library

`[projected:reasoned:internal]` This packet is not testing whether outside contribution exists at all. It is testing whether the current middle-space answer is actually the most defensible one once direct analogues and counterexamples are compared.

## Why this hypothesis matters

`[governing:cited:internal]` Contribution identity is load-bearing because it changes:

- what users infer about endorsement
- what support responsibility seems promised
- whether discovery teaches `browse Prix's curation` or `browse creators`
- whether later hosting, solo/public-light wrappers, and content-supply assumptions drift toward a different product identity

`[assumed:reasoned:internal]` If the current hypothesis is wrong, a later sensitivity pass could freeze the wrong thing:

- flattening contributor identity into too much Prix ownership
- over-downgrading creator-forward discovery even where it becomes the honest later value
- or underestimating how hard unsupported listings are to keep out of the official trust surface

## Gap justification

`[evidenced:cited:internal]` The residual synthesis and chunk D are useful because they finally separate:

- official curated library
- guest-editor/editorial activation
- partner-brand discovery
- unsupported operator-only listing

`[assumed:reasoned:internal]` The remaining risk is that the current hierarchy may still be over-fitted to the safest editorial posture. That is exactly what this challenge packet has to probe.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal]` There is also semantic drift still hiding inside the favored answer:

- `guest editor` is a label, not yet a proven doctrine
- the functional pattern could survive while the label does not
- contributor visibility may need to be stronger than the phrase `guest editor` implies

## What is already settled and not being relitigated

`[governing:cited:internal]` The following constrain this packet:

- Prix Guesser remains private-first, browser-first, curated, and host-led in its current product center.
- `Open marketplace / public publishing` remains rejected-for-now.
- `Trusted-private sharing / import` remains the first non-internal seam worth protecting.
- Visibility remains staged: trusted private, then unlisted/share-by-link, then narrower selectively visible shells, with broader public discovery only if stronger obligations are accepted deliberately.
- The official library remains the current trust anchor.
- `directly sensitivity-ready doctrine`, `bounded-open anti-hard-coding`, `preserve-only`, `reversal-sensitive`, and `inquiry debt` still need to stay separate.

## Strongest rival framing(s)

### Rival 1: `creator-forward partner brand / creator profile discovery`

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` The rival claim is that once the product's later value comes from repeat author affinity, creator pages, and browsing by author rather than only by Prix curation, the honest later identity is not `guest editor`. It is `creator-forward discovery`, even if the platform still curates or verifies who gets in.

### Rival 2: `tighter Prix-owned curation or delayed activation`

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` The rival claim is that `editorial activation` sounds cleaner than it really is. If Prix cannot sustain editorial review, proofing, rights handling, retirement, and support routing, then the more honest answer is either:

- keep outside work in `trusted-private sharing / import` longer
- or selectively absorb especially strong packs as `Prix-curated copies`

### Rival 3: `operator-only unsupported catalog as the only honest home for non-activated packs`

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` The rival claim is that once a pack is searchable in a platform surface, users infer endorsement no matter what disclaimers say. Under that reading, non-activated packs should never sit near the main library and should only live in sharply segregated operator/self-host surfaces.

## Path of inquiry

- Entry point:
  - `05-pre-sensitivity-challenge-d-contribution-identity-task-spec.md`
  - the residual synthesis and chunk D claims above
- Branches considered:
  - editorially activated contributor inside an official library
  - creator-forward public or public-light discovery
  - stronger Prix-owned curation / adaptation
  - unsupported operator-only catalog
- Branches pursued:
  - official partner/creator programs with public creator identity
  - curated official libraries that still credit outside creators
  - editorial submission programs that preserve contributor voice while keeping a publication-first identity
  - unsupported or third-party catalogs separated from official/default surfaces
  - support-queue and expectation spillover once items are listed officially
- Branches deferred or abandoned:
  - payouts and creator-economy mechanics
  - exact legal terms for a future public catalog
  - exact UI taxonomy for future contributor labels
  - ratings/recommendation systems
- Unexpected branches / reframings:
  - the strongest challenge is not only `creator profile versus guest editor`; it is also `can Prix actually afford a standing editorial activation program?`
  - `unsupported listing` behaves more like placement doctrine than a softer version of creator identity
  - curated libraries can still expose much more contributor identity than `guest editor` may imply

## Negative-case / counterexample note

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` The strongest counterexamples to the current hypothesis are real:

- Kahoot and Fortnite show creator identity can become the main value surface through creator pages, follows, public profile metadata, and public discovery.
- Atlas guidelines show an official curated library can still allow creators to advertise themselves and contributors, include a website and trailer link, and remain more creator-visible than the phrase `guest editor` implies.
- The Atlas agreement also says publication does not guarantee attribution and that creator-user issues are not automatically owned by the platform, which means official curation does not automatically simplify support identity.
- Puzzmo explicitly treats open submissions as special windows rather than an indefinitely open lane, which is a direct warning that editorial activation has real capacity cost.
- WordPress, HACS, and Obsidian all show that searchable third-party listing creates expectation spillover even when the host says `not officially supported` or keeps review narrow.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` This means the current hypothesis can fail in two directions:

- `creator-forward discovery` becomes the honest later shape if repeat author affinity and public-light browse matter enough
- `guest-editor activation` proves too operationally expensive, in which case tighter Prix-owned curation or longer delay beats it

## Evidence that strengthens the current hypothesis

### 1. The best middle-space analogue is still publication-first curation with visible contributor credit

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` Realms and Puzzmo both show that an official library can remain the trust anchor while still surfacing named outside contributors. The platform/publication controls activation, quality, and presentation, but contributor identity is not erased.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` That is closer to Prix's current doctrine than a creator-forward catalog. It supports the underlying functional pattern behind `guest editor / editorial activation`, even if the exact label remains open.

### 2. Creator-forward systems import a visibly different product identity

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` Kahoot creator pages and Fortnite creator pages teach users to navigate by creator identity itself. That is not a small metadata choice. It is a different discovery promise.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` This strengthens the current hypothesis by making the rival branch easier to name accurately: `partner brand / creator profile` is not just a louder label for the same system. It is a different later product identity with different burdens.

### 3. Unsupported listing still fits advanced/operator surfaces much better than the main library

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` HACS and Obsidian both separate community or custom additions from official/default surfaces through explicit opt-in and stronger risk language. WordPress shows that official listing still drags support expectations in even with strong boundary language.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` This is the most stable part of the current hypothesis. `Directory-listed but unsupported` survives the challenge better than the `guest editor` label itself.

### 4. Public creator discovery is not first-value for the current product center

`[governing:cited:internal]` The current center remains private recurring groups and private online sync, not public browsing or creator-follow behavior.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` That makes `creator-forward partner brand / creator profile discovery` a weak first-value answer even if it remains a live later branch.

## Evidence that weakens or complicates it

### 1. `Guest editor` may be the wrong level of visible author identity

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` Atlas guidelines let creators surface contributors, websites, and trailers in an official curated environment. Realms therefore supports more visible creator presence than the phrase `guest editor` may suggest.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` The functional pattern survives, but the favored label may undershoot how visible later approved contributors need to be.

### 2. Editorial activation is not a light operational seam

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` Puzzmo's editorial program is time-boxed, heavily edited, and proof-based. WordPress and Kahoot both show that review does not end once content is listed. Realms agreements still need rights and support-language handling.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` If Prix cannot own review, proofing, rights provenance, metadata, versioning, and retirement, the honest answer may be `delay this seam` or `selective Prix-curated copy`, not a standing guest-editor program.

### 3. Solo ritual and public-light browse could re-rank creator-forward identity upward

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` Kahoot and Fortnite both show that creator pages make more sense when users return by creator affinity, not only by platform curation.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` This weakens any reading that `partner brand / creator profile` is merely a distant preserve-only curiosity. For the wrong later wrapper mix, it becomes a real competitor.

### 4. Official curation does not automatically clean up support ownership

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` The Atlas agreement explicitly leaves some creator-user issues with creators and does not guarantee attribution simply because the content is published in an official environment.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` The current hypothesis therefore only works if Prix explicitly decides who supports, retires, or updates activated outside packs. The identity answer is coupled to support posture more tightly than the current stronger-default framing admits.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Private recurring group` | strongest fit; library trust and low ambiguity matter most | low value; creator browse adds little first-value | plausible fallback if editorial ops stay weak | poor fit for guest-facing play |

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` This is the main correction to the current stronger-default framing: the guest-editor lane survives best for room-first archetypes, but it is not equally strong across all later wrappers.

## Comparative verdict

`[projected:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Hypothesis status: `survives, but only conditionally`.

`[projected:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` The best current reading is:

- `editorial activation inside an official Prix library` still survives as the strongest later default for approved outside packs
- but it survives as a `functional pattern`, not as finalized `guest editor` doctrine
- and it survives mainly for the room-first, private-first, official-library-centered branches of the product

`[projected:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` This challenge packet materially revises the stronger-default framing in three ways:

1. `Partner brand / creator profile` should not be treated as merely a heavy preserve-only curiosity.
   - It is a real later competitor under solo ritual, public-light discovery, creator-follow, or off-Prix browse conditions.
2. `Guest editor` should not enter sensitivity as settled vocabulary.
   - The product rule that survived is `Prix-controlled editorial activation with visible outside contributor credit`.
   - The exact label remains open.
3. `Directory-listed but unsupported` in advanced/admin/self-host surfaces is now stronger than before.
   - That part of the residual synthesis was pressure-tested successfully.

`[projected:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Net result:

- `official library as trust anchor` is close to `directly sensitivity-ready doctrine`
- `editorial activation of outside contributors` is better treated as `bounded-open anti-hard-coding`, not fully settled doctrine
- `partner brand / creator profile` remains `preserve-only`, but as a live later competitor rather than a nearly-dismissed branch
- `directory-listed but unsupported` belongs in `preserve-only` operator surfaces and should stay out of the main guest-facing library

## What would falsify or materially re-rank the hypothesis

- repeated evidence that users choose packs by creator identity more than by Prix curation
- a later wrapper strategy that centers solo ritual, public-light browse, or creator-follow loops
- contributor relationships that require public profiles, social links, or direct creator support routing to feel honest
- inability to sustain editorial review, proofing, rights provenance, versioning, and retirement for activated outside packs
- a content-supply model that depends on many recurring outside authors faster than Prix can editorially absorb
- strong evidence that especially high-value outside packs need long-term Prix maintenance more than visible contributor identity, which would strengthen `Prix-curated copy / delayed activation`

## What remains challengeable

- exact contributor namespace such as `guest editor`, `featured author`, `contributing curator`, or something else
- how visible contributor identity should be inside the official library
- whether some collaborators later graduate from editorial activation to stronger partner identity
- whether solo/public-light wrappers should expose creator-follow or creator-page surfaces even if room-first wrappers do not
- whether reviewed submissions, if ever activated, should feed only editorial activation or also a segregated operator-only catalog
- who owns support, deprecation, and update responsibility for activated outside packs

## Sensitivity implications

`[governing:reasoned:internal]` A later sensitivity pass may now assume:

- the official Prix-curated library remains the main trust anchor
- unsupported listings do not belong in the main guest-facing library
- creator-forward discovery is not a first-value default for the current product center

`[governing:reasoned:internal]` A later sensitivity pass should not assume:

- that `guest editor` is the locked vocabulary
- that `partner brand / creator profile` is basically dead
- that activated outside packs can be treated as if Prix fully owns support and maintenance by default
- that `reviewed submissions` are ready to activate just because editorial activation remains live

`[governing:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Sensitivity should specifically watch for:

- docs that flatten outside contributors into invisible house-brand authorship
- docs that quietly treat creator profiles as already-promoted default discovery
- docs that leak unsupported/operator-only packs into the main library
- docs that describe `reviewed`, `approved`, `featured`, and `curated` as if they were interchangeable

## What should remain out of sensitivity

- final namespace or co-branding doctrine for outside contributors
- creator economics, donations, or creator-follow feature commitments
- a standing reviewed-submission queue or detailed moderation workflow
- open marketplace / public publishing
- exact support-routing contracts between Prix and outside authors
- final UI or policy design for any operator-only unsupported catalog

## What later converged challenge synthesis must reconcile

- with the hosting challenge:
  - official hosted convenience or public-light discovery could raise the value of creator-forward identity
- with content-supply and flywheel work:
  - if outside supply becomes load-bearing, the sustainability of editorial activation must be tested harder
- with money/service challenge work:
  - creator pages often travel with donation, monetization, or creator-benefit expectations
- with wrapper/audience and recurrence work:
  - solo ritual, showcase, and event-like surfaces may make creator identity more salient than private recurring rooms do
- with doctrine classification:
  - `official trust anchor` is closer to stable doctrine
  - `editorial activation` is still challengeable and trigger-dependent
  - `creator-forward profiles` remain preserved, not promoted
  - `unsupported listing` should stay segregated
