# 05 Epistemic Opening Output

## Review framing

`[governing:cited]` This pass is not another provenance rerun. It asks where the bundle moved too quickly from useful reframing into ranked closure, especially after the synthesis explicitly marked several targets as lower-confidence or still research-needing (`05-gap-closure-synthesis.md:75-92`, `161-183`).

`[evidenced/governing:mixed]` The key distinction is between:

- settled doctrine that should stay fixed
- pattern-family openings that outside research does support
- lane-level rankings, sequencing decisions, and carry-forward judgments that the current source base does not yet justify closing

That distinction is exactly where the current bundle blurs. The reference memo is strongest when it identifies obligation gradients and live families, and weakest when downstream lanes treat those families as if they already settle order, rank, or branch priority (`05-gap-closure-reference-patterns-output.md:216-235`). The verification reruns converge on the same structural problem even though they disagree about how much of C and D can proceed after patching: the bundle is not closure-grade end-to-end, and Lane E's branch ordering remains substantively open (`05-gap-closure-verification-r2-a-output.md:50-69`, `110-167`; `05-gap-closure-verification-r2-b-output.md:45-58`, `108-146`).

## Bundle-level opening verdict

`[assumed:reasoned]` The bundle has not primarily failed by choosing the wrong doctrine. It has failed by converting good conceptual separations into stronger rankings than the present inquiry actually closed.

`[evidenced/governing:mixed]` The real gains that should be preserved are:

- `community` is better framed as shells, rights, and memory layers rather than one feature bucket (`05-gap-closure-synthesis.md:52-53`; `05-gap-closure-lane-c-community-shells-output.md:104-116`)
- `support` is better framed as promise burden and obligation threshold rather than `free versus paid` (`05-gap-closure-synthesis.md:55-60`; `05-gap-closure-lane-d-support-premium-output.md:35-39`, `95-105`)
- `grassroots hosting` is better framed as authority, actor burden, and governance rather than `centralized versus decentralized` (`05-gap-closure-synthesis.md:61-67`; `05-gap-closure-lane-e-grassroots-transition-hosting-output.md:119-121`)
- `full browser P2P authority` remains a strong negative result for the main ladder (`05-gap-closure-lane-e-grassroots-transition-hosting-output.md:174-185`; `05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:46-49`, `95-111`)

`[assumed:reasoned]` The premature closure begins immediately after those gains. The bundle often keeps options alive at the label level, then re-closes them at the rank level:

- Lane C turns shell families into an ordered ladder even though the synthesis marked first-ranked wrapper ordering as lower-confidence and the reference memo explicitly left exact shell order open (`05-gap-closure-synthesis.md:79-80`; `05-gap-closure-reference-patterns-output.md:197-199`, `223-225`; `05-gap-closure-lane-c-community-shells-output.md:209-228`)
- Lane D turns a promise-discipline frame into a narrower premium-family preference than the underlying research had actually settled (`05-gap-closure-lane-d-support-premium-output.md:131-153`, `225-247`; `02-funding-access-and-transparency-models.md:58-67`, `166-191`, `213-235`)
- Lane E turns a live comparison space into a planning-facing ladder faster than the reference memo and earlier xhigh research justify (`05-gap-closure-lane-e-grassroots-transition-hosting-output.md:152-172`, `193-228`; `05-gap-closure-reference-patterns-output.md:197-199`, `223-235`; `05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:138-189`)

`[assumed:reasoned]` Bundle-level verdict: the right next move is not to discard the bundle. It is to reopen the places where the bundle mistook `pattern family identified` for `ordering sufficiently closed`.

## Per-lane opening analysis

### Lane C

`[evidenced/governing:mixed]` Lane C productively opened the question by escaping `community yes/no` and reframing it as shell ordering, audience rights, and memory attachment (`05-gap-closure-lane-c-community-shells-output.md:104-126`). That opening should be kept.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Lane C then closes too quickly in three places:

- it treats `selective recap/showcase spillover` as the first bounded-public shell and `hosted bounded live audience` as the first live shell (`05-gap-closure-lane-c-community-shells-output.md:130-167`, `209-228`)
- it treats `ambient community layer` as the main later bucket, which collapses several distinct middle forms into one obligation-heavy catch-all (`05-gap-closure-lane-c-community-shells-output.md:195-216`, `246-254`)
- it turns `audience rights should be separated` into a fairly specific rights ladder without enough counterfactual work on passive async read, challenge/share-by-link, or mixed spectator-participant shells (`05-gap-closure-lane-c-community-shells-output.md:156-167`)

`[evidenced:cited]` The reference memo does not close that order. It supports rights separation and the shell-family concept, but explicitly says it cannot establish which later shell comes first after trusted private rooms (`05-gap-closure-reference-patterns-output.md:218-225`, `230-235`). The synthesis also marked first-ranked wrapper recommendation as a lower-confidence target, not a settled requirement (`05-gap-closure-synthesis.md:77-80`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` The next inquiry should reopen:

- whether the real first post-private wrapper is `showcase aftermath`, `frozen challenge/share-by-link`, `bounded live audience`, or a narrower hybrid
- whether `publicness` in this lane is mostly about synchronous audience, asynchronous read/share, or durable room/event memory
- whether `ambient community layer` is one thing at all, or a bundle of distinct later surfaces that should not be treated as one terminal rung

### Lane D

`[evidenced/governing:mixed]` Lane D productively opened the question by separating `support`, `access`, and `service obligation`, and by centering promise burden rather than generic monetization (`05-gap-closure-lane-d-support-premium-output.md:35-39`, `81-87`, `155-173`). That opening should also be kept.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Lane D then narrows the inquiry too fast by treating `official hosted convenience` and `editorial programming/content` as the two premium families most worth preserving (`05-gap-closure-lane-d-support-premium-output.md:131-153`, `235-247`). That is a reasonable provisional read, but it is stronger than the supporting inquiry shape.

`[evidenced:cited]` The earlier funding research kept more rival models live than Lane D now foregrounds:

- support-only and light symbolic tiers (`02-funding-access-and-transparency-models.md:71-83`, `213-221`)
- invite-only beta plus optional support (`02-funding-access-and-transparency-models.md:64-65`, `223-226`)
- queueing or capacity windows as a later honest-capacity bridge (`02-funding-access-and-transparency-models.md:121-141`, `161-163`)
- trusted-circle community hosting as a distinct adjunct path (`02-funding-access-and-transparency-models.md:108-119`, `232-235`)

`[evidenced:cited]` The reference memo supports the high-level rung distinction between optional support, hosted convenience, and paid guaranteed access, but it deliberately deferred Patreon/Ko-fi-like examples and exact benefits questions as still too abstract for the memo itself (`05-gap-closure-reference-patterns-output.md:72-75`, `143-167`, `223-228`). So Lane D's carry-forward is stronger than the memo it would need to stand on.

`[assumed:reasoned]` The next inquiry should reopen:

- the difference between `earliest ethically coherent support posture` and `later premium family worth preserving`
- whether `invite-only beta plus optional support` is merely transitional detail or actually one of the primary operating families still alive
- whether `financial transparency`, `status communication`, and `support-copy interpretation` belong in the doctrine-level answer rather than being treated as downstream implementation detail

### Lane E

`[evidenced/governing:mixed]` Lane E productively opens the hosting question by moving away from `centralized versus decentralized` and toward `who owns authority`, `who carries burden`, and `how legible that remains to guests` (`05-gap-closure-lane-e-grassroots-transition-hosting-output.md:103-130`). It also closes the anti-P2P default strongly enough to keep that negative result (`05-gap-closure-lane-e-grassroots-transition-hosting-output.md:174-185`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` Lane E re-closes too fast when it turns the live comparison into a planning ladder:

- `trusted-circle volunteer operators` become the primary grassroots extension (`05-gap-closure-lane-e-grassroots-transition-hosting-output.md:152-170`, `203-212`)
- `supporter-funded official hosted convenience` becomes the cleanest broader-use branch (`05-gap-closure-lane-e-grassroots-transition-hosting-output.md:172`, `214-215`, `193-197`)
- `sanctioned mirrors / small host collectives` become a later conditional branch without enough inquiry into narrower or more bounded collective forms (`05-gap-closure-lane-e-grassroots-transition-hosting-output.md:170`, `211-212`, `240-242`)

`[evidenced:cited]` The memo and older research do not close that order. The reference memo explicitly says it cannot settle whether sanctioned mirrors or host collectives are worth the burden before simpler hosted convenience (`05-gap-closure-reference-patterns-output.md:197-199`, `223-235`). The synthesis called sanctioned volunteer-host or community-operated hardware a lower-confidence live target, not a solved sequence point (`05-gap-closure-synthesis.md:85-87`). The xhigh P2P comparison preserved multiple rival models and explicitly asked what bottleneck each branch is solving, what would change the view, and whether future wrappers could justify narrower exceptions (`05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:11-12`, `47-49`, `138-145`, `174-189`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` The next inquiry should reopen:

- which bottleneck each branch is actually solving: operator burden, resilience, social autonomy, continuity of one canonical service, or broader access
- whether `trusted volunteer host`, `sanctioned mirror`, and `official hosted convenience` are truly one ladder or partly rival product identities
- whether the missing comparison is really `branch order`, or `promotion threshold`: when does a private trusted host become a promoted host ecology with materially different obligations?

## Foreclosure surfaces identified

`[assumed:reasoned]` The main foreclosure surfaces are:

- `claims`: pattern-family evidence got translated into carry-forward claims about `what should come first`, `what deserves active preservation`, and `what is the primary extension`, even where the memo only supported gradients and family distinction (`05-gap-closure-reference-patterns-output.md:216-235`; `05-gap-closure-lane-c-community-shells-output.md:222-242`; `05-gap-closure-lane-d-support-premium-output.md:225-247`; `05-gap-closure-lane-e-grassroots-transition-hosting-output.md:201-228`)
- `assumptions`: the bundle implicitly assumes that low-obligation shells will also be the best first shells, that convenience/content are the strongest later money families, and that trusted volunteer operators are the strongest first resilience branch. Those may all be true, but they are still challengeable heuristics, not settled doctrine.
- `rankings`: the synthesis itself flagged wrapper order, contribution posture, sanctioned host branches, and streamer-adjacent strategic weight as lower-confidence (`05-gap-closure-synthesis.md:75-92`), yet the lanes often render them as ranked ladders.
- `binaries`: several bad binaries were escaped, but new ones remain partly hidden, such as `showcase aftermath versus ambient community`, `support versus entitlement`, and `official hosted path versus collective host ecology`. The middle forms inside each pair remain underexplored.
- `sequencing decisions`: branch order is often chosen without explicit trigger conditions. Earlier research was more careful here and repeatedly used `what would change this view` and measurement prompts (`02-funding-access-and-transparency-models.md:286-310`; `05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:174-189`; `02-hosting-transition.md:185-201`).
- `scope boundaries`: the reference memo's deferrals were mostly valid for a narrow memo, but downstream lanes sometimes treat omitted families as if they were adequately challenged rather than simply out of scope. Examples include Patreon/Ko-fi-like support structures, broader creator-platform comparisons, and pure P2P references used only as contrast (`05-gap-closure-reference-patterns-output.md:72-75`, `210-214`).
- `ontology / vocabulary choices`: `shell`, `audience rights`, `premium family`, `official hosted convenience`, and `grassroots ladder` are useful names, but they compress several axes into one term. They should be unpacked into matrices rather than allowed to harden into doctrine on their own.
- `deferrals`: some rankings depend on the very specifics the lanes defer. Lane D defers exact tiers and benefits while still narrowing later premium families; Lane E defers the threshold where official hosted convenience outranks mirrors while already calling hosted convenience the cleaner broader-use branch; Lane C defers exact wrapper taxonomy while effectively choosing the first wrapper (`05-gap-closure-lane-d-support-premium-output.md:261-289`; `05-gap-closure-lane-e-grassroots-transition-hosting-output.md:230-272`; `05-gap-closure-lane-c-community-shells-output.md:244-279`).
- `translation layers`: the April 10 research kept more gray areas visible than the bundle now does. Funding kept queueing, tier framing, transparency fit, and community hosting as live tensions; xhigh hosting kept rival models, actor differences, and wrapper-specific exceptions alive; the hosting-transition lane repeatedly named measurement needs and change conditions (`02-funding-access-and-transparency-models.md:166-209`, `286-310`; `05-xhigh-comparison-cooperative-scaling-and-p2p-feasibility.md:103-189`; `02-hosting-transition.md:142-201`).
- `source-selection patterns`: official product and documentation pages dominate. They are strong for role boundaries and advertised obligations, but weak for failure, burnout, user misread, private-club usage, and practical operator tradeoffs. That bias favors legible ladders and under-samples messy but relevant middle cases.
- `conditional spike opportunities`: the current gaps are still mostly research-shape gaps, not yet proof that reading-based inquiry has hit a hard limit. Spikes may later help, but they are not the first remedy.

## Inquiry opportunities

`[assumed:reasoned]` The most useful next inquiry openings are:

1. `Post-private wrapper sequencing`
   Compare `showcase aftermath`, `share-by-link / challenge`, `bounded live audience`, and other low-obligation public-light shells on distinct axes instead of one ladder.

2. `Audience-right granularity`
   Separate `async read`, `watch live`, `predict/react`, `light co-presence`, and `full participation` rather than treating audience rights as one upward ladder.

3. `Promise grammar and economic ladders`
   Separate `earliest ethical support posture` from `later premium families worth preserving`, and compare support-only, support-plus-beta windows, queue/capacity tools, hosted convenience, and editorial content without assuming one later pair already dominates.

4. `Contribution posture and discovery`
   Reopen the middle space between internal authorship and open publishing: curated partner authors, external premium activation, manually reviewed submissions, directory-listed but unsupported contributions, and what endorsement actually promises.

5. `Transition-host ecology`
   Compare `trusted-circle operator`, `sanctioned mirror / collective`, and `official hosted convenience` not only by burden, but by what product identity each one implies.

6. `Trigger conditions`
   Require explicit answers to `what would move this branch up or down?` Examples: operator adoption, room-start success, moderation burden, queue frequency, content cadence, or streamer-visible demand.

7. `Future wrapper exceptions`
   Keep open whether a future analyst/co-op or asymmetric audience wrapper could justify narrower exceptions without reopening the main ladder wholesale.

## Recommended source classes by inquiry

`[assumed:reasoned]` Recommended source classes, ordered by usefulness:

- `Post-private wrapper sequencing`
  Primary: official product/help docs and host/operator docs for host-screen party games, bounded-spectator tools, challenge/share-link surfaces, and event-wrapper products.
  Secondary: product walkthroughs, maintainer talks, and postmortems about how audience/public-light layers were introduced.
  Caution: official docs are strong on intended roles, weak on what got abandoned or misunderstood.

- `Audience-rights and moderation thresholds`
  Primary: permissions docs, spectator/stage-mode docs, moderation tooling docs, trust-and-safety policies, and public-event host guidance.
  Secondary: incident writeups and moderator/operator retrospectives.
  Caution: community forum anecdotes are useful only after the official control surface is understood.

- `Support, access, and promise surfaces`
  Primary: official creator-platform docs, hosted-service billing/help docs, waiting-room / queue docs, early-access rules, refund/cancellation help docs, and status-page norms.
  Secondary: creator postmortems on tier burnout, entitlement drift, and queue backlash.
  Caution: pricing pages are good evidence of promise surfaces, not proof that the same model is wise here.

- `Contribution posture and curated discovery`
  Primary: marketplace/partner docs, contributor onboarding docs, activation and compatibility docs, listing criteria, endorsement/removal policy docs, and curated directory rules.
  Secondary: maintainer blogs or talks on review burden and partnership breakdowns.
  Caution: open-platform examples should be used as boundary cases, not imported whole.

- `Transition-host ecology`
  Primary: self-host install docs, host-directory or covenant criteria, backup/succession docs, host-labeling docs, hosting partner docs, and operator runbooks.
  Secondary: host-collective community handbooks, operator incident reports, and trust-boundary writeups.
  Caution: ecosystems like Mastodon and Foundry are useful for obligations, but they may over-represent public-service or admin-heavy settings unless paired with more bounded host examples.

- `Failure and adoption reality`
  Primary: official incident writeups, security/trust docs, and operator-facing recovery documentation.
  Secondary: postmortems, talks, issue trackers, and long-form maintainer blogs.
  Caution: this class is lower-authority than official docs, but it is often the only place where hidden failure modes and user misread actually appear.

## Conditional spike candidates

`[assumed:reasoned]` The following are only `possible`, `conditional on research outcome`, and `not yet recommended`:

- `Support-copy comprehension spike`
  Possible if comparative research still leaves it unclear whether users will read `support`, `beta`, `waitlist`, and `hosted convenience` language as entitlement or as honest capacity/status copy.

- `Host-mode labeling spike`
  Possible if research still cannot tell whether `official`, `self-hosted`, and `sanctioned community-hosted` modes can be made legible enough in join flows and room UI.

- `Trusted-operator runbook dry run`
  Possible if research still leaves unresolved whether a trusted non-Logan operator could realistically run the same canonical bundle without turning the project into a de facto support organization.

- `Audience-rights storyboard spike`
  Possible if reading-based inquiry still cannot discriminate between `showcase aftermath first`, `bounded live audience first`, or a narrower hybrid, and the uncertainty is now about user/comprehension shape rather than missing reference families.

## What should remain open

`[open:reasoned]` The next research round should keep these explicitly open:

- the exact first post-private wrapper order
- the exact audience-rights ladder beyond the basic separation from player rights
- whether streamer-adjacent posture is mainly a compatibility/stress-test concern or a materially important later growth branch
- whether the strongest later money path is hosted convenience, editorial programming, support-plus-beta windows, or some still-unsettled mix
- whether sanctioned mirrors / bounded host collectives are worth their burden before simpler official hosted convenience
- the exact curated-contribution workflow, rights model, and discovery posture
- the public-service threshold for payments, queueing, and public-facing reliability promises
- which empirical trigger conditions would move any branch up or down
- whether some future wrapper justifies a narrow topology exception without reopening default doctrine

## Recommended next research topology

`[assumed:reasoned]` The smallest responsible next topology is:

1. Treat the current bundle as `useful doctrine plus over-strong rankings`, not as failed work and not as closure.
2. Reopen only the inquiry surfaces that were over-collapsed:
   - wrapper sequencing and audience-right granularity
   - promise grammar and later money-family ranking
   - host-ecology comparison and promotion thresholds
   - curated contribution and discovery posture
3. Require each next research packet to include:
   - live alternatives, not just preferred answer
   - what would change the view
   - source-class limits
   - trigger conditions
   - what should remain open after the packet
4. Rerun or supplement Lanes C, D, and E only after that comparative reopening, and do not allow planning-facing rank language unless the lane explicitly shows what rival branches were tested and why they remain lower-confidence.
5. Write the converged mature-product synthesis so it distinguishes:
   - settled doctrine
   - preferred but challengeable branch
   - unresolved branch
   - empirical trigger
   - possible spike candidate, if any
6. Run sensitivity only after that synthesis exists.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Important negative guidance:

- do not restart the whole 05 bundle from scratch
- do not broaden into a business-plan or public-scale systems pass
- do not treat the reference memo as if it already settles lane ordering
- do not jump to spikes before the comparative research openings are actually exhausted

`[assumed:reasoned]` Bottom line: the next research round should preserve the bundle's improved conceptual vocabulary while taking away its premature confidence about order.
