# 05 Pre-Sensitivity Challenge B: Money-Family Ordering And Service-Bearing Branches Output

## Research Frame

- Mode: `hypothesis testing`
- Classification: `initial architecture research/planning`
- Question:
  does the current stronger-default framing survive direct comparison against the strongest service-heavy rivals, or is the current `editorial/content > hosted convenience` ordering still too eager before sensitivity?
- Scope:
  - stress-test `editorial/content` versus `hosted convenience`
  - stress-test whether `paid guaranteed participation` really belongs outside the ordinary later-money ladder
  - use direct external comparison, not only local carry-forward
  - preserve archetype differences instead of forcing one universal monetization answer
- Non-goals:
  - exact pricing
  - exact perks
  - a final monetization roadmap
  - final hosting roadmap selection
  - reopening the repo's private-first or anti-guaranteed-access doctrine
- Stop condition:
  enough evidence exists to say whether the current ordering survives, where it weakens, and what sensitivity may and may not treat as robust

## Challenge framing

`[governing:cited:internal]` This packet follows the shared pre-sensitivity rule: treat the current stronger-default framing as a hypothesis, not a conclusion.

`[evidenced:cited:internal]` The residual synthesis currently leans toward:

- `editorial/content` as the stronger general later-value family
- `hosted convenience` as a conditional service-bearing family
- `paid guaranteed participation` as a reversal-sensitive booking/service branch outside the ordinary later-money ladder

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` The strongest challenge is not "content is wrong." It is:

- whether the current framing underweights host-paid service patterns that are common in online room products
- whether `general later-value family` is silently flattening archetype differences
- whether `paid guaranteed participation` is really outside the ladder, or only being kept there by current doctrine

## Target hypothesis

`[evidenced:cited:internal]` Target hypothesis under test:

1. `editorial/content` is still the strongest general later-value family.
2. `hosted convenience` remains live, but only as a conditional service-bearing branch rather than the dominant general answer.
3. `paid guaranteed participation` should remain outside the ordinary later-money ladder because it changes the product into a booking/service relationship.

## Why this hypothesis matters

`[assumed:reasoned:internal]` This matters because the next sensitivity pass will read the current ordering as one of three things:

- doctrine strong enough to protect directly
- a bounded-open branch ranking
- a still-underdescribed comparison that sensitivity should not silently ratify

`[assumed:reasoned:internal]` If the hypothesis is too strong, sensitivity will under-test:

- remote-private pain as a monetization selector
- host-side service identity as a first-class later value family
- the possibility that content cadence assumptions are doing hidden work

`[assumed:reasoned:internal]` If the hypothesis is too weak, the project risks preserving every branch equally and learning nothing from the residual pass.

`[evidenced:cited:internal]` Why these belong together:

- `RGR-03` matters because `hosted convenience` is not just a money family. It is a service identity branch with hosting consequences.
- `RGR-06` matters because exact benefit wording still acts like a branch selector.
- `GCO-08C` matters because the ranking may vary by archetype rather than staying universal.

## What is already settled and not being relitigated

`[governing:cited:internal]` This packet is not reopening:

- the private-first, watchable, browser-first product center from `PROJECT.md` and `LONG-ARC.md`
- the separation between `support`, `access`, and `service obligation`
- the deferral of `paid guaranteed access` as a Milestone 1 or default Milestone 2 posture
- the preservation of both `editorial/content` and `hosted convenience` as live later families
- the classification that exact pricing and exact perk design are not ready to close

`[evidenced:cited:internal]` This packet is also not relitigating that `paid guaranteed participation / priority access` already sits near a threshold where fairness, refunds, and service expectations change materially.

## Strongest rival framing(s)

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Strongest rival framing A:

- `hosted convenience` may actually be the stronger general later-value family for the most plausible remote-private future, because one paying host unlocking convenience for the whole room is a recurrent pattern in online play products.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Strongest rival framing B:

- `editorial/content` may only look like the stronger general family because it assumes a later authored-drop/content-supply model that the project still has not fully closed.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Strongest rival framing C:

- `paid guaranteed participation` may look outside the ladder only because the project is private-first today; if later event shells become real, ticket/seat purchase could re-enter as an intelligible branch rather than a total category error.

## Assumptions surfaced

- `[assumed:reasoned:internal]` `editorial/content` depends on a real later content-supply model.
  If the project cannot sustain enough authored drops, packs, or programming, this branch weakens quickly.

- `[assumed:reasoned:internal]` `hosted convenience` depends on remote-private friction becoming more painful than content scarcity.
  If remote coordination remains secondary, this branch can stay conditional.

- `[assumed:reasoned:internal]` one-host-pays / many-players-join is socially acceptable for this product family.
  Several comparators assume that pattern; if Prix Guesser rejects it, some service-heavy analogies weaken.

- `[assumed:reasoned:internal]` eventized paid participation would require a later shell change, not just a perk wording change.
  If that assumption is false, `paid guaranteed participation` could creep back in through softer language.

  - exact storefront or processor choice
  - broad public marketplace monetization
  - detailed economics/TAM estimation

- Unexpected branches / reframings:
  - the strongest challenge is not "service exists"; it is "which actor pays for what"
  - the key distinction became:
    - host-paid convenience for the table
    - authored content bought by a host or library owner
    - participant-paid booked seats

| Reference | What is analogous | What is not analogous | Borrowed as concern vs solution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jackbox | host-led party play, one buyer, browser/phone join, authored packs, audience play-along | no self-host branch, less persistence, less remote-service burden | borrow mainly as `solution`: authored content can be the sellable unit without charging participants recurring fees |
| Foundry | self-hostable core, separate content marketplace, optional hosted partners | heavier GM/tooling ecosystem, broader creator economy | borrow both: `solution` for clean family split; `concern` that hosting convenience can become a real later business line |
| Roll20 | creator-paid subscription confers features to everyone in the game | public account platform, broad system library, different discovery dynamics | borrow mainly as `solution` for a host-side paid convenience model; borrow as `concern` because service features create expectations fast |
| Board Game Arena | one premium organizer can unlock table creation / same-location play for the group | licensed board-game platform with public lobby norms | borrow as `counterexample`: service-bearing host-side premium can be a stable central model |
| Nabu Casa | paid official convenience layered over a non-cloud-first base; recurring fee funds service and development | home automation lacks group fairness and party-room social dynamics | borrow mainly as `concern`: official convenience creates account, connection, and support obligations |
| StartPlaying | participant-paid seats, approval flows, cancellation, refund verification | stranger-facing marketplace, professional GM context | borrow mainly as `concern`: once money buys participation, the product has entered booking/service territory |

## Negative-case / counterexample note

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` Negative-case evidence against service-light readings:

- Roll20 and BGA both normalize a pattern where one paying organizer unlocks value for the whole table.
- Nabu Casa shows that official convenience does not stay lightweight; it carries connection, account, backup, and support obligations.
- StartPlaying shows that paid participation directly triggers booking semantics, cancellation windows, and refund workflows.

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]` Counterexample evidence against overconfident service-first readings:

- Jackbox operates a successful host-led party model with discrete content purchases and no recurring required subscription.
- Foundry keeps its core software on a one-time license and monetizes premium content separately from hosted convenience.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Practical implication:

- the service-bearing rivals are real and strong enough to challenge universal content-first language
- but the challenge does not collapse the content branch
- instead it narrows where the current hypothesis is actually strongest

## Evidence that strengthens the current hypothesis

### Direct evidence

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]`

- Jackbox says other players do not need to buy anything to join, that purchased games do not require recurring charges, and that party packs are discrete bundles of authored games.
- Foundry sells one core license with no premium software tiers, then separately offers premium content through its marketplace, external keys, or creator Patreon links.
- Foundry's premium content can be installed without changing who hosts the game server or how players join it.
- StartPlaying's seat/session model explicitly uses booking, cancellation, and refund semantics rather than support or content language.

### Inference and interpretation

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]`

- The comparators closest to Prix Guesser's current center as a watchable, host-led party product point toward authored-content value being easier to sell without changing fairness doctrine.
- `editorial/content` remains the cleaner cross-archetype later family because it monetizes authored substance, not room rights.
- The challenge strengthens the residual claim that `paid guaranteed participation` is outside the ordinary later ladder. The direct booking comparator makes that boundary sharper, not weaker.

## Evidence that weakens or complicates it

### Direct evidence

`[evidenced:cited:external-direct]`

- Roll20 subscriptions explicitly unlock creator/host-facing features that confer value to the whole game, including table features, higher limits, and shared content access.
- BGA Premium lets one member create premium tables for everyone else and unlocks Hotseat / same-location rights.
- Nabu Casa's official cloud product sells remote access, backup, and convenience directly, with recurring pricing and a dependence on connected service state.
- Foundry's hosting partners market always-on availability, easier setup, backups, regional servers, and flat-rate managed hosting on top of the software license.

### Inference and interpretation

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]`

- The service-heavy rival is real: host-paid convenience is a common monetization family for online group products where one organizer bears coordination burden for many participants.
- That means the residual synthesis currently overstates its case if `editorial/content` is read as the universal later winner instead of the doctrine-friendlier default.
- The strongest complication is archetype-specific: for `private online sync` or recurring remote groups, `hosted convenience` could plausibly outrank content as the first money family users will actually pay for.
- The current hypothesis is therefore vulnerable where remote friction, uptime, operator absence, backups, or always-on persistence become more salient than new authored drops.

## Comparative verdict

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` Verdict:

- The hypothesis **survives, but only in narrowed form**.
- `editorial/content` still looks like the stronger **cross-archetype default** later family for the current doctrine because it aligns with authored-substrate value and avoids selling room fairness.
- `hosted convenience` is stronger than the residual synthesis currently implies. It is not merely a conditional footnote; it is a robust rival branch for remote-recurring private play.
- `paid guaranteed participation` remains best treated as **outside** the ordinary later-money ladder. The direct booking comparator sharpened that separation rather than collapsing it.

`[assumed:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` The strongest corrected reading is:

- `editorial/content` is the stronger general later family only if `general` means "most doctrine-compatible across multiple still-open wrappers"
- `hosted convenience` may be the stronger first monetizable family for specific remote/private archetypes
- `paid guaranteed participation` should remain reversal-sensitive and outside the normal money-family comparison

## What would falsify or materially re-rank the hypothesis

`[open:reasoned:internal+external-direct]` The current narrowed verdict would weaken or flip if:

- remote recurring private rooms become the dominant real use pattern, and users show more willingness to pay for reliability and start/join convenience than for new content
- the project's content-supply model stays too thin to support meaningful editorial drops, premium packs, or programming
- official hosting/support obligations become unavoidable before a credible content library expansion exists
- later event shells become real enough that payment for seats/participation is no longer a category error but a chosen product identity

## What remains challengeable

`[open:reasoned:internal]` Still challengeable after this packet:

- the exact archetype breakpoint where `hosted convenience` outranks `editorial/content`
- whether a hybrid `official hosted convenience + private unsupported operators` shape becomes the first serious service lane
- whether `editorial/content` means evergreen pack expansion, scheduled drops, event programming, guest-editor activation, or some narrower subset
- whether any bounded later event shell could justify ticket-like participation without importing full marketplace/service doctrine

## Sensitivity implications

`[assumed:reasoned:internal]` Sensitivity should now treat:

- `editorial/content` as a stress-tested but still challengeable default, not a closed winner
- `hosted convenience` as a serious rival branch whenever remote/private recurring use is in view
- `paid guaranteed participation` as a reversal-sensitive branch that must not be smuggled back in through softer terms like `premium access`, `priority`, `reserved slots`, or `member-only game nights`

`[assumed:reasoned:internal]` Sensitivity should explicitly test for wording that quietly chooses:

- one-host-pays-for-table convenience
- service expectations around uptime, backups, easier room start, or account-linked access
- content-program expectations around drops, packs, or recurring authored cadence

## What should remain out of sensitivity

`[governing:cited:internal]` Sensitivity should not try to close:

- exact prices
- exact supporter benefit matrix
- refund/cancellation policy design
- concrete subscription cadence
- a final hosted-service roadmap
- any near-term re-entry for paid guaranteed participation

## What later converged challenge synthesis must reconcile

`[assumed:reasoned:internal]` Later challenge synthesis must reconcile:

- this packet's archetype-conditioned money-family verdict with challenge C's service-identity / hosting-branch verdict
- the content-supply uncertainty from `GCO-00A` with any claim that `editorial/content` is the later general answer
- the difference between:
  - host-paid convenience
  - host-owned authored content
  - participant-paid booked access
- couch/local recurring play, private online sync, and bounded event shells, rather than speaking as if one mature-product money story covers all of them

## Dependencies and relations

| Item | Depends on | Constrains or affects | Vulnerability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `editorial/content` as stronger cross-archetype default | real authored-content supply, host-led party center, one-buyer acceptability | pack/program design, contribution identity, content operations | medium-high |
| `hosted convenience` as strong rival branch | remote friction becoming salient, willingness to run official service, support tooling | hosting roadmap, account model, support posture, challenge C | medium |
| `paid guaranteed participation` outside the ladder | fairness doctrine, private-first posture, rejection of booking semantics | queue language, event-shell design, refund/service expectations | low |

## Scope expansions and deferrals

- Follow-and-mark:
  comparing party-pack content models against host-side subscription/service models was necessary because the target hypothesis is specifically about family ordering, not just existence.

- Defer:
  exact economics, payment processors, and legal/commercial hardening remain outside this packet.

- Revisit later:
  if a real bounded event shell becomes live doctrine later, re-test whether `paid guaranteed participation` stays outside the ladder or becomes a separately chosen product identity.
