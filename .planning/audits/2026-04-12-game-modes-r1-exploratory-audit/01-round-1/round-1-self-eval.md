# Round 1 Self-Eval

## What Round 1 did well

### It resisted premature flattening

Round 1 did not collapse the portfolio into:

- one ideal fan
- one ideal context
- one ideal seriousness level
- one universal online/community ladder

That was the biggest risk going in, and the lane outputs consistently resisted it.

### It separated “funny premise” from “game with legs”

This was especially important in Lane D and Lane C, but it mattered across the whole corpus.

Round 1 was strongest when it asked:

- where is the loop?
- what creates the return?
- what is still only flavor, wrapper, or scenario?

### It surfaced session-role thinking

This was not fully solved, but it emerged repeatedly and looks load-bearing:

- headliner vs spike
- private recurring mode vs public-facing mode
- remote specialist vs couch default
- appointment reveal vs anytime async

That feels like a real advance over the original grid.

### It found useful category-exceedance signals

The most useful exceedance signals were:

- `private folklore generation`
- `async preparation + sync reveal`
- `appointment / eventized reveal`
- `discussion-first remote play`
- `predator-hunter social chaos`

These seem worth carrying into later rounds as real categories, not footnotes.

## What Round 1 may still be getting wrong

### It may still be overweighting current documentation maturity

Even though the audit tried not to equate user-origin or doc depth with quality, the better-developed entries still naturally got clearer judgments.

Possible distortion:

- `Words of Wisdom`, `Paddock Fashion`, and the predator-hunter branch may partly benefit from stronger writeup maturity
- `Grid Walk`, `Team Principal's Desk`, `Pit Stop`, and some sound branches may still be under-read because their current docs are thinner

This is not a reason to flatten the current judgments. It is a reason to remember that some “weaker” results may really mean “less developed on the page.”

### It did not yet solve portfolio composition

Round 1 surfaced `session role` as important, but did not fully answer:

- what a single great night of play looks like
- what should be front door versus hidden discovery
- what should be recurring weekly return versus occasional novelty spike

That remains open.

### It did not deeply pressure-test tonal risk

The lane outputs surfaced tonal edges:

- humiliation / mockery risk in radio content
- emotional temperature in judgment modes
- embarrassment tolerance in expression modes
- naming / joke-surface risk in predator-hunter branches

But Round 1 did not yet fully integrate those into the top-line comparative judgments.

### It still under-models host performance

Several lane outputs hinted that host energy, commentary, timing, and presentation cadence matter a lot.

Round 1 noticed this, but did not yet build a strong vocabulary for:

- host-performed spectacle
- room energy management
- reveal cadence as a design variable

That could matter a lot in later rounds.

### It blurred two different architecture questions

The later architecture follow-up exposed a scope problem:

- `general architectural exposure`
- `large-scale player-count / massive-room research`

Those are related, but they are not the same audit.

The first asks:

- which promising game shapes pressure early substrate decisions?
- what early assumptions might silently foreclose future modes?

The second asks:

- what would it take to support much larger rooms or `50+` players?
- what reference designs or case studies matter for that?
- what scaling/topology implications would those modes introduce?

Future rounds should not bundle those together into one muddy lane unless the user explicitly wants a combined pass.

### It sometimes overfixed one realization and one ontology too early

This showed up most clearly in parts of Lane E.

Failure mode:

- one plausible realization got named and then treated as if it were the game
- a reusable taxonomy of `family`, `mode`, `variant`, and related terms was applied too broadly
- some net-new ideas drifted toward talk-heavy/off-screen performance because couch-social readability was overweighted relative to on-screen mechanic shape

The correction is:

- treat promising ideas as design terrain, not just the current named pitch
- compare multiple candidate realizations where context, online sync, streamer value, or community potential might change the best form
- derive ontology from the game itself rather than forcing one portfolio-wide schema onto every idea

This matters especially for:

- `Pit Wall`
- `Garage Panic`
- any future driver-themed ideation
- any branch where “family vs game vs mode” starts blurring into mush

### It may still under-read meme-heavy cultural specificity

Another failure mode became clearer in the driver-themed follow-up:

- some ideas were being "improved" by abstraction in ways that quietly cut away the exact F1-cultural charge that made them vivid in the first place

This matters especially for games like:

- `The Verstappen Game`
- `Talibantonelli / Russell`-style crash-chaos

For these, the right task is not always:

- extract the clean mechanic from the meme

Sometimes the right task is:

- preserve the cultural voltage
- while giving it enough mechanical spine, replay logic, and return structure to survive repeat play

Round 2 should pressure-test that tension directly rather than assuming abstraction is automatically refinement.

## What Round 1 should not pretend to have solved

- exact launch sequencing
- final naming / tonal packaging of sensitive joke branches
- public/community roadmap
- feasibility thresholds
- how adjacent modes fit around the geography anchor in a fully modeled product plan

Those are later synthesis and planning questions, not failures of this round.

## What I would pressure-test next

### 1. Family boundaries

Most important:

- split the sound family
- split the action lane mentally into `Pit Stop` vs predator-hunter
- keep `Grid Walk` distinct from verdict theater

### 2. Session composition

Test concrete night structures:

- which modes feel like openers?
- which feel like late-night unlocks?
- which want recurrent private-room history?
- which want race-weekend/event cadence?

### 3. Tone and social-risk thresholds

Pressure-test:

- how mean is too mean?
- how much embarrassment is too much embarrassment?
- how private do some joke branches need to stay?

### 4. Under-shaped but promising branches

Most worth another round:

- `commentary / crowd recognition`
- `Team Principal's Desk`
- `The Grid Walk`
- `Meme Prompts`
- `Pit Stop Co-op`

### 5. Native ontology and design-terrain reads

For the most promising games, the next round should ask:

- what is the core fantasy?
- what is the design terrain around it?
- what competing realizations should be compared?
- what is this game's own ontology, if any, rather than the audit's imposed schema?

This should be treated as a methodological correction, not a side note.

### 6. Cultural charge vs mechanical spine

For the strongest meme-heavy ideas, the next round should ask:

- what part of the cultural specificity is actually load-bearing?
- what gets lost if the idea is cleaned up too aggressively?
- what replay structures can sit underneath without flattening the joke-source into generic mechanics?
- where does the best realization preserve both meme voltage and legitimate game structure?

## What would count as progress in Round 2

Round 2 should not merely restate Round 1 more confidently.

Real progress would mean:

- sharper distinctions between family branches
- better session-role hypotheses
- clearer creator agreement/disagreement on the strongest judgments
- a better read on tone, privacy, and public-surface boundaries
- more confident separation between “keep alive” and “best as editorial lens / sub-branch / later research”
- less ontology mush, especially around the difference between game, mode, realization, and broader design territory
- stronger comparison of alternative realizations instead of prematurely locking one named form
- better handling of games whose success depends on holding both cultural specificity and underlying loop quality
