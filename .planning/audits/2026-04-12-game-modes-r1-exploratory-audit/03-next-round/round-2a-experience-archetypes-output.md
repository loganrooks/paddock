# Round 2A Experience Archetypes Output

## Position in the sequence

`[governing:cited]` This artifact is the central synthesis requested by `03-next-round/round-2a-experience-archetypes-task-spec.md`. It is meant to answer `RESP-03` strongly, advance `RESP-01` and `RESP-02` in a repaired and better-calibrated form, and leave `RESP-04` open for the later foreclosure synthesis rather than smuggling it in early.

`[governing:cited]` The primary steering sources for this synthesis are the governance stack and the four Round 2A lane outputs, not the older `01-round-1/round-1-self-eval.md` or `03-next-round/round-2-prompts.md`, which remain background-only historical artifacts under the Round 2A rules.

## Calibration carry-forward

`[governing:cited]` The main creator-corrected commitments that now govern later work are:

- treat ideas as design terrain rather than prematurely fixed pitches
- do not force one ontology across the whole portfolio
- allow a game's own ontology to emerge from the game
- preserve the tension between cultural charge and mechanical spine where that tension is load-bearing
- treat examples as probes rather than mandatory branches
- allow absurd but still F1-rooted realizations
- separate general architecture exposure from large-room feasibility research
- make traceability, justification, and epistemic reliability concrete without pretending every meaningful judgment can be directly cited

`[assumed:reasoned]` Round 2A also sharpened a further calibration point: the product should not be framed as if there is one default social form and several degraded fallback contexts. Local recurring party, private online sync, solo async ritual, and bounded public/event shells each create different goods. Later synthesis should compare them rather than quietly choosing one and treating the others as support modes.

## Path of inquiry

`[evidenced:cited]` The four-lane split worked as intended. It prevented local party, remote sync, solo async, and community/public logic from collapsing back together into one vague platform story.

`[assumed:reasoned]` The useful comparison axis that emerged is not "offline vs online." It is:

- shared-stage spectacle
- mixed-visibility trust play
- quiet private ritual
- bounded audience shell
- topology-sensitive hybrids that sit between co-presence and separation

`[assumed:reasoned]` That is already a better experience map than the project had before Round 2A, because it names the lived goods the platform might want to preserve before architecture is narrowed.

## Experience archetype map

### 1. Local recurring party night

`[evidenced:cited]` Lane A makes the strongest case that the local anchor is not a quiet quiz but a compact party show built around short private commitment, host-shaped reveal, public reaction, and laughter-buffered failure.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Core good:
- shared-stage spectacle
- visible comparison and argument
- recurring house memory
- mixed-skill play made legible through reveal cadence and social texture

`[assumed:reasoned]` Strongest topology:
- host screen plus phones
- occasional shared-screen or hot-seat variants
- optional same-house hidden-info variants

### 2. Private online synchronous group

`[evidenced:cited]` Lane B makes the strongest case that remote sync should be treated as its own medium rather than lesser couch play. Voice, secrecy, confidence, selective disclosure, and uneven presence are the core texture.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Core good:
- trusted-room voice play
- role asymmetry
- suspicion and confidence signaling
- mixed visibility with strong reveal staging

`[assumed:reasoned]` Strongest topology:
- private room plus per-player screens
- voice-first interaction
- topology-sensitive same-house-separated forms as siblings rather than curiosities

### 3. Solo async ritual / race-weekend return loop

`[evidenced:cited]` Lane C makes the strongest case that solo async is not "live play, but alone." Its core good is editorial return, private mastery, calendar-shaped ritual, and evergreen deepening.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Core good:
- private ritual
- quick return loops
- race-weekend mood shifts
- evergreen collection and recognition depth

`[assumed:reasoned]` Strongest topology:
- personal progression and history
- authored drops and evergreen libraries
- light traces of friend presence without constant public pressure

### 4. Community / public / streamer-adjacent event shell

`[evidenced:cited]` Lane D makes the strongest case that the product's public/community future is most coherent as a bounded shell around private-first play, not as a permanently public social core.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Core good:
- appointment energy
- spectator-readable reveal arcs
- hosted or editorially framed events
- folklore, rivalries, and event memory

`[assumed:reasoned]` Strongest topology:
- distinct player, host, and audience surfaces
- event-bounded participation
- selective visibility rather than universal transparency

### 5. Hybrid and unconventional local forms

`[evidenced/governing:mixed]` Round 2A required this class explicitly, and the lane split plus routing rule revealed that it is not a niche footnote. Same-house partial separation, shared-stage plus handsets, and private-device play are not merely implementation quirks. They are part of the actual experience map because they let the product combine co-presence with secrecy, shame-buffering, sabotage, or role asymmetry.

`[assumed:reasoned]` These forms do not deserve a single universal bucket of their own. Some belong closer to Lane A when shared-stage energy is doing the real work; others belong closer to Lane B when topology and private information are doing the real work. The important carry-forward is not a rigid category but the insistence that architecture should not erase them by assuming only one-room-one-surface local play.

## Per-archetype pressures and opportunities

### Local recurring party

`[assumed:reasoned]` Opportunities:
- strong reveal machines
- hostable session composition
- embarrassment transformed into laughter, bluff, or argument
- recurring house folklore

`[assumed:reasoned]` Pressures:
- room energy dies if devices dominate too much
- repeated public failure can become punishing without shame-buffering
- some action ideas work best as spikes rather than whole-night backbones

### Private online sync

`[assumed:reasoned]` Opportunities:
- remote-specialist modes
- strong asymmetry and private-state play
- role-framed cooperation or judgment
- recurring friend-group ritual without public exposure

`[assumed:reasoned]` Pressures:
- scheduling burden is part of the product cost
- hidden-state complexity can turn into admin drag
- voice-first play changes what counts as legible and fun

### Solo async

`[assumed:reasoned]` Opportunities:
- anchor geography/circuit mastery
- commentary or crowd-recognition ritual
- verdict or prediction drops around live F1 incidents
- editorial calendar plus evergreen collections

`[assumed:reasoned]` Pressures:
- grind risk if repetition loses emotional texture
- content cadence can become operationally heavy
- many expressive and action ideas should resist forced async parity

### Community / public event shell

`[assumed:reasoned]` Opportunities:
- race-weekend events
- hosted reveal nights
- streamer-readable shells
- folklore, clips, and recurring rivalries

`[assumed:reasoned]` Pressures:
- publicness can distort private-first tone
- audience participation can add noise more easily than depth
- host/editorial authority becomes materially more important

## How existing game territories read differently under each archetype

### `Words of Wisdom`

`[assumed:reasoned]` Strongest as local party and bounded event shell. Still viable remotely where private composition plus staged reveal matter. Weaker as pure solo async except in narrow editorial derivatives.

### `The Stewards' Room`

`[assumed:reasoned]` Strengthens across more archetypes than most. Local party gives it argument theater, private online gives it voice-led judgment and secret commitment, and event shells give it spectator readability. Solo async derivatives exist, but the core pleasure is still social judgment.

### Anchor geography / circuit recognition

`[assumed:reasoned]` The strongest all-weather territory. It anchors solo async most naturally, still supports local party as a shared reveal machine, and can produce remote or event-shell derivatives without losing its identity.

### `The Grid Walk`

`[assumed:reasoned]` Upgraded as a private-online specialist. It reads less like a universal flagship and more like one of the clearest remote sync territories.

### `Team Principal's Desk`

`[assumed:reasoned]` Still underdefined as a single named game, but it reads better when pulled toward role asymmetry and private-online trust/confidence dynamics than when judged as a flat local symmetric mode.

### `Paddock Fashion`

`[assumed:reasoned]` Stronger as local party plus curated public spillover than as a truly universal mode. Private-first creation and house-memory seem more central than permanent public exposure.

### Commentary / crowd recognition

`[assumed:reasoned]` Strong fit for solo async ritual and event-shell readability. Also useful as local or public interstitial content because the reveal is fast and legible.

### Predator-hunter and action-chaos branches

`[assumed:reasoned]` Best read as spikes, unlock-driven action branches, or separate action-heavy experiments rather than universal context leaders. They benefit from local energy and may gain from event shells, but they should not distort the experience map into assuming real-time action is the mature product center.

## Community, privacy, recurrence, and event cadence

`[assumed:reasoned]` The strongest cross-lane convergence is this:

- privacy is not the opposite of community
- recurrence begins privately before it becomes public
- event cadence matters, but not every archetype wants the same cadence

`[assumed:reasoned]` More concretely:

- Lane A says recurrence first appears as house memory, in-jokes, recurring hosts, and rematches
- Lane B says recurrence looks like trusted-room ritual, recurring Discord groups, and private pack history
- Lane C says recurrence looks like editorial return, quiet mastery, and shadow community
- Lane D says recurrence looks like appointment shells, clips, folklore, rivalries, and featured events

`[assumed:reasoned]` This means the mature product should not be framed as a choice between "private rooms" and "community features." The likely shape is layered:

- private-first play remains foundational
- recurring memory attaches at room, group, and player levels
- bounded public shells emerge where they genuinely add spectator value or shared ritual

## Which experiences feel central, peripheral, or speculative

`[assumed:reasoned]` Central:

- local recurring party
- private online sync
- solo async ritual

`[assumed:reasoned]` Important but likely secondary in product center-of-gravity terms:

- bounded community/public/event shells
- unconventional local topologies that bridge co-presence and separation

`[assumed:reasoned]` More speculative:

- any future where publicness becomes the default product center
- any future where all major families are expected to support every archetype equally
- any future where real-time action/racing determines the whole platform architecture by itself

## What this round parks for later foreclosure synthesis

`[governing:cited]` Round 2A was never supposed to close `RESP-04`. It was supposed to produce the lived-experience map that later foreclosure work can pressure against the engineering-exposure research in `02-lanes/architecture/lane-i-output.md` and `02-lanes/architecture/lane-j-output.md`.

`[projected:reasoned]` The next synthesis should explicitly test at least these questions:

- what room/container shape keeps local party, private sync, solo async, and event shells simultaneously possible without pretending they are one thing?
- what visibility model must stay open so private state, staged reveal, audience shell, and shadow-community traces can coexist?
- what identity/history model is needed so house memory, private-room ritual, solo progression, and bounded public event memory do not collapse into one user-profile abstraction?
- what authority boundaries must stay portable so local-hosted, private-room, and later event-shell forms do not silently diverge into incompatible product paths?
- what cadence/content abstractions are needed so editorial drops, event packs, evergreen collections, and one-night local sessions can all exist without one global schedule model?

`[projected:reasoned]` It should also test what not to universalize:

- not every mode needs solo parity
- not every mode needs public shell support
- not every local form needs to become remote
- not every remote topology needs a host-screen analogue
