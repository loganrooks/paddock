# Round 2A Launch Plan

## Position in the sequence

This plan sits between:

- the governance and gap-review layer
- the actual Round 2A lane launches

It assumes that a prelaunch meta-review may still tune the bundle before dispatch.

## Why Round 2A is split

Round 2A should not run as one giant undifferentiated experience-mapping pass.

Main reason:

- the experience classes are materially different enough that one agent is likely to flatten them together again

The split is meant to keep:

- local social texture
- remote sync topology
- solo cadence
- public/community/event shell logic

from collapsing back into one mushy category map.

## Central ownership

The main thread should retain ownership of:

- calibration carry-forward
- final comparison across archetypes
- what gets parked for later foreclosure synthesis
- the final Round 2A synthesis artifact

## Proposed lane set

### Lane A: Local recurring party and unconventional local forms

Main scope:

- couch and host-screen social play
- recurring friend-group night
- hybrid-in-the-room forms
- separate rooms, private phones, partial separation, and other unconventional local shapes

Why separate:

- local room energy, reveal cadence, embarrassment thresholds, and host performance are easiest to flatten if treated as just another context row

### Lane B: Private online sync and topology-sensitive remote play

Main scope:

- private online synchronous groups
- Discord-group play
- hidden-info remote play
- separated-local or same-house-different-rooms forms when they are closer to remote topology than to shared-stage couch play

Why separate:

- this lane needs to think about comms, private views, trust, and role-specific experience without getting absorbed into generic "online" language

### Routing rule for Lane A vs Lane B overlap

If a case is ambiguous:

- send it to Lane A when its main differentiator is shared-stage room energy, co-presence, host timing, or in-room social spectacle
- send it to Lane B when its main differentiator is topology, private views, role secrecy, comms structure, or remote-like interaction constraints

Same-house-but-separated forms may belong to either lane depending on which of those two logics is doing the real work.

### Lane C: Solo async ritual and race-weekend return loops

Main scope:

- daily/weekly async play
- race-weekend ritual
- progression and cadence
- repeat return structures that do not depend on everyone showing up at once

Why separate:

- async retention and ritual design have a very different texture from party-night design

### Lane D: Community, public surface, and streamer-adjacent event shells

Main scope:

- public/private boundary
- audience shell
- streamer/watchability
- community persistence
- event-shell logic

Why separate:

- the mature-product and public-surface questions are large enough to deserve their own lane rather than being bolted onto solo async or local party work

## Recommended output set

- `round-2a-lane-a-local-party-output.md`
- `round-2a-lane-b-private-online-output.md`
- `round-2a-lane-c-solo-async-output.md`
- `round-2a-lane-d-community-event-output.md`
- `round-2a-experience-archetypes-output.md`

## Dependency logic

The lane outputs should feed the central synthesis.

The central synthesis should then feed:

- the later architecture-foreclosure synthesis

That later synthesis should explicitly combine:

- Round 2A experience mapping
- `02-lanes/architecture/lane-i-output.md`
- `02-lanes/architecture/lane-j-output.md`

## What not to do at launch

Do not:

- turn the lane split into isolated silos that never get compared centrally
- ask each lane to solve architecture foreclosure itself
- reintroduce broad family ranking as the hidden center of gravity
- merge large-room feasibility back into this round
- let historical but stale carry-forward docs quietly steer the round over the newer governance and gap-review bundle

## Ready-to-launch condition

Round 2A is ready to launch when:

- the prelaunch meta-review finds no blocking framing problems
- the lane boundaries feel clear enough
- the governance trail is coherent enough that later outputs can be assessed against the current gap review
