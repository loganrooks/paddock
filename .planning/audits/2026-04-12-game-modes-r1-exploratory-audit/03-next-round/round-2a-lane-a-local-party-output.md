# Round 2A Lane A Output

## Lane framing

`[evidenced:cited]` Local party is not just one context among many in the source set. It is the M1 anchor experience, defined in the handoff as "friends on the same couch, host screen as shared spectacle, immediate social reaction," and this lane is explicitly tasked with local recurring party night, host-screen-plus-phone play, and unconventional same-house variants rather than remote comparison work (`.planning/explore/2026-04-11-product-vision-game-design/HANDOFF.md:96-104`, `.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/03-next-round/round-2a-lane-a-local-party-task-spec.md:25-30`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` The live question here is not "is couch multiplayer good?" It is what forms of recognition, bluffing, performance, shame-buffering, and recurring group folklore become strongest when bodies are co-present and the room can react as one.

`[open:reasoned]` This lane stays exploratory. It identifies local-first pressures and promising shapes, but it does not claim to settle the later cross-archetype foreclosure matrix that `RESP-04` still reserves for synthesis.

## What this experience feels like in practice

`[assumed:reasoned]` A great local Prix Guesser night feels less like a quiet quiz app and more like a compact F1 party show. The room shares one stage, the host can pace buildup and release, and the reveal lands as a collective beat rather than as isolated private feedback.

`[assumed:reasoned]` The best local cadence is usually:
- shared prompt intake
- short private commitment or shouted speculation
- visible hesitation, bragging, or bluffing
- reveal with comparison, argument, or laughter
- fast reset into the next beat.

`[projected:reasoned]` What makes it distinct is not just co-presence. It is that co-presence turns near-misses into entertainment, lets mixed-knowledge players contribute via confidence, humor, sabotage, or interpretation, and gives the host real performer leverage over silence, embarrassment, and escalation.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Embarrassment tolerance is central. Local play can create bigger laughs than remote play, but it can also punish wrong answers harder. That means the strongest local forms usually either:
- make failure funny and social
- keep submissions private until reveal
- or distribute authority so nobody is always "on stage."

`[assumed:reasoned]` Recurring local groups also accumulate memory faster than other archetypes. The experience becomes partly about the mode and partly about house folklore: who always overconfidently guesses Monza, who panics under timers, who becomes the resident steward, who starts fake radio lines before the reveal.

`[projected:reasoned]` Unconventional same-house forms matter because they change the feel without becoming remote. Host-screen-plus-phone play adds secrecy and cleaner reveal control. Partial separation or separate rooms can create spy-vs-panel, hidden-role, or embarrassment-buffered structures while still preserving the shared return-to-stage moment that makes the night feel local.

## What kinds of game territories fit or resist it

`[evidenced:cited]` Round 1 already surfaced several families with strong social replay, reveal energy, spectator value, or rematch scaffolding, which makes them natural raw material for local party interpretation rather than proof that they all belong in the same package (`.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/01-round-1/round-1-output.md:36-89`, `.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/01-round-1/round-1-output.md:193-216`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` Strong local fits:
- authored reveal and bluff territories such as `Words of Wisdom`, because co-present laughter, fake confidence, and staged answer drops are the engine rather than decoration
- verdict and argument territories such as `The Stewards' Room`, because debate theater and social-identity court read strongly in one room
- expressive reveal territories such as `Paddock Fashion`, because spectator reaction is immediate and recurring group taste becomes part of the fun
- short coordination spikes such as `Pit Stop Co-op`, because local presence helps shouted timing, blame, and retry energy
- readable action-chaos spikes such as predator-hunter variants, because spectacle and rematch energy are high even if they are not the full-night anchor.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Mixed but promising local fits:
- `Team Principal's Desk` if it leans into asymmetry, confidence, or team-talk rather than flat symmetric guessing
- commentary/crowd recognition as opener, interstitial, or eventized burst rather than the whole identity of the night.

`[evidenced:cited]` Some families already read as better elsewhere, or at least not primarily here. `The Grid Walk` was explicitly called out in Round 1 as one of the strongest remote-sync discussion-first co-op candidates, which weakens its claim to be the core local headliner even if a local variant could still exist (`.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/01-round-1/round-1-output.md:119-124`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` Local recurring party resists:
- long solitary deduction with little reveal theater
- modes whose main reward is daily return rather than same-session escalation
- designs that require prolonged silent handset time
- public-community moderation problems being imported into a private-room experience too early.

## What this experience wants from topology, authority, visibility, and cadence

`[evidenced:cited]` The architecture lanes already argued that modes should not be forced into one natural room shape, and specifically named `shared_stage + private handsets`, `all_private_screens + meeting phases`, and related topologies as first-class declarations rather than exceptions (`.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/02-lanes/architecture/lane-i-output.md:307-318`, `.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/02-lanes/architecture/lane-i-output.md:502-523`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` Central local topology:
- one shared host screen or shared stage
- players committing on phones, hot-seat devices, or small team surfaces
- re-convergence on the stage for reveal.

`[projected:reasoned]` Valuable secondary local topologies:
- same-house split rooms for hidden-role or sabotage variants
- one actor plus experts or one driver plus pit wall patterns
- shared-stage plus a larger passive audience shell for party-watch variants.

`[evidenced:cited]` Visibility needs to be explicit. The earlier architecture work said different modes will want fully public prompts, private submissions, per-role secrets, and moderator-only controls, and that visibility belongs in state publication rather than UI hiding (`.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/02-lanes/architecture/lane-i-output.md:274-284`, `.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/02-lanes/architecture/lane-j-output.md:235-237`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` For this lane, the highest-value visibility patterns are:
- public prompt, private answer, shared reveal
- role-specific private information for asymmetric local variants
- audience-visible aggregates or scores that do not leak private hands too early.

`[evidenced:cited]` Authority should stay separable from presentation topology. The earlier lane argued for a runtime-neutral authority module that can run locally in the host browser for party play and later move to a dedicated service, precisely so M1-local choices do not force a rewrite when sync or larger rooms arrive (`.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/02-lanes/architecture/lane-i-output.md:450-469`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` For local recurring party, that translates into a practical product read:
- host-led or local-hosted authority is enough for the core experience
- the platform should still treat rules/state progression as a module, not as UI glue
- local feel should come from cadence and reveal design first, not from trusting every client.

`[evidenced:cited]` The architecture work also split sparse prompt/reveal transport from future action/simulation transport and warned against forcing both through one naive path (`.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/02-lanes/architecture/lane-i-output.md:474-500`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` That fits this lane well. Most local party headliners want an `evented_turn_profile`: sparse commands, timers, votes, text, reveals, and spectacle beats. If a future local action spike exists, it should be treated as a bounded special case layered on authority rather than as the definition of local play.

`[evidenced:cited]` Earlier engineering review noted that even when local responsiveness matters, mature systems usually preserve authoritative truth and add a narrowly scoped local-feel layer on top rather than giving clients full trust (`.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/02-lanes/architecture/lane-j-output.md:239-255`).

`[open:reasoned]` The unresolved topology question inside this lane is not whether unconventional local forms matter. They do. The open question is which of them should be considered core enough to shape Phase 01-facing abstractions now, versus being preserved as optional but real extension targets.

## What this experience does to privacy, recurrence, and community

`[evidenced:cited]` Round 1 already warned that expression modes read best as private-first with curated public/community later, and that virality should not be mistaken for retention (`.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/01-round-1/round-1-output.md:172-179`, `.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/01-round-1/round-1-output.md:193-205`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` Local recurring party strengthens that reading. The first community layer here is the recurring friend group itself, not a public feed. Privacy is not just safety policy; it is part of why riskier, funnier, or more embarrassing formats can work.

`[projected:reasoned]` Recurrence in this archetype wants:
- house-level memory
- recurring hosts and recurring packs
- race-weekend themed nights
- rematch structures
- lightweight longitudinal stats that produce bragging rights without turning the room into homework.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Community in this lane is best thought of as micro-community and folklore generation. The product value is in helping a group remember, remix, and revisit its own history: last week's funniest fake radio line, a recurring controversy packet, a season-long in-house steward table.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Public community is derivative here, not central. Clip-sharing, pack-sharing, or spectator crossover may later matter, but importing public-facing moderation, discoverability, and identity systems into the local archetype too early would distort what makes it work.

`[evidenced:cited]` This matters because the handoff's maturity vision asked not only about modes, but about retention loop, streamer/spectator integration, and community features beyond gameplay; local party answers those questions first through recurrence and hostable social ritual, not through daily-feed mechanics (`.planning/explore/2026-04-11-product-vision-game-design/HANDOFF.md:106-114`).

## What this lane changes about existing game judgments

`[assumed:reasoned]` `Words of Wisdom` looks even stronger under the local lens than it did in Round 1, because its social fabrication engine benefits directly from shared laughter, bluff-read energy, and hostable reveals. Local does not just "support" it; local helps explain why it felt load-bearing in the first place.

`[assumed:reasoned]` `The Stewards' Room` reads less like a dry judgment mode and more like one of the clearest local headliner candidates, provided packet design preserves argument theater and does not overburden the room with reading or procedural drag.

`[assumed:reasoned]` `Paddock Fashion` becomes more central to product identity in local rooms than a flat "UGC mode" label would suggest. Its immediate spectator energy and recurring group taste make it especially suitable for house folklore generation even if public-community tooling stays later.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Predator-hunter and `Pit Stop Co-op` families read best as local spikes, chaos rounds, or palate cleansers rather than as the whole night's durable backbone. Local strengthens them, but mostly in session composition rather than in long-form identity.

`[assumed:reasoned]` `Team Principal's Desk` becomes more promising if it embraces asymmetry and role play. Local co-presence makes live huddles, betrayal, and confidence signaling more legible than flat synchronous guessing does.

`[assumed:reasoned]` `The Grid Walk` is still valuable, but this lane reinforces that its strongest read is not "must headline local." It looks more like a different archetype should claim it, with local adaptation as an optional fork rather than the center.

`[assumed:reasoned]` This lane also sharpens one cross-portfolio judgment from Round 1: "session role matters" is not abstract. Local recurring party strongly wants a portfolio mix of headliners, spikes, and interstitials rather than one mode stretched across an entire evening (`.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/01-round-1/round-1-output.md:207-216`, `.planning/audits/2026-04-12-game-modes-r1-exploratory-audit/01-round-1/round-1-output.md:245-256`).

## What remains uncertain

`[open:reasoned]` It remains unclear which unconventional local variants are central enough to shape early abstractions now:
- host-screen plus phones almost certainly is
- same-house split rooms and partial separation probably matter
- but the exact threshold between "real product shape" and "valuable niche variant" is still open.

`[open:reasoned]` The lane does not yet settle the best embarrassment-management mix. Some groups will want loud public failure; others will need more private commitment, team shielding, or role rotation.

`[open:reasoned]` It is still uncertain how much of the long-term local portfolio should rely on phones versus occasional shared-device or hot-seat play. Too much handset time risks cooling the room, but too little private surface limits asymmetric and shame-buffered designs.

`[open:reasoned]` The boundary between "local action spike" and "architecture-defining local action branch" remains open. The current read is that party-local mostly wants evented turns, with faster control patterns as secondary experiments.

`[open:reasoned]` This lane also does not resolve how local micro-community features should later connect to broader community/public systems without flattening the privacy and intimacy that make the local archetype work.

## What to carry into foreclosure synthesis

`[projected:reasoned]` Carry forward that local recurring party is not equivalent to "everyone sees the same screen all the time." The synthesis should preserve at least a family of local-first topologies: shared stage, shared stage plus private handsets, and same-house hidden-info variants.

`[projected:reasoned]` Carry forward that participant rights should be richer than `host` and `player` if local social play is taken seriously. Narrator, judge, expert, audience, or saboteur roles are not exotic edge cases here; they are part of the local design surface.

`[projected:reasoned]` Carry forward that visibility-scoped publication is an early concern, not late polish. Private submissions until reveal and per-role secret information are central to several of the best local forms.

`[projected:reasoned]` Carry forward that local-first does not justify coupling rules/state progression to one browser UI shell. M1 may be locally hosted, but the authority boundary should remain portable.

`[projected:reasoned]` Carry forward that local recurrence creates a distinct product layer: house memory, event cadence, host rituals, and session composition. If early planning only models single isolated matches, it will underfit one of the strongest mature-product paths local play offers.

`[open:reasoned]` Do not carry forward a closed claim that local is the universal center of the product. Carry forward that it is a primary archetype with unique strengths, and that later synthesis still needs to compare it against online sync, solo async, and community/event shells before architecture is closed.
