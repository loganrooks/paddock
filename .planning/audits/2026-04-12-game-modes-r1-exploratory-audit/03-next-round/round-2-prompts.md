# Round 2 Prompts

These are not meant as yes/no checks. They are prompts meant to sharpen the next round after creator feedback.

## Methodological carry-forward

The next round should explicitly avoid two failure modes that became clearer after Round 1:

- treating one plausible realization of an idea as if it were already the game
- imposing one shared ontology across all games

Carry-forward rule:

- treat each promising idea as design terrain
- compare multiple realizations when useful
- let the ontology come from the game itself
- do not assume abstraction is always refinement for meme-heavy or culturally specific ideas
- do not mix a `general architecture exposure` pass with a `50+ / massive-room feasibility` pass unless that combination is explicitly desired

This especially matters when evaluating:

- couch vs online sync vs streamer-friendly realizations
- how community or persistent-group history might change the strongest form
- whether something is actually one game, several modes, or several related games sharing a fantasy
- where a game's success may depend on holding both F1-cultural charge and legitimate underlying loop quality

Architecture carry-forward:

- if the goal is to expose early planning implications across promising game ideas, keep the pass broad and substrate-focused
- if the goal is to study `20-50`, `50+`, or larger-room support, run that as a separate research lane with external reference designs and networking/topology comparison

## A. Strong-family reactions

### `Words of Wisdom`

- Which read feels right to you:
  `Words of Wisdom` as a headliner family, as a strong mid-session staple, or as a more selective cultural-fluency layer?
- Of the three live branches surfaced in Round 1, which feel most alive to you:
  social Fibbage core, audio-enhanced reveal, eventized/appointment derivatives?
- Where do you think Round 1 still under-read or over-read its depth?

### `The Stewards' Room`

- Does the split between serious verdict play and social-identity court play feel real to you, or does one of those directions feel obviously more alive?
- What kind of room energy do you want this family to create:
  smart disagreement, performative argument, casual talk-radio chaos, or something else?
- How much do you want this mode to depend on actual incident packets versus more abstract judgment structures?

### `Paddock Fashion`

- Which sub-forks feel most promising:
  constrained drawing, reference recreation, commentary/performance, recognition/history, or something else?
- How much embarrassment/performative energy do you want this family to require?
- Do you see Fashion as one of the product’s likely standout social modes, or more as a hidden-discovery branch?

### Predator-hunter action family

- Does the read of `Verstappen / Talib` as a shared predator-hunter family feel right to you?
- Which parts of that family feel most exciting:
  readable chase, hunter asymmetry, unlock ladders, circuit variation, challenge modes, social rematch energy?
- How much do you want the current joke/naming layer to matter to the long-term identity of those modes?
- For these games specifically, how should the next round hold the tension between:
  memey cultural specificity and legitimate underlying game structure?
- Where would abstraction improve the game, and where would abstraction accidentally cut away the exact F1-meme voltage that makes the game worth building?
- What parts of the naming, audio, presentation, and driver mythology are actually load-bearing rather than optional wrapper?
- Which replay systems belong underneath that meme charge:
  unlock ladders, vehicle classes, challenge modes, hunter profiles, circuit packs, spectator powers, or something else?

## B. Under-shaped branch pressure-tests

### `commentary / crowd recognition`

- Does this feel like a real branch worth another round of design, or mostly an interesting derivative of `Words of Wisdom`?
- What makes commentary/crowd recognition exciting to you:
  cultural fluency, audio drama, race-weekend freshness, spectator value, or something else?
- Where do you think the family meaningfully splits from engine-identification fantasy?

### `Team Principal's Desk`

- What is the real pleasure you want from this family:
  strategy hindsight, asymmetric role-play, confidence testing, reactive race drama, or another pleasure entirely?
- What would make it feel like a game instead of a themed scenario prompt?
- Which forks do you want another round to pressure:
  asymmetric comms, team orders, strategy ladders, confidence declarations, reactive incident packs?
- What are the strongest competing realizations of this design territory:
  couch host-screen + phones, private online sync, unfolding multi-decision rounds, or something else?
- Which parts of its ontology feel native to the game itself, and which parts are the audit forcing onto it?
- Does a Ferrari-coded chaos version feel materially better than a neutral strategy version?
- Would a deliberately confusing / overloaded / comedic wall interface make this territory more alive?
- Does the strongest form stay literally about racing strategy, or could the driver / control-room asymmetry be abstracted into stranger but funnier scenarios?
- Could a creative local form work the way `Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes` can:
  same house, different rooms, phones, partial separation, or other asymmetric local setups?
- Does this want to be clearly distinct from `Pit Wall`, and if so, is the distinction:
  tone, interface chaos, authority split, context, or something else?
- Which realizations stay recognizably F1-rooted even when they become highly absurd?
- Are we accidentally treating literal F1 -> abstract -> absurd as a hierarchy when these may just be different but equally live forms?
- What kinds of absurd scenarios actually preserve the right F1 grammar:
  push calls, pit windows, traffic management, racing lines, grip, overcomplicated support systems, rival teams, or something else?

### `The Grid Walk`

- Does the remote-sync/co-op read resonate, or do you imagine a different social shape for it?
- What kind of information tension do you want:
  partial clues, communication limits, role asymmetry, prediction under uncertainty, or a different form?
- Should the next round treat this as a sleeper specialist, or as a broader family candidate?

### `Meme Prompts`

- Do you want this to survive as a standalone mode, or are the more interesting futures actually its descendants and stricter subformats?
- What kind of structure would make it feel less generic to you:
  better prompt architecture, stronger reveal/judgment, recurring packs, role constraints, performer framing?
- How private or public do you want this family to be by default?

### `Pit Stop Co-op`

- Do you mainly imagine this as a sharp microgame, or do you want it to grow into a deeper repeatable family?
- What kind of replay do you want from it:
  mastery, scenario variation, role complexity, spectator comedy, escalating breakdown, or something else?
- What would make it feel like more than “funny three times”?
- What realizations of the operational-crisis fantasy seem most alive:
  pure pit-stop spike, cascading garage crisis, conflicting incentives, online coordinated roles, or something else?
- Which distinctions here are actually native to the game, and which ones should not be forced into generic audit buckets?

## C. Cross-portfolio prompts

### Session role

- Which modes currently feel like headliners?
- Which feel like short spikes or palate cleansers?
- Which feel like hidden-discovery side modes?
- Which feel like recurring-group staples rather than front-door draws?

### Context profile

- Which modes do you most want optimized for couch / host-screen play?
- Which modes feel most promising for private online sync?
- Which modes feel like they could support async derivatives without hollowing out the fun?
- Which modes should simply be allowed to remain context-specialists?
- Where does exploring a different context reveal a better realization of the same game rather than a separate game entirely?
- Which current ideas are being under-read because the audit is still too attached to one first realization?
- Which ideas become more alive in unconventional local arrangements:
  separate rooms, phone calls, hidden screens, host screen plus private phones, or other asymmetric setups?

### Privacy / community / public surface

- Which families feel strongest as private-room culture builders?
- Which, if any, really want public/community layers?
- Which ones should stay deliberately private-first even in later rounds?

### Tone and social risk

- Where do you want the project to stay warm and affectionate?
- Where are you happy for it to get sharp, chaotic, or a bit mean?
- Which current joke surfaces feel fun in private but questionable on any broader-facing surface?

### Native ontology / design terrain

- For the games you care about most, what do you think their own ontology actually is?
- Which distinctions feel real inside the game, and which ones feel like outside analyst language?
- Where do you want the next round to map the terrain of possible realizations instead of trying to define the game too fast?

### Cultural charge / mechanical spine

- Which current ideas need to preserve a very specific F1 meme or cultural surface in order to stay alive?
- Where has the audit been too eager to "clean up" an idea by abstracting away what made it special?
- Where should the next round explicitly search for replay structures that sit underneath the cultural charge without flattening it?
- Where are we under-reading absurd but still F1-rooted realizations because the audit is too attached to literalism?

## D. Possible next-round structures

These are not commitments, only candidate shapes for the next round:

### Option 1: Deepen the strongest families

- `Words of Wisdom`
- `The Stewards' Room`
- `Paddock Fashion`
- predator-hunter action family

### Option 2: Pressure-test the under-shaped but promising branches

- `commentary / crowd recognition`
- `Team Principal's Desk`
- `The Grid Walk`
- `Meme Prompts`
- `Pit Stop Co-op`

### Option 3: Portfolio composition round

Focus not on single modes, but on:

- session role
- context profile
- private vs public expansion
- how adjacent modes sit around the broader product shape

### Option 4: Design-terrain / ontology round

Focus on:

- native ontology per promising game
- strongest competing realizations
- where online/community/context shifts reveal a better version of the same game
- where the audit is overfitting to one named pitch or one schema

### Option 5: Cultural-charge / loop-quality round

Focus on:

- meme-heavy ideas that need both F1-cultural specificity and real replay structure
- where abstraction helps versus where it kills the idea
- what presentation, naming, audio, and social framing are actually load-bearing
- what systems can support return without sanding off the joke-source

### Option 6: Tone / naming / social-threshold round

Focus on:

- warmth vs mockery
- embarrassment tolerance
- public-surface viability
- whether some strong mechanic families need different packaging

## E. Most useful creator response format

The next round will be sharpest if creator feedback does some mix of:

- identifying the judgments that feel most right
- identifying the judgments that feel most wrong
- naming which families you most want another round to deepen
- naming where the current tone/context read feels off
- naming which branches feel like real long-term product material versus fun but secondary territory
