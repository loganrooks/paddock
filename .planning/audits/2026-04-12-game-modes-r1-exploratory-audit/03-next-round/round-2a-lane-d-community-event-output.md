# Round 2A Lane D Output

## Lane framing

`[evidenced/governing:mixed]` Lane D is best understood as a wrapper-and-boundary lane, not a claim that Prix Guesser should become broadly public by default. The handoff explicitly asks about platform maturity, streamer/spectator integration, community features, and social experiences beyond the immediate session (`HANDOFF.md:96-115`), while the lane spec narrows that into community-facing experiences, public/private boundaries, spectator readability, and event shells (`round-2a-lane-d-community-event-task-spec.md:24-32`, `round-2a-lane-d-community-event-task-spec.md:57-78`). Round 1 already pushed against universal-context thinking and said some expression modes should stay private creation first, curated public later (`round-1-output.md:172-191`). The useful question here is therefore not "how public should Prix Guesser become?" but "which mature-product shells add energy, memory, and watchability without distorting the private-first core?"

## Traceability and scope

`[evidenced:cited]` This lane answers the exact scope it was given: it directly supports `RESP-03`, most strongly informs `GAP-03` and `GAP-07`, contributes evidence toward `RESP-04`, and does not itself close `RESP-04` (`round-2a-lane-d-community-event-task-spec.md:34-41`). That matches the gap review, which says the next round should include explicit experience classes for party, private online, solo async, and community/public/event surfaces because the mature-product and community-experience layer remains under-synthesized (`next-round-gap-review.md:374-407`, `next-round-gap-review.md:474-489`).

`[evidenced:cited]` Historical carry-forward remains background-only here. The newer governance stack explicitly treats the gap review and Round 2A spec as the steering source, not the older Round 1 self-eval or stale prompt bundle (`round-2a-experience-archetypes-task-spec.md:76-90`, `round-2a-lane-d-community-event-task-spec.md:43-46`, `review-trail-framework.md:290-311`, `review-trail-framework.md:325-339`).

## Calibration carry-forward

`[governing:session]` The live calibration for this lane is:

- publicness is not automatically desirable
- private-first experiences may still be the center of the product
- community features should be treated as experience shapes, not bolt-on platform furniture
- public surfaces need judgment about whether they are healthy, bounded, and on-tone rather than merely legible

`[evidenced/governing:mixed]` This is consistent with the lane spec's own carry-forward (`round-2a-lane-d-community-event-task-spec.md:48-55`), the root Round 2A rule that examples are probes rather than mandatory branches and that architecture exposure should not be prematurely collapsed (`round-2a-experience-archetypes-task-spec.md:63-75`), and Round 1's warning that virality and retention are distinct and that not every mode needs every context (`round-1-output.md:183-216`).

## What this experience feels like in practice

`[assumed:reasoned]` The strongest public/community shell for Prix Guesser does not feel like open matchmaking or a permanently public social network. It feels more like an appointment layer around authored or hosted play: a race-weekend live challenge, a streamer-hosted reveal night, a community event pack, or a semi-public room where the audience understands the arc even if only a few people are actively playing. This reading is grounded in the handoff's interest in streamer watchability, season-long events, and community features beyond the single session (`HANDOFF.md:103-115`), plus Round 1's recurring emphasis on reveal machines, spectator value, and eventized derivatives (`round-1-output.md:47-52`, `round-1-output.md:95-104`, `round-1-output.md:193-205`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` In practice, the good version of this shell probably has four layers:

- a host or editorial frame that makes the event legible
- a player-facing contest or participation loop
- an audience-facing reading surface with clear reveal rhythm
- a memory surface afterward: clips, winners, rivalries, references, or "remember that call?" folklore

`[assumed:reasoned]` The lane therefore points toward bounded publicness. Prix Guesser looks more naturally suited to public episodes, featured events, and watchable shells than to a permanently open communal core. That keeps the mature-product question alive without forcing the product to stop being private-first.

## What kinds of game territories fit or resist it

`[evidenced:cited]` Strong fits are the territories Round 1 already described as readable, reveal-driven, or eventizable:

- `Words of Wisdom` fits well because Round 1 already saw a social Fibbage core plus eventized or appointment-like derivatives (`round-1-output.md:36-52`).
- `The Stewards' Room` fits well because verdict reveal and argument loop are inherently audience-readable (`round-1-output.md:53-64`, `round-1-output.md:202-205`).
- `commentary / crowd recognition` fits well because Round 1 explicitly named spectator value and eventized freshness as its strongest qualities (`round-1-output.md:95-104`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` Conditional fits are territories that become interesting only if the shell is curated and bounded:

- `Paddock Fashion` can support showcase or audience energy, but Round 1's stronger read remains private creation first and curated public/community later (`round-1-output.md:65-77`, `round-1-output.md:172-179`).
- predator-hunter action can be clip-worthy and stream-readable, but publicness is probably secondary to the core rematch and unlock energy; virality alone is not enough (`round-1-output.md:78-90`, `round-1-output.md:193-205`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` Resistant territories are the ones whose main pleasure is intimacy, low-stakes embarrassment, private folklore, or topology-specific trust. Those should stay mostly in Lanes A and B. Public shell logic can frame them occasionally, but it should not become the default reading of the whole portfolio.

## What this experience wants from topology, authority, visibility, and cadence

`[assumed:reasoned]` Topology: this shell wants a clear distinction between performer/player surfaces and audience surfaces. The mature-product question is not merely "can strangers join?" but "can the same underlying experience be seen differently by players, hosts, and spectators?" That follows from the handoff's streamer/spectator question and the lane's required lenses around audience shell and stream readability (`HANDOFF.md:112-115`, `round-2a-lane-d-community-event-task-spec.md:61-69`).

`[assumed:reasoned]` Authority: public or semi-public shells want more host, editorial, or event authority than private rooms do. Someone or something has to own pacing, featured content, audience boundaries, and trust decisions. This is not yet a technical moderation architecture answer, but it does imply that "room authority model" matters more once public shells exist than when the product is only friend-group play.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Visibility: public surfaces want selective visibility, not universal transparency. Some experiences get stronger when spectators can see the reveal arc without seeing every contestant secret. Others only work if the audience sees aggregate drama, not raw intimate play. This makes visibility a design seam in its own right rather than a cosmetic overlay.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Cadence: the most natural cadence is probably eventized and seasonal rather than constant. The handoff explicitly asks about race-weekend-tied packs and season-long community structures (`HANDOFF.md:104-115`, `HANDOFF.md:145-148`). Public shells therefore seem most coherent when attached to editorial rhythm: race weekends, featured rivalry nights, creator-hosted specials, or recurring community rituals.

## What this experience does to privacy, recurrence, and community

`[evidenced/governing:mixed]` Privacy becomes a product judgment, not just a setting. Round 1 already says some expression-driven modes should remain private-first (`round-1-output.md:172-179`), and the lane spec explicitly asks which experiences still want to remain private even in a mature product (`round-2a-lane-d-community-event-task-spec.md:71-78`). The likely answer is that the strongest mature version of Prix Guesser keeps private rooms central while allowing opt-in public wrappers, curated showcases, and event participation where the shell genuinely adds value.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Recurrence gets stronger when publicness is episodic. Community memory here is less "always-on public discourse layer" and more recurring folklore: signature reveal moments, recurring hosts, weekend rituals, score grudges, clip circulation, and seasonal reference points. That matches the handoff's interest in friend histories, league tables, and calendar-tied events (`HANDOFF.md:103-115`) while staying closer to Prix Guesser's likely tone than a generic social feed.

`[assumed:reasoned]` Community, then, looks promising when it is:

- event-bounded rather than ambient all the time
- opt-in rather than mandatory for every mode
- built around authored packs, reveals, and shared memory
- comfortable with semi-public shells, not only fully open participation

## What this lane changes about existing game judgments

`[assumed:reasoned]` The main change is that event-shell design now looks like a first-class product territory, not an afterthought. The lane spec explicitly asks where event-shell design matters more than the individual mode itself (`round-2a-lane-d-community-event-task-spec.md:73-78`), and the answer appears to be: quite often. A recurring public or streamer-facing shell may be more important to mature-product differentiation than inventing a brand-new inherently public mode.

`[assumed:reasoned]` This lane strengthens some existing judgments:

- `Words of Wisdom` looks even stronger because its reveal-and-fabrication rhythm travels well into hosted or watchable events.
- `The Stewards' Room` gains weight as a spectator-readable community-night candidate.
- `commentary / crowd recognition` becomes easier to justify as an event shell because spectator value was already one of its best traits.

`[assumed:reasoned]` It also sharpens some limits:

- `Paddock Fashion` should still be read as private-first with curated public spillover, not as evidence that all expressive play should be public by default.
- clip-worthiness should not be mistaken for centrality; Round 1 already warns that virality and return loop are different judgments (`round-1-output.md:193-205`).
- public shell potential should remain deliberately secondary for modes whose real value is intimate room energy, private trust, or low-exposure experimentation.

## What remains uncertain

`[open:reasoned]` This lane does not yet settle:

- whether the healthiest public surface is live-streamed, semi-public room-based, or mostly editorial/asynchronous
- how much audience participation improves Prix Guesser versus making it noisier and thinner
- whether community persistence should center on profiles, clubs, leagues, event history, or lighter folklore traces
- how dependent the strongest public shell is on human hosting or streamer performance
- how much of this needs early architectural accommodation versus later wrapper work

`[open:cited]` Those unknowns are acceptable here because the lane was asked not to solve technical moderation architecture and not to close `RESP-04` by itself (`round-2a-lane-d-community-event-task-spec.md:80-87`, `round-2a-lane-d-community-event-task-spec.md:34-41`).

## What to carry into foreclosure synthesis

`[projected:reasoned]` Later foreclosure synthesis should test at least these seams against early architecture:

- whether the product can support distinct player, host, and audience visibility layers
- whether public or semi-public events are modeled as separate shells rather than as the same thing as private rooms
- whether event cadence and featured-pack scheduling need explicit support
- whether community memory should be able to attach to packs, events, rivalries, or recurring rituals without forcing every mode into public identity
- whether authority and trust boundaries can stay flexible enough to support private-first play plus bounded public wrappers

`[projected:reasoned]` Just as important: the synthesis should preserve the option that public/community shells remain secondary. Lane D surfaces meaningful pressures, but it does not show that publicness is the product center. The likely mature shape is still one where private friend-group and small-group experiences remain foundational, with public and streamer-facing shells layered on where they create real spectator energy or recurring community memory.
