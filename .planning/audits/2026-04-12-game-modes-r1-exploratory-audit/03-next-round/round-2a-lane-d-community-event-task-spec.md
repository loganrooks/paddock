# Round 2A Lane D Task Spec

## Lane purpose

This lane explores:

- community-facing experiences
- public/private boundaries
- streamer and spectator readability
- event shells
- recurring public or semi-public participation shapes

## Traceability and scope

This lane:

- directly supports `RESP-03`
- most strongly informs `GAP-03` and `GAP-07`
- contributes evidence toward `RESP-04`
- does not itself close `RESP-04`

Background-only historical artifacts:

- `01-round-1/round-1-self-eval.md`
- `03-next-round/round-2-prompts.md`

## Calibration carry-forward

Preserve these corrections locally:

- publicness is not automatically desirable
- private-first experiences may still be central to the product
- community features should be thought through as experience shapes, not just platform add-ons
- keep room for judgment about which public surfaces are healthy versus distortive

## Core question

What kinds of Prix Guesser experiences become possible or desirable once the project is viewed as more than a private friend-group toy, without assuming everything should become public?

## Required lenses

- audience shell
- spectator value
- stream readability
- privacy boundaries
- moderation and trust
- event cadence
- community persistence and folklore

## Required questions

- which experiences want to remain private-first even in a mature product?
- which experiences plausibly want public or streamer-facing shells?
- what kinds of publicness fit Prix Guesser without distorting its tone?
- where does event-shell design matter more than the individual mode itself?
- what kinds of community memory or persistence are promising?
- where should public or community potential remain deliberately secondary?

## Anti-goals

Do not:

- treat publicness as automatically desirable
- collapse this lane into pure monetization or growth thinking
- treat community features as if they are independent from gameplay experience
- solve the technical moderation architecture here

## Required output sections

1. `Lane framing`
2. `Traceability and scope`
3. `Calibration carry-forward`
4. `What this experience feels like in practice`
5. `What kinds of game territories fit or resist it`
6. `What this experience wants from topology, authority, visibility, and cadence`
7. `What this experience does to privacy, recurrence, and community`
8. `What this lane changes about existing game judgments`
9. `What remains uncertain`
10. `What to carry into foreclosure synthesis`
11. `Coverage note`
