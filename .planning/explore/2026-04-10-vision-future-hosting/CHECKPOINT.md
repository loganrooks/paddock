## Working Model

Working model, not a final decision:

- Prix Guesser should be treated as a staged product rather than one static app definition
- the likely first strong proof is still a private, watchable, shared-screen, host-led ritual with phone/browser participation
- the longer horizon still includes remote live play, solo and async wrappers, and possibly later adjacent or spectator-facing branches
- `shared substrate plus wrappers` remains useful language, but the reruns sharpen that `wrapper`, `stage-shape`, and `sibling product` are not interchangeable
- future-awareness should be made explicit in artifacts and planning checks, but lightly
- watchability should be treated as several distinct design concerns, not one vague aspiration
- product, hosting, funding, public transition, and trust cannot be reasoned about in isolation from one another

## What The Research Now Seems To Support

### Broadly Reinforced

- the product is not well-described as "GeoGuessr with F1 paint"
- the private watchable game-night shell still looks like the strongest first proof
- solo and async still look more like support or extension paths than the emotional center
- browser-first is compatible with staged operator paths:
  - LAN-first
  - self-hosted remote
  - modest public hosting
- future-aware planning is best encoded with low-drag fields and checks, not heavy governance

### Sharpened By The Reruns

- `shared substrate plus wrappers` was directionally right, but too neat by itself
- some later F1 futures may be true wrappers on the same substrate
- some may instead be sibling products, or cross-cutting branches, that only share theme or audience
- feasibility must be split at least across:
  - technical
  - operational
  - adoption
  - economic
  - ethical / trust
- the key hosting transitions are not just traffic steps; they are also expectation and accountability steps
- publicness changes observability, abuse-handling, trust language, and service obligation earlier than raw scale numbers alone suggest
- public transition should be treated not only as a wrapper-order question but also as a `visibility-state and product-boundary` question
- one live possibility now stays more explicitly alive: a mostly private room product plus a sibling public-facing challenge, showcase, or creator surface

## Important Tensions Still Alive

1. Preserve future expansion without overengineering v1.
2. Keep local party ritual strong without making solo or async extensions feel bolted on later.
3. Decide which future candidates are real substrate extensions versus merely adjacent F1 ideas.
4. Balance browser-first coherence against operator convenience and packaging burden.
5. Think about queueing, donations, and transparency honestly without drifting into manipulative scarcity or premature service promises.
6. Determine whether any cooperative or peer-assisted scaling idea is genuinely worth the trust, adoption, and authority costs.
7. Carry future-awareness into roadmap and planning artifacts without turning exploratory work into bureaucracy.
