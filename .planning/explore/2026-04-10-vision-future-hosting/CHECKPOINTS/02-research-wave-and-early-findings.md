# Checkpoint 02: Research Wave And Early Findings

Date: 2026-04-10
Session: `2026-04-10-vision-future-hosting`

## What Actually Happened

After the discussion widened into hosting, staging, sustainability, and public transition questions, the user explicitly asked for research support.

My first instinct was to structure that research as bounded lanes with relatively narrow questions. The user challenged that approach and argued, correctly, that this phase of the discussion was still exploratory enough that narrow question design risked foreclosing the space too early.

That changed the research design.

Instead of using the agents to answer prematurely narrow recommendation questions, the research wave was reframed as:

- option-space mapping
- hidden-assumption surfacing
- trajectory comparison
- precedent analysis
- future-aware planning analysis

In other words, the user pushed the research from "solution evaluation" toward "terrain mapping."

## The Substantive Discussion Shift

This was not just a process change. It reflected a deeper philosophical point in the conversation:

- research here should help keep possibilities open long enough to understand them properly
- but it should still be organized and inspectable

That is why the eventual structure became:

- written orchestration brief
- written lane specs
- research agents writing findings to disk
- explicit epistemic hygiene requirements

The key thing to preserve is that the user did not want "go research X" in a simplistic sense. The user wanted research that was exploratory, self-critical, traceable, and philosophically careful about not collapsing the space too early.

## What The Early Findings Changed

The first landed research findings shifted the discussion in two important ways.

First, the product-futures lane strengthened the idea that the product should not be framed as a binary between local party mode and solo/async play. Instead, it argued that Prix Guesser is better understood as multiple wrappers around one authored F1 substrate.

That matters because it gives a cleaner conceptual language for what had previously been a fuzzier discussion:

- not "which single experience wins?"
- but "what substrate is shared, and which wrappers become available when?"

Second, the future-aware-planning lane reinforced that the repo already has some of the right instincts, especially in the current Phase 1 `CONTEXT.md`. The main issue is not the absence of future-awareness, but that it is not yet consistently preserved in the canonical planning layers.

That means the planning problem is less:

- invent a totally new process

and more:

- preserve and systematize the good future-aware behavior already present

## Important Nuances Preserved

Several nuances should not be lost here:

- the user was not merely asking for "more research"; the user was asking for research with epistemic discipline and traceability
- the user was also implicitly arguing against solution-space foreclosure as a methodological error
- the early research findings did not settle the vision; they mainly gave the conversation better categories and better planning language

## Where This Left The Exploration

By the end of this checkpoint, the shared understanding had advanced from:

- "we should talk about the future"

to:

- "we likely need to think in terms of substrate, wrappers, staged affordances, and explicit non-foreclosure"

The conversation was still open, but better armed.

## Open Questions At This Point

1. If Prix Guesser is a shared substrate with multiple wrappers, what exactly is that substrate?
2. Which wrappers feel like natural extensions of the same product, and which would actually imply a second product line?
3. How should future-aware planning be encoded lightly enough that it protects the long arc without freezing exploration?
