# Checkpoint 01: Resume And Reframe

Date: 2026-04-10
Session: `2026-04-10-vision-future-hosting`

## What Actually Happened

The session began with a resumption error. The active Phase 1 state was initially read too much through the stale `STATE.md` lens, which still sounded like "ready to execute." Re-reading the active `.continue-here.md` corrected that immediately: the paused agenda was not execution but strategic exploration.

That changed the posture of the whole session. The question stopped being "what should happen next in the workflow?" and became "what are we actually trying to build over time, and how should that future affect early decisions?"

## The Framing I Brought In

I summarized the current repo posture as something like:

- not "GeoGuessr with F1 paint"
- more social and authored than generic geography
- leaning toward a host-screen/game-night shape
- but with a roadmap that still behaves more like one deep anchor-mode product than an explicit party-platform trajectory

I suggested that the project looked like it had a tension between:

- **Identity A**: one deep geography/circuit mode
- **Identity B**: an F1 party-platform shell with the geography mode as the first major mode

I also floated the question of which experience was the "core" or "emotional center" of the product, mainly to understand what should exert the strongest shaping pressure on early decisions.

## The Pushback And Why It Mattered

The user pushed back on that framing in an important way.

The pushback was not "those possibilities are wrong." It was "do not reduce the product to one experience too early, because the product will afford different experiences at different stages, and those future stages should already be influencing how we think now."

That correction mattered because it shifted the exploration from:

- choosing a primary mode

to:

- understanding the app/platform as something that evolves across stages
- asking what it should be able to become
- asking how future stages should shape early architecture and planning

## Shared Understanding Reached At This Point

By the end of this checkpoint, the shared ground was roughly:

- the conversation should stay exploratory
- the product should be discussed across multiple stages of development
- local party mode remains important, but it is not the only relevant future
- remote play, async play, streamer-friendliness, and broader platform ambitions are all legitimate parts of the exploration
- future-orientation matters because it will affect how the first iteration should be built

## Important Nuance Preserved

At this stage, there was still an unresolved tension about whether asking for a "core" experience was clarifying or prematurely narrowing.

The user’s concern was that if we frame the product too early around one dominant experience, we may accidentally foreclose better futures.

My underlying concern was the opposite: if every future is equally primary, early product decisions can become too abstract and underconstrained.

That tension remained unresolved here and should be preserved, because it is one of the defining tensions of the whole exploration.

## Open Questions At This Point

1. How should Prix Guesser be understood as a staged product rather than one static app?
2. How should local party, remote live, solo practice, and async challenge play relate to one another?
3. How can future-orientation shape early development without turning everything into speculative overengineering?
