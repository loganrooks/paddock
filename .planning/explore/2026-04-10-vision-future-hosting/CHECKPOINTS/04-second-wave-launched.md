# Checkpoint 04: Second Wave Launched

Updated: 2026-04-10

## Why This Checkpoint Exists

This checkpoint captures the shift from broad exploratory mapping into a more difficult second-wave inquiry about transition, scaling, public exposure, and risk.

It does not mark closure. It marks a change in what the exploration is now trying to pressure-test.

## What Changed In The Discussion

The conversation moved beyond the earlier question of what Prix Guesser might become in the long run.

The user pushed on a harder set of practical questions:

- how a private-first project might scale without requiring reckless personal spending
- whether transparent capacity limits and contribution models could be part of the product's public story
- whether there are transition models besides straightforward paid hosting
- whether some form of peer-assisted or cooperative compute is even worth considering
- how discovery, trust, abuse, security, and operational burden interact with whatever public-facing model is chosen

This was not a move away from vision. It was a move toward treating material constraints as part of the design problem.

## Important Reframing

The user was not asking for a quick monetization brainstorm.

The deeper concern was that answers in this space depend on many variables at once:

- what the product becomes
- what kinds of modes it supports
- how state and authority are structured
- how much friction casual users will tolerate
- what kinds of trust obligations a public service posture creates

That means the inquiry cannot responsibly pretend to settle everything from abstract design reasoning alone.

## Methodological Upgrade Preserved

Before this wave launched, the user pressed for a stronger research standard than the first wave had met.

The desired improvement was not just "more sources" or "more detail." It was:

- more explicit gray-area dwelling
- more willingness to follow unexpected lines of concern
- more honest marking of scope expansion
- better visibility into the path of inquiry itself
- stronger attention to dependencies between questions rather than isolated answers

This matters because transition questions can look deceptively simple when split into neat categories even though they may only make sense relationally.

## What Remains Alive

No decision has been made about:

- whether peer-assisted scaling is viable or a dead end
- whether donations, paid guarantees, free tiers, queues, or contribution models are aligned with the ethos of the project
