# Words of Wisdom — Radio Moment Catalog

> Draft content catalog for the "Words of Wisdom" game mode (Fibbage-style).
> Each entry is framed as it would appear in-game: the setup the host screen shows,
> what players would likely write, and the real answer.
>
> **This is exploratory material, not committed content.** Quotes are sourced from
> F1 broadcasts, reporting, and fan documentation. Exact wording may vary across sources.
> Some entries would need transcript verification before use in a shipping product.

---

## Tier System

- **S-tier**: The real answer is so unexpected that no player would write it. Peak Fibbage material.
- **A-tier**: Strong gap between expected and actual response. Works great in-game.
- **B-tier**: Good moment, but the real answer is guessable by knowledgeable fans. Better as a "knowledge reward" round or bonus context.
- **C-tier**: Famous enough that most F1 fans know it. Use as warm-up / introductory rounds only.

## S-TIER — The Unguessable

### 1. Hülkenberg — "Like a Cow" (2018 Abu Dhabi GP)

**Setup**: Nico Hülkenberg's car has just flipped completely upside down and caught fire at Yas Marina. He radios in:

**What players would write**: "Get me out!" / "The car's on fire, I'm stuck!" / "I need marshals NOW"

**Real answer**: "I'm hanging here like a cow! Get me out, there's fire!"

**Why S-tier**: "Like a cow" is a detail no human would fabricate in a Fibbage context. The absurdist simile delivered while upside down in a burning car is unbeatable.

---

### 2. Button to Alonso — "Pee in Your Seat" (2017 Monaco GP)

**Setup**: 2017 Monaco GP. Jenson Button is racing as a one-off substitute while Alonso races at the Indy 500. Before the race, Alonso calls in on team radio to wish Button good luck. Button responds:

**What players would write**: "Thanks mate, enjoy Indy" / "Cheers Fernando, I'll keep the seat warm" / "Good luck to you too"

**Real answer**: "I'm going to pee in your seat." Alonso: "No, please!"

**Why S-tier**: The setup leads everyone toward sportsmanship. The real answer is playground humor from a 37-year-old world champion. Confirmed Button later said he did not actually do it.

---

### 3. Montoya — Deer on Track (2001 Austrian GP)

**Setup**: A deer has wandered onto the track at the A1 Ring. The Williams engineer needs to warn Juan Pablo Montoya. The engineer radios:

**What players would write**: "Animal on track, Turn 3, stay left" / "Caution, obstacle on circuit" / "Slow down, there's something on track"

**Real answer**: "There's a deer on the track, Juan Pablo — like a horse with horns."
Montoya: "I know, I know. Oh deer! Ha, ha, ha, ha, ha."

**Why S-tier**: The engineer's attempt to describe a deer by analogy ("like a horse with horns") followed by Montoya's dad-joke pun at race speed. Two layers of absurdity.

---

### 4. Grosjean — "I Think Ericsson Hit Us" (2018 Azerbaijan GP)

**Setup**: Behind the safety car in Baku, Romain Grosjean has just spun and hit the wall. Replays show nobody was near him — he hit his own brake magic switch. He radios:

**What players would write**: "I lost the rear!" / "Something broke, check the data" / "The brakes locked up"

**Real answer**: "I think Ericsson hit us."

**Why S-tier**: Marcus Ericsson was nowhere near him. The instinctive blame-deflection to a specific named driver who wasn't involved became one of F1's greatest memes. Perfect for the game because it sounds PLAUSIBLE — which is exactly what makes real Fibbage rounds work.

---

### 5. Russell — "I Think It's Sweat" (2023 Spanish GP)

**Setup**: George Russell desperately wants rain to shake up the Spanish GP. He radios excitedly to report rain at Turn 5. No other driver reports rain. His engineer asks him to confirm. Russell responds:

**What players would write**: "Definitely rain, it's getting heavier" / "Just a few drops for now" / "The track is damp through the sector"

**Real answer**: "Is anyone else reporting rain?... I think it's sweat from the inside of my helmet."

**Why S-tier**: The escalating embarrassment of the self-correction. He thought his own sweat was rain. He had to explain this at the post-race press conference.

## A-TIER — Strong Fibbage Rounds

### 6. Leclerc / Bozzi — "Must Be the Water" (2025 Australian GP)

**Setup**: Mid-race in Melbourne, Charles Leclerc notices his seat is filling with water from a leaking drinks bottle. He has no hydration and is sitting in a wet seat. His engineer Bryan Bozzi responds:

**What players would write**: "Copy, we'll investigate after the race" / "Can you manage?" / "Try to stay focused, Charles"

**Real answer**: "Must be the water." Leclerc: "Let's add that to the Words of Wisdom."

**Why A-tier**: The engineer gives a technically true and completely useless response. The driver's meta-commentary turns it into a cultural moment. ESPN confirmed it was an inside joke between them. THE NAMING INSPIRATION for the game mode.

### 7. Vettel — "Something Loose" (2018 Brazilian GP)

**Setup**: Sebastian Vettel feels something rattling and loose in his cockpit during the race and reports it to his engineer:

**What players would write**: "Something's come loose under the steering column" / "There's a vibration, check the floor" / "I've got a rattle somewhere below"

**Real answer**: "There's something loose between my legs... apart from the obvious."

**Why A-tier**: Self-aware comedy mid-race at 300km/h. Players writing mechanical reports can't compete.

### 8. Leclerc on Magnussen — "It's a Fact" (2018 Japanese GP)

**Setup**: Kevin Magnussen has just collided with Charles Leclerc at Suzuka, ruining his race. Leclerc radios in his assessment:

**What players would write**: "He drove into me!" / "Unbelievable, he ruined my race" / "What was he doing?!"

**Real answer**: "Magnussen is, and will always be, stupid. It's a fact."

**Why A-tier**: Structured like a philosophical theorem. Not anger — a calm declaration. "It's a fact" is what elevates it from a vent to a verdict.

---

### 9. McLaren Engineer to Norris — "Sunday Morning Meetings" (2024 Hungarian GP)

**Setup**: Norris has been refusing team orders to let Piastri past for 20 laps. His engineer Will Joseph, increasingly desperate, says:

**What players would write**: "Lando, the team is asking you to comply" / "Think about the championship" / "Oscar was ahead before the stops"

**Real answer**: "Just remember every single Sunday morning meeting we've had."

**Why A-tier**: Bizarre, specific, and threatening in a way that's impossible to fake. What happens in those meetings? Nobody outside the team knows. That's the power.

### 10. Verstappen — "LOL, What Happened?" (2024 Miami Sprint)

**Setup**: Max Verstappen has just taken pole position for the Miami Sprint despite struggling all weekend. He radios his engineer:

**What players would write**: "Let's go!" / "Good job guys, the car felt great" / "That was a clean lap"

**Real answer**: "LOL, but what happened to the others? Mate, this was terrible."

**Why A-tier**: He literally says "LOL" on team radio while taking pole. And he's genuinely confused about how he got it.

### 11. Lambiase to Verstappen — "Childish" (2024 Hungarian GP)

**Setup**: After contact with Hamilton, Verstappen is venting on the radio about other teams and stewards. His engineer Gianpiero Lambiase responds:

**What players would write**: "Copy, Max, we'll talk about it after" / "Understood, let's focus on the next stint" / "We'll raise it with the stewards"

**Real answer**: "I'm not going to get into a radio fight with the other teams, Max. It's childish on the radio, Max. Childish."

**Why A-tier**: The engineer scolding a multiple world champion like a primary school teacher. Says "childish" twice. Maximum dad energy.

### 12. Alonso — "Karma" (2017 Italian GP)

**Setup**: At Monza, Alonso has been furious with Palmer for cutting a chicane while defending. A few laps later, Palmer retires with engine failure. McLaren radios Alonso to inform him:

**What players would write**: "Good, one less to worry about" / "Finally some luck" / "About time"

**Real answer**: "Karma."

**Bonus twist**: Alonso himself then retired with three laps to go. Double karma.

**Why A-tier**: The one-word response is devastating. And the twist that karma came back for HIM is perfect narrative structure.

### 13. Alonso — "I Will Disconnect the Radio" (2025 Singapore GP)

**Setup**: Fernando Alonso, frustrated with a slow pit stop and traffic, has 34 laps remaining. His engineer gives him a routine update. Alonso responds:

**What players would write**: "Copy, keep me posted" / "Just let me drive" / "I know, I know"

**Real answer**: "If you speak to me every lap, I will disconnect the radio."

**Why A-tier**: The threat is probably not technically possible and definitely not legal. But Alonso delivers it with total conviction. Went viral immediately.

### 14. Stroll — "Worst Piece of S**t" (2025 British GP)

**Setup**: Lance Stroll has just finished seventh at Silverstone — actually a strong result in difficult conditions. His engineer congratulates him: "Crazy car to drive, amazing finish." Stroll responds:

**What players would write**: "Thanks guys, tough one" / "Good points" / "We'll take that"

**Real answer**: "Crazy is an understatement. It's the worst piece of s**t I've ever driven in my life, to be honest."

**Why A-tier**: The engineer opens with praise; Stroll immediately destroys the mood. The disconnect between "P7 is good" and "worst car ever" is perfect Stroll.

**Setup**: At Marina Bay, a large monitor lizard has appeared on the track (this has happened multiple times). In 2023, Verstappen radios: "There's another lizard on the track. Maybe a smaller one this time." His engineer GP responds:

### 20. Gasly — "What Did You Do?" (2020 Italian GP)

## HISTORICAL / 1990s-2000s ERA

### 37. Brawn to Schumacher — "19 Qualifying Laps" (1998 Hungarian GP)

**Setup**: Ferrari has devised an audacious three-stop strategy. Ross Brawn needs Michael Schumacher to drive at absolute maximum pace for 19 consecutive laps to make it work. He radios:

**Real answer**: "Michael, you have 19 laps to pull out 25 seconds. We need 19 qualifying laps from you."

**Why it works**: The pure audacity of the ask — 19 consecutive qualifying laps in a race. And Schumacher delivered. The calm engineering request that asks for the impossible.

### 38. Schumacher — "You Are Great, Ross" (2000 Japanese GP)

**Setup**: Michael Schumacher has just clinched both championships for Ferrari, ending their 21-year title drought. He radios:

**Real answer**: "You are great, Ross! All of you guys! We did it, we did it!"

**Why it works**: The machine becomes human for three seconds. Schumacher's voice breaks. Good contrast round after comedy — show the range of team radio.

### 39. Smedley to Massa — "Felipe Baby, Stay Cool" (2009 Malaysian GP)

**Setup**: Monsoon conditions, race red-flagged. Massa is anxious about not having a clear visor for the restart. His engineer Rob Smedley calms him:

**Real answer**: "Felipe, baby, stay cool."

**Why it works**: "Baby" is completely unironic. Smedley genuinely cared. The tenderness against the chaos of a restart is what makes it — sounds like a dad calming a child. Players would write professional engineering instructions.

## CONTENT NOTES

### Coverage by era
- **1990s-2000s**: Limited (radio wasn't broadcast as much) but what exists is legendary — Brawn/Schumacher, Smedley/Massa, Montoya
- **2010s**: Rich era — Vettel, Räikkönen, Webber, Alonso, Hamilton provide consistent material
- **2020s**: Deepest content pool — Verstappen/Lambiase relationship alone could fuel dozens of rounds, plus Leclerc, Norris, Tsunoda, Stroll, Russell

### Coverage by type
- Engineer deadpan/humor: entries 1, 3, 5, 6, 9, 11, 16, 39, 40
- Driver self-awareness: entries 7, 8, 10, 21, 30, 46
- Driver absurdity under pressure: entries 1, 4, 5, 13, 18, 45
- Driver-engineer relationship: entries 6, 9, 11, 16, 30, 39, 44
- Emotional/dramatic: entries 20, 24, 35, 36, 38, 41, 43
- Team dynamics/orders: entries 9, 19, 33, 40
- Penalty/steward reactions: entries 8, 12, 26, 27, 28

### Rounds per game night
A typical Words of Wisdom session might use 5-8 rounds. This catalog of 50 entries, assuming ~20 are strong Fibbage rounds (S and A tier), supports 2-3 full game nights before repetition. New F1 race weekends would continuously add to the library.
