# Exploratory Creative Audit — F1 Party Platform Game Modes (Round 1)

## What this is

This is Round 1 of an iterative creative audit of brainstormed game modes for an F1 fan game platform. The goal is NOT to evaluate ideas against a fixed rubric — it's to explore the possibility space around them, expand what's there, identify what's interesting and why, and generate new material alongside the existing brainstorm.

Further rounds may follow after the project creator reviews this output and provides direction. Whether there's a Round 2 (and beyond) depends on what comes out of Round 1 and what the creator thinks warrants deeper exploration. If further rounds happen, they'll cover both the original ideas and new material generated here.

### What this is NOT
- Not a go/no-go evaluation
- Not a feasibility assessment (mark implementation/technical concerns for later research, don't let them constrain ideation)
- Not a checklist audit — follow interesting threads wherever they lead, even if they weren't anticipated by this spec

## Context

The platform vision is a multi-mode F1 party game platform — think Jackbox Games meets F1 fandom. The anchor mode is a GeoGuessr-style F1 circuit geography game. Beyond that, 10+ game modes have been brainstormed across two sessions between the project creator and Claude Opus 4.6.

The brainstormed modes are not rigid designs — they are **families of possibilities**. Each mode entry describes a core concept with multiple variations, round types, and open questions. The audit should treat them as starting points, not finished proposals.

Important: these games don't all need to be funny and memey. Some should be. Some should be cerebral, some competitive, some chill, some chaotic. The portfolio should have range. But all should have some quality of longevity, replayability, or "hook" — something that makes people want to come back.

## The work — structured as phases, not a checklist

### Phase 1: Initial orientation

Before diving into assessment, orient yourself:
- What does the overall game mode portfolio look like? What types of experiences are represented?
- What seems to be the design philosophy / sensibility behind the brainstorming? What does this team value in game design?
- What does the competitive landscape look like (BoxBoxd, Jackbox, GeoGuessr)? Where does this platform sit?
- What audiences exist within F1 fandom? (Hardcore strategy nerds, meme culture fans, casual viewers, social/party groups, solo players, competitive players, creative types, etc.) Different games may serve different audiences — that's a strength, not a problem.

Write a brief orientation section in your output establishing your read on these before proceeding.

### Phase 2: Explore each mode's possibility space

For each brainstormed mode (or family of modes), explore in this order:

**A. What's here — understand the brainstormed concept.**
Read the entry. Understand what was brainstormed, what variations exist, what's designed vs. what's a seed. Don't rush to judgment.

**B. What possibilities does this concept afford beyond what's listed?**
The brainstorm captured some variations and round types. What else could this concept enable? What other directions could the core mechanic go? What combinations with other modes or platform features haven't been explored? Think generatively before evaluatively.

**C. Who is this for, and how might it serve different audiences?**
There is no single "ideal F1 fan." Different players want different things. For each mode:
- What type of player or social context is this naturally strongest for?
- How might the mode be modified or varied to appeal to a different type of player?
- How might it increase general appeal (difficulty levels, variations, accessibility features)?
- Can the same core mechanic serve multiple audiences through configuration rather than redesign?
- Does the mode balance multiple contexts well without one clearly central version, or do different variants want different commitments?
- Is there a way of playing this mode that exceeds the obvious categories in an interesting way?

**D. What's interesting about this — and what isn't?**
Not "is this good" (yes/no) but: what about this concept is genuinely interesting, novel, or has the quality of "I want to play this"? And what aspects feel flat, derivative, or unlikely to sustain engagement? Be specific — identify which parts, which variations, which round types.

**E. What's the replayability / longevity picture?**
What brings people back to this mode? Is it content-dependent (eventually exhausts its content well), skill-dependent (you get better), socially-dependent (your friends make it different each time), or something else? How deep is the replay potential?

**F. Flag for later research.**
Any aspects that depend on material feasibility (technical constraints, content sourcing, legal questions, implementation difficulty) — note them but don't let them constrain the ideation. The question is "would this be fun if we could build it?" not "can we build it right now?"

### Phase 3: The portfolio view

After exploring individual modes, step back and look at the collection:

- What **game design territories** are unrepresented? Think about: mechanic types, social configurations (competitive/cooperative/solo/team), energy levels (high-energy to chill), knowledge requirements (expert-only to anyone-can-play), play duration (30-second rounds to 20-minute sessions).
- What **audiences within F1 fandom** are underserved by the current portfolio?
- How do these modes **compose** — if you imagine a party night cycling through several, what's the energy arc? Where are the gaps?
- What's the **differentiation story** vs. BoxBoxd and other F1 fan games? Where is the platform offering something that genuinely doesn't exist?
- What **contexts and user situations** does the current portfolio cover well, and where are the gaps? Think local couch play, private online sync, async, hybrid, spectator/watch-party potential, recurring friend-group history, solo between-race play.
- Where does the portfolio have strong **virality hooks** but weak **return loops**, or the reverse?

### Phase 4: New proposals

Propose new game modes or mode variations that address gaps identified in Phase 3, or that simply seem compelling for this platform. For each:
- Core mechanic — what do players DO?
- Social shape — competitive, cooperative, solo, team, asymmetric?
- What makes it fun — why would someone want to play this?
- Why F1 specifically enables it — what about F1 as a domain makes this possible?
- Who is it for — what type of player or context?
- How does it relate to existing brainstormed modes — complement, alternative, or entirely new territory?

Aim for 3-7 proposals with enough specificity to compare against existing entries.

### Phase 5: Open threads

Anything else that came up during the audit that doesn't fit the above phases. Questions, tensions, observations, connections between modes, platform-level implications, things that surprised you, ideas that need more development before they can be evaluated. Follow threads that seem interesting.

## Output format

Structured markdown document. Use the phase structure above but don't be rigid — if a thread wants to span phases, let it.

Mark uncertainty honestly. "I'm not sure about this but..." is better than false confidence.

Cite specific passages from the brainstorming docs when referencing ideas.

## Tone

You are a creative collaborator joining mid-brainstorm, not an evaluator with a rubric. The project creator wants to be surprised, challenged, and given new material to react to. The bar for creative quality is high — "kind of lame" ideas were explicitly rejected during the brainstorming. Match that bar.
